# Google Calendar Connector

Better-Cal can read calendars through a connected Google account: ones you own, ones you subscribe to, and ones other people have shared with you, including shared-but-not-public calendars no iCal address can reach. A connected calendar is an ordinary subscribed calendar here, with a color, folders and feed health. Changes made in Google arrive within the poll interval (5 minutes by default, one small request per poll).

Where the account may edit the calendar (Google's access role `writer` or `owner`: your own calendars, and ones shared with "make changes"), you can create, edit and delete events here too. That's **write-through**, not two-way sync: each change goes to Google first, and Google's answer is saved here the same way a poll would save it, so there's never a local-only version of a Google event. If Google refuses (network, revoked token, a changed role), the request fails with a clear error and nothing here changes. Calendars the account can only view stay read-only, like any feed.

Better-Cal reads the account's access from Google at every poll and at the start of every change, from a request it makes anyway. When the calendar's owner lowers it to view only or busy times only, or stops sharing it, Better-Cal tells you once in Activity and in Review (a poll at that access is no failure of the calendar, so no "started failing" line or warning follows it), and the calendar works read-only here: changes, and undoing past changes, are refused with the reason, and nothing is sent. Better-Cal doesn't try to work around it. A change that was half done at that moment stays listed in Review, and its event takes no other change, until full editing is restored in Google Calendar or you remove the calendar. Better-Cal asks Google again at the next poll, when you choose Check again (on the Review notice, or beside the calendar's source in its settings), and when you undo a change on it or answer an invitation on it; if Google lets the account make changes again, they work at once. The Review notice can be dismissed; it comes back if the access changes again, or when something you try is refused because of it. When Google shows the account only busy times, it lists no details, so Better-Cal applies nothing it lists: the events here stay as they were and don't update until Google shows full details again, and the next poll after that catches up.

## What Better-Cal Promises, and What It Doesn't

The short version, as of 1.5.8. The sections below have the detail, and [the edge cases](design/google-edge-cases.md) list the rare circumstances found so far, how likely each is and what happens in each.

**It promises:**

- **Google Calendar first.** A change to a Google event goes to Google Calendar before anything is saved here, and the calendar shows what Google Calendar answers. If Google Calendar refuses, nothing changes here and the message says why. There's never a version of a Google event that exists only here.
- **It reads before it writes.** Every change, undo, Finish and Put back reads the event in Google Calendar first. If the account can now only view the calendar, nothing is sent and Better-Cal says so (see the top of this page).
- **No mail to guests.** Guest lists are never sent, and every write tells Google Calendar to send no updates. An answer to an invitation reaches the organizer only, as Google Calendar passes it on.
- **Only what you changed is sent.** A change sends the fields it changes (title, notes, place, time, Planned or Maybe, Busy or Free, how it repeats, the link) and nothing else, so a change made in Google Calendar to another field stays. Guests, reminders and color are never sent ([What Writes Send](#what-writes-send)).
- **What only Better-Cal holds stays.** Tags, people, reminders, your answer, an icon, a map pin you picked and trip links stay on an event, and on each day of a repeating event, whatever Google Calendar does to it. When a day goes because the repeating event no longer has it, Activity names it and what it held ([How Syncing Works](#how-syncing-works)).
- **One event changed in Google Calendar since you saw it** (an event that doesn't repeat): a change to another field goes through and the toast names what changed there and was kept; a change to the same field asks, with both values, Cancel or Overwrite; a delete asks over any change ([When Someone Else Changes the Same Event](#when-someone-else-changes-the-same-event)).
- **A change to all events of a repeating event shows first** what it does to each edited and deleted day, then carries those days across where Google Calendar would drop or overwrite them. It ends by reading Google Calendar back: where Google Calendar holds something other than what you asked, Activity and Review say what it holds and what was asked, with both values.
- **A change that stops part way is not lost.** It is recorded before anything is sent. Review lists it as "Half done in Google Calendar" with Finish and Put back, and the repeating event takes no other change until one of them has run. Dismissing the notice hides it and changes nothing else.
- **Undo** of a change to one event, or to one day of a repeating event, is a second write. It puts back only the fields that change changed, and is compared with what Google Calendar holds like any change: something else changed there since is kept and named, and a field it would put back that was changed again since, there or here, is asked about first ("After this change to 'Weekly sync', the title was changed again: it's 'Team sync' now. Undo puts back 'Standup', replacing 'Team sync'."), with Cancel or Undo anyway. Two cases still refuse whole when the repeating event changed in Google Calendar since the last poll: the undo of a change that made a day an ordinary day again, and a redo that would give an ordinary day its change back.
- **A poll never empties a calendar by accident.** A full listing with no events is not applied, and a listing of busy times only is not applied over the details held here.

**It refuses, before sending anything, with a sentence saying why:**

- A change to a repeating event, or to one of its days, when the repeating event changed in Google Calendar since the calendar here last read it. The message doesn't yet say what changed there.
- A switch of a repeating event between timed and all-day at a time where Google Calendar would stop showing some of its days. It names the days and says what works: move the time first, then switch.
- A change that would leave a repeating event only deleted days ("Delete it instead?"), since Google Calendar would delete the whole event.
- Any change to a repeating event that has a change half done in Review.
- Moving an event between a Google calendar and any other calendar (copy it, then delete the original).
- Any change on a calendar the account can only view.

**It drops, and says so:**

- An edited day past the first 730 days of a repeating event with no end, which Google Calendar doesn't hold: left as Google Calendar has it, and named.
- A change made in Google Calendar to a day while a half-done change waited for Finish, where Finish changes that day: replaced, or lost where the change gives the days other times, and named with the value Google Calendar held.
- A day a poll removes that held tags, people or reminders: named in Activity.

**It doesn't promise:**

- **Nothing guards the seconds of its own change.** While a change to all events is being sent, the last write wins: a change made to the same repeating event in Google Calendar in those seconds may be written over. The calendar afterwards shows what Google Calendar holds.
- **Undo of a change to all events, or to this and following,** is off for now ([#274](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/274)). It says so where its Undo would be.
- **Notes go back as Better-Cal's copy.** When a change sends a day again, its notes are Better-Cal's cleaned copy, so markup made in Google Calendar (a non-breaking space, a link's attributes) can come back changed.
- **What only Google Calendar holds on a day isn't carried.** A color or a guest set on one day in Google Calendar isn't sent again when a change makes Google Calendar drop that day's own record, and nothing says so.
- **The days are worked out here.** Better-Cal works out a repeating event's days from its rule and doesn't compare them with Google Calendar's own list, so a fault in Google Calendar that hides days shows here as days Google Calendar doesn't show, with nothing said ([#266](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/266)).
- **Same clock time in any zone** isn't something Google Calendar holds: such an event is sent at your Home zone's time.
- **Finish and Put back after rare sequences.** A few sequences of two or three uncommon things are known to end wrongly or with an untrue sentence: Finish bringing back an event deleted in Google Calendar after the stop, for one. Each is listed in [the edge cases](design/google-edge-cases.md).

## One-time setup (the operator)

Google requires each install to have its own OAuth client, so whoever runs a Better-Cal install sets one up once, in their own Google Cloud project. It takes about ten minutes.

- **Only needed for the Google connector.** Everything else works without it, including following a Google calendar by its secret iCal address (Subscribe) and bringing calendars over from Google Calendar's export or a Takeout export (Import).
- **No cost and no billing account.** A Google Cloud project is free with any Google account, and the Calendar API has no charge at this scale.
- **The app stays "unverified"**, which is fine for your own install. Verification is for apps offered to the public; an unverified app shows a one-time warning when an account connects.

The console's layout changes often. As of October 2026, OAuth lives under **Google Auth Platform** (Overview, Branding, Audience, Clients, Data Access, Verification Center). The links below open each page for whichever project is selected in the project picker at the top.

1. **Pick or create a project:** [console.cloud.google.com/projectcreate](https://console.cloud.google.com/projectcreate), or choose an existing one in the project picker. One that already has an OAuth setup saves the branding step.
2. **Enable the Google Calendar API:** [its page in the API Library](https://console.cloud.google.com/apis/library/calendar-json.googleapis.com) → **Enable**. Do this first: the scope picker in step 5 only lists enabled APIs.
3. **Branding:** [Google Auth Platform → Branding](https://console.cloud.google.com/auth/branding). App name (say "Better-Cal"), a support email, a developer contact. Save.
4. **Audience:** [Google Auth Platform → Audience](https://console.cloud.google.com/auth/audience). User type **External**, then **Publish app**, so the publishing status reads *In production*. This matters: in *Testing* status Google expires refresh tokens after seven days, and every connected account would need reconnecting weekly. In production the app is still unverified, so Google shows a warning once per account when connecting ("Advanced", then "Go to Better-Cal").
5. **Scopes:** [Google Auth Platform → Data Access](https://console.cloud.google.com/auth/scopes) → **Add or remove scopes**. Filter "Google Calendar API" and tick `.../auth/calendar.readonly`, `.../auth/calendar.events` and `.../auth/calendar.app.created` (lets Better-Cal create calendars of its own, for moving a calendar to Google, and touches nothing else), then `openid` and `.../auth/userinfo.email` from the top of the list. Update, Save. The request itself carries the scopes, so this only sets what the consent screen describes; an unverified app works without it, but it takes a minute.
6. **The client:** [Google Auth Platform → Clients](https://console.cloud.google.com/auth/clients) → **Create client**. Application type **Web application**, any name. Under **Authorized redirect URIs** add `https://<your host>/api/v1/google/callback`. It must match `BETTERCAL_BASE_URL` exactly, including the scheme; no JavaScript origins are needed. Create, then copy the **Client ID** and **Client secret** (shown once; there's a download button).
7. **Put the client ID and secret in the server's settings file, `.env.php`:**

```
BETTERCAL_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID=....apps.googleusercontent.com
BETTERCAL_GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET=...
```

Refresh tokens are encrypted with `BETTERCAL_SESSION_SECRET` (already set on any working install) before they're stored, and so is each account's current access token, with its expiry and the grant it came from, so the worker and the browser share one token for its hour instead of asking Google for a new one each time. A reconnect, a disconnect, the compromise reset or a 401 from Google clears it. Changing the session secret invalidates stored tokens; reconnecting each account fixes it.

Until both variables are set, Settings → Connections says the connector isn't set up and offers nothing.

**Google's own documentation**, if a screen looks different from the steps above:

- [Get started with the Google Auth Platform](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15544987): the Branding, Audience, Clients and Data Access pages.
- [Configure the OAuth consent screen](https://developers.google.com/workspace/guides/configure-oauth-consent) and [create access credentials](https://developers.google.com/workspace/guides/create-credentials), from Google's Workspace developer guides.
- [Unverified apps](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/7454865): what the warning means, and why your own install doesn't need verification.
- [Refresh token expiration](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2#expiration): the seven-day limit in Testing status.

## Connecting (Each User)

Settings → Connections → **Connect a Google account**. Google asks for consent (calendar read access, event write access, permission to create Better-Cal's own calendars, and your email address, which labels the account here), then sends you back to the browser session that started it. Several accounts can be connected.

Connecting, adding one of its calendars, moving a calendar and disconnecting ask for your Better-Cal password if this browser hasn't confirmed it in the last ten minutes (a fresh sign-in counts). Ordinary use and syncing don't ask. An account connected without the permission to create calendars is asked to reconnect once, the first time you move a calendar to Google.

The account lists every calendar Google shows it, with Google's access role and a kind read from the calendar id:

- **Yours**: your primary calendar, or a secondary one you own.
- **Shared with you**: someone else's, shared with you (the kind no iCal address can reach).
- **Feed copy**: Google's own copy of an ICS subscription, always behind the source; subscribing to the ICS address here directly is fresher.
- **Google**: holidays, birthdays.

**Add** subscribes to one and syncs it right away.

**Disconnect** revokes the token at Google and forgets it. Calendars already subscribed from that account keep their events but stop updating, with "Google account disconnected" as their poll error. To bring one back, connect the account again and use **Re-attach** on that calendar in its list: it keeps its events and everything added to them (tags, people, reminders, Planned or Maybe, trips) and picks up what changed at Google meanwhile. Or adopt it as local to keep its events without syncing.

If Google stops accepting an account's grant, Settings says **Reconnect needed**, with a Reconnect button; saved events stay visible while syncing and editing pause. A temporary network failure says **Connection problem** instead. A revocation already sent to Google can still land after you reconnect; if it does, reconnect again. Likewise, an invitation reply already sent to Google can't be recalled.

**Move to Google Calendar** (a local calendar's settings) puts a calendar that started here into your Google account; see [Moving a calendar to Google](#moving-a-calendar-to-google). **Adopt as local** (a Google calendar's settings) goes the other way: the calendar stays here with every event, the link to Google is cut in both directions, and the copy in Google Calendar is left as it is. It's the last step of a migration; [migration.md](migration.md) has the order.

**After an account compromise:** an ordinary password change keeps Google connections. The server-side compromise reset, `php server/bin/seed.php --email=you@example.com --revoke-tokens`, pauses every Google account, stops unfinished moves, and then asks Google to revoke their refresh tokens. Cached events stay visible, but Google calendars are read-only and stop polling until each account is reconnected. A request already in flight may finish, events a partial move already uploaded stay in Google Calendar (Better-Cal can't retract them from an account the owner may no longer control), and cancelled moves are never resumed automatically.

## Work Accounts (Google Workspace)

A work account managed by a company's IT department follows that department's settings, which decide what's possible here. Whether you may copy work calendar data to a server you run is a matter of your employer's policies, and checking and following them is entirely your responsibility; Better-Cal can't tell. What to try:

- **Connect the account** as above. Where IT restricts third-party apps, Google says your administrator has blocked access. IT can allow one app by its OAuth client ID, but be realistic: few IT departments approve a self-hosted app from a one-person project. If you ask, the [security reviews](../SECURITY.md#what-has-been-reviewed) may help make the case.
- **Subscribe by its secret iCal address** (the calendar's settings in Google Calendar, "Secret address in iCal format"). Companies that limit sharing outside the organization often hide it.
- **Share the work calendar with a personal Google account**, and connect that one. Where outside sharing is limited to free/busy, you get your busy times without the details.
- **Forward single invitations** to Better-Cal's mail address ([Email ingest](email-ingest.md)). Automatic forwarding to an outside address is usually blocked.

## Moving a Calendar to Google

For a calendar that started in Better-Cal and now needs to be seen by people who use Google Calendar: a trip calendar for travel companions, say, or a family calendar. An outbound feed also works for read-only sharing, but Google refreshes it on its own schedule (often once a day) and nobody in Google Calendar can add to it.

**How:** the calendar's settings (the gear beside it) → **Move to Google Calendar…** → into a new Google calendar of the same name (or one you already have, ideally empty) → **Move to Google**.

- **Upload:** Better-Cal creates the calendar in your Google account and uploads every event with Google's import call, which keeps each event's identity and sends no mail. A large calendar uploads in the background, with progress in its settings, and takes no edits until the upload is done.
- **After the upload:** it's a Google calendar here, edited in place like any other: a change goes to Google first, and changes made in Google Calendar arrive within the poll interval.
- **What stays:** events keep their identity here, so their tags, people, reminders, trip membership and links all stay.
- **Sharing:** from Google Calendar: the calendar's settings there, **Share with specific people**, with "See all event details" or "Make changes to events".
- **If the upload stops** (Google unreachable, say), the calendar stays local and untouched, and **Try again** continues where it stopped. Nothing switches over until every event is in Google Calendar. Disconnecting the account partway cancels the move instead (the Disconnect confirmation says so): the calendar stays local, events already uploaded stay in Google Calendar, and a new move starts over.

**What changes:** as with any Google calendar, an undo is a second write to Google (see Activity below). Activity entries from before the move stay listed but can't be undone. Moving events between it and a local calendar is refused (copy, then delete). **Adopt as local** reverses the whole move, leaving the copy in Google Calendar as it is.

## What Writes Send

Title, description, location, start and end (dates for all-day, RFC 3339 with the time zone otherwise), status, whether the event takes up time ("Show as": `transparency`), the recurrence rule with its exceptions, and the event's link as Google's `source`, which polls read back so the link isn't replaced by Google's own page for the event. An event with no link of its own has none here: Google's page for the event is not its link. Not sent: attendees (they would mail people), reminders (per-user in Google Calendar) and color. Tags, people, reminders and the trip flag stay local and survive polls. A write sends only the fields it changes, so a change made in Google Calendar to another field stays; a change of nothing Google holds, for one day or for all events of a repeating event, sends Google nothing and undoes here like a local change.

Recurring edits: "this occurrence" patches Google's instance and comes back as an exception; deleting one occurrence cancels the instance in Google Calendar, which is stored here as a skipped day. A change to all events, or to this and following, goes through the same dialog as on a local calendar, and then to Google in steps: the series itself, then each day Google doesn't hold as the plan says (Google drops every edited day when a series' time, zone, start day or rule changes, overwrites their fields when a field changes for all events, and leaves a day's own end where it was when the start moves), each sent again on its own, so a day with its own time, length or notes keeps them; "this and following" ends the old series in Google Calendar and inserts the new one with the edited days after the cut. The series is read back after the writes, and the calendar shows what Google stores; where that differs from the plan, the Activity entry and a Review notice say what Google Calendar holds and what the change asked for, with both values ("On Mon 12 Apr Google Calendar holds the title 'Rehearsal'; this change asked for the title 'Dress rehearsal'."), and never guess at why. Google keeps a record for every day a write touched, so a day it holds at the series' own values (a day put back to the series, a skipped day brought back) is an ordinary occurrence here, not an edited day; a day that differs only in things Google doesn't hold (tags, people, reminders) keeps its row. Deleting a series removes its edited days in Google Calendar with it. Google lists the first 730 occurrences of a series with no end and no more, so an edited day past them can't be held there: it is left as Google has it and named in the Activity entry and Review. One day of an all-day series can be given a clock time, and one day of a timed series made all-day; Google takes both. A change Google refuses before taking any part of it (the series changed there since, or the account may not edit the calendar) records nothing: the calendar, Activity and Review stay as they were, and the message says only why Google refused. A change that stops half way, after Google took part of it (Google unreachable, the series changed there meanwhile, or the server stopped mid-way), is listed in Review as "Half done in Google Calendar", with **Finish** (the rest of it, then its part here: tags, people, reminders and flags) and **Put back** (every step sent, reversed, a step whose answer never came included, all or nothing). Finish first checks whether Google holds the event as the change left it. If it does, Finish sends the rest of the change as planned. If it doesn't, what Finish does depends on whether Google had taken the change's write to the event itself. Where it had not, Finish applies only what the change changed over what Google and the calendar hold now: anything changed elsewhere since on a day or field the change didn't touch stays as it is, and a day or field changed since in something the change does touch (a day deleted meanwhile, a tag list set meanwhile) is kept and named, never overwritten. Where it had, Better-Cal can't tell what Google did to the days with that write from a change made in Google Calendar since, so Finish sets every day as the change has it and names each day it changed, with what Google held and what Finish set ("This change had stopped part way, and Google Calendar no longer held the event as it was left. On Mon 8 Mar Google Calendar held the notes 'Bring the keys'; Finish set the notes 'Agenda attached', as this change has it."). A change made to a day in Google Calendar between the stop and Finish is replaced there, so read that list. A field of the event the change didn't change stays as Google has it either way. One exception where Google had not taken that write: a change that stopped before Google took its first write and that gives the series' days other keys there: a new time, another zone, a start moved to another day, a new rule, all-day made timed or the reverse. Which day of the changed series a day of the old one becomes is not something Finish can read off Google, so a day changed in Google Calendar meanwhile is not kept. Finish names each one and what was done to it, with the reason ("While this change was waiting to be finished, Fri 8 Jan was renamed in Google Calendar. That was lost when the event's time changed. Make it again there if you still want it."), and that change has to be made again. A change that keeps every day where it is (a rename, a new length, a new end) keeps what was changed in Google Calendar meanwhile, on its day; only a day the series no longer has is named the same way: past a new end ("... because the series now ends before that day."), or handed to the series a "this and following" change makes ("... because this change split the series before that day."). Put back goes ahead only while nothing on the series changed in Google Calendar since, in any field Google keeps (guests, colour, reminders and visibility included) and on any of its days; if anything did, even in a series this change inserted, nothing is put back, the answer names what changed, and Finish is the way to settle it. Put back compares with what Better-Cal last read of the event while the change was being sent; if the connection went right after a write, before that read, Put back may be refused too, since it can't tell, and Finish settles it. A day with only things Google doesn't hold (tags, people, reminders, coordinates) is never sent to Google, by a change or by an undo, and a day Google keeps at the series' own values is never written. An edited day goes with its own fields and the series' values for the rest, so an undo of a rename never sends a day back with the old title. The record of the change is saved before anything is sent to Google, so it survives whatever stops the change, and either way every day keeps what only Better-Cal holds for it: its tags, people, reminders, answer, icon and trip links, and the coordinates picked for the place it shows. Until it is finished or put back, the series takes no other change: an edit, delete, answer, undo or redo touching it is refused with a pointer to Review, and polls leave its events here as they are (Finish and Put back read what Google holds when they run).

Show as (1.5.8): an event marked Free in Google Calendar is free here, and one made free here is sent as free (`transparency: "transparent"`); busy is Google's default and is sent as nothing. A new all-day event made here on a Google calendar is free unless you pick Busy, as Google Calendar's own page makes it (Google's API alone would make it busy); a new timed event is busy. The editor shows which before you save, and follows the calendar and the all-day box until you pick. An existing event keeps its setting when it is made all-day or timed.

An event that keeps its clock time in any zone (a floating time, in iCalendar's word) is sent to Google pinned in your Home zone, since Google has no such times, and keeps the setting here, whether it was made that way or set later: a time change made in Google Calendar is read back as a new clock time.

Moving an event between a Google calendar and any other calendar is refused, because half of such a move could never be undone here. Use **Copy to** (the stack icon on an event), then delete the original if you meant a move. A trip on a Google calendar moves one event at a time.

Activity records each write ("Updated 'Team lunch' on Google calendar 'Book club'"). Every write to Google has Undo, in its notice and in Activity: a field or time change, a delete, a new event, Planned or Maybe on an event you organize, an answer to an invitation, and a change to a whole repeating series (all events, this and following, an answer from a day on, a new repeating event, a series deleted). Undo is a second write to Google, sending back what the event or series was before, and only if it hasn't changed in Google Calendar since (Google checks its version); if it has, the undo is refused and nothing changes. A series goes back the way a change to it goes (the series, then each edited or skipped day, read back), and its tags, people and edited days' own fields come back here. A deleted event comes back under the same identity in Google Calendar, and here as it was, with everything Google doesn't hold: its tags, people, reminders, trip and trip links, and the clock-time setting. The undo is an Activity entry of its own, so it can be undone too (that is redo, from Activity); the app's Undo passes over it and steps one change further back, as on any calendar. Changes Google never sees, such as a trip link, Planned, tags or reminders, undo here like a local calendar's. An undo of a series that stops half way is listed in Review as "Half done in Google Calendar", like a change. Since 1.5.5 the undo and redo of a change to a whole series (everything in that list but a single day) are held back, until a change to all events is rebuilt on stored state ([#274](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/274)): they say so and change nothing, the app's Undo steps past them to the change before, and the undo of a single day works as described. For operators: changes recorded before upgrading to 1.5.5, on any calendar, become log only with the upgrade ("Recorded by an earlier version of Better-Cal" in Activity), so for the week after it Undo reaches only changes made since; nothing else about them changes.

## When Someone Else Changes the Same Event

Better-Cal reads Google first, before every change it makes: before a change, before Finish, before Put back and before an undo. For an event that doesn't repeat (since 1.5.7), what it read is compared with what you were shown: a change in Google Calendar to a field you aren't changing stays, and the toast names it with its value; a change to the same field stops your change, nothing is sent, and you're shown what Google Calendar holds now and what your change would make it, with Cancel or Overwrite; a delete asks over any change, with Cancel or Delete. For a repeating event, or one of its days, a change in Google Calendar since the calendar here last read it refuses the change and nothing is sent; that notice doesn't yet name what changed there (refresh to see it). A change's first write to the event also tells Google which version it was made from, and Google refuses it if that version is gone; the days of a change to all events, written right after it, go without that check. A change in Google Calendar to a field Better-Cal isn't changing is no conflict and stays as Google has it.

While one of Better-Cal's own changes is being sent (a few requests, a few seconds for a change to all events of a repeating event), the last write wins: Better-Cal doesn't try to catch a change someone else makes to the same event inside that window. If someone renames a day of the series in Google Calendar in the same seconds, the change from here may write over it. What the calendar shows afterwards is what Google holds.

Other calendar apps work the same way as far as this project's survey of them goes ([CalDAV apps](design/research/google-series/caldav-apps.md), [smaller handlers](design/research/google-series/small-handlers.md), [the survey](design/google-series-survey.md)): they check each event's version before writing it and, on a conflict, ask (Thunderbird), let one side win whole (vdirsyncer, and Evolution's "keep local", which sends no version at all), or report it (KDE's Google resource). None of those surveyed describes any guard against a change made between its own requests.

## Invitations

An event on a connected Google calendar that lists your account as a guest, organized by someone else, is an invitation: its details show who invited you, how many are invited, and Accept / Maybe / Decline, with your current answer in Google Calendar selected. Unanswered ones also appear on the Review page.

Answering sets your response in Google Calendar (`attendees[self].responseStatus`). Google passes a guest's answer to the organizer itself, so Better-Cal sends it with `sendUpdates=none` and nobody else on the guest list gets mail. An answer changed in Google Calendar shows here on the next poll. If Google refuses or can't be reached, nothing changes here and the error says why.

Google puts invitations on the account's main calendar, so this needs that calendar connected. A recurring invitation is answered for the whole series (or, for an occurrence Google already treats separately, for that one). Answering leaves each day of it as it was: a write to a repeating event can make Google clear a day's own notes, so after the answer Better-Cal reads the event back and sends any such day again, and a Review notice says so if Google holds a day otherwise at the end.

## How Syncing Works

- The first sync lists every event on the calendar (Google's `events.list`: series with their `RRULE`, exceptions as instances) and stores the sync token Google returns.
- Every later poll sends the sync token and gets back only what changed; an unchanged calendar is one request with an empty answer. Changes go through the same path as ICS feeds, so deletions, the CalDAV change log, Activity and health states behave as for any feed.
- A cancelled instance of a recurring event becomes an `EXDATE` on the series here; a cancelled series is deleted whole.
- A day keeps what only Better-Cal holds for it (tags, people, reminders, an answer, an icon, coordinates picked on the map) whatever Google does to its record. While Google holds an edited version of the day (its own title, place text, notes or time), that version shows. When Google drops it (a new clock time for the series drops every edited day there, a new zone does when the series' moments move, and a new rule drops those on days it no longer has) or never had one, the day follows its series: its title, place, notes and time come from the series on the next poll and it moves with the series' new clock time. If the series no longer has the day (a new end, a new rule, the day skipped, the series split), the day and what it held go, named in the poll's Activity entry. A skipped day is Google's too: when a new clock time, zone, rule or count in Google Calendar brings a skipped day back there, the next poll brings it back here. A series changed between timed and all-day in Google Calendar is read the same way: its edited and skipped days of the old kind are no longer Google's, so each of those days follows its series. If the day lands where another row already is (Google edited that day in the same change), what it held moves onto that row, and Activity says so. ICS feeds work the same way.
- When Google says the token is stale (HTTP 410, after long gaps), the next poll lists everything again. Nothing is lost either way.
- An empty listing never deletes. A full listing (the first, or after a 410) that comes back with no events for a calendar that has some here is not applied: nothing is removed, the poll shows the reason, Review lists the calendar, and the next poll lists it again. A calendar the account lost access to can look like this; one you emptied in Google Calendar on purpose still empties through an ordinary poll.
- Whatever a poll removes that held something only Better-Cal has (tags, people, reminders, an answer, coordinates) is named in its Activity entry, by the event and the date it showed.
- A poll has a size and time budget. If a response is too large or stops making progress, Better-Cal keeps the last successful events and sync token, records the reason on the calendar and moves on to other work. [Scale and limits](limits.md) has the numbers and the settings that raise them.
- Reminders aren't synced (they're per-user in Google Calendar). Guest lists are read only to recognize invitations.
- The poll interval, stale threshold, "Refresh now" and the rest of a calendar's settings work as for any feed.

## Failure Modes You Will See

- Poll error "Google token request failed: HTTP 400 (Token has been expired or revoked.)", Google's `invalid_grant`: the refresh token was revoked (you removed Better-Cal under Google Account → Security → Third-party access, or the OAuth client was in Testing status for over seven days). Reconnect the account.
- "Google is busy and refused the change for now": Google answered that a rate or quota limit was reached. Better-Cal tried twice more with a pause in between; nothing was changed. Try again in a minute.
- "This event was deleted in Google Calendar": the event an edit was for no longer exists there. Nothing was changed; refresh the calendar. A delete of something Google had already removed is not refused: it completes here, and for an event or one day Activity says Google Calendar had already removed it.
- Poll error "Google Calendar listed no events for this calendar": a full listing came back empty while the calendar has events here, so nothing was removed. Check that the calendar is still shared with the connected account in Google Calendar.
- Poll error "Google account disconnected": you disconnected the account. Connect it again and use **Re-attach** on the calendar in its list, or adopt the calendar as local.
- "Google did not return a refresh token" on connect: Google issues one only on first consent unless asked, and Better-Cal does ask (`prompt=consent`). If it still happens, remove Better-Cal under Third-party access and connect again.
