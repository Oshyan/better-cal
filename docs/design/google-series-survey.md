# Google Series: the Survey Before the Build

What the mature open-source Google Calendar clients do with recurring events, what Google itself documents, and what the CalDAV world and the big products do with the same five edits. Read 2026-11-07, before Google calendars move onto the series model ([#172](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/172)); it sets the floor the build has to clear and the baseline the measured comparison after it is judged against ([#225](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/225)). The six source reports, with every file, function and URL, are in [research/google-series/](research/google-series/).

The five edits throughout: one occurrence moved (date or time), one skipped, one changed in a field only, "this and following", and the whole series' rule or start changed.

## What Google Documents, and What It Doesn't

- A recurring master's `recurrence` holds RRULE, RDATE and EXDATE lines (the reference also lists EXRULE; the guide doesn't), never DTSTART or DTEND, and the master's `start.timeZone` is the zone the rule expands in. Google documents no normalization of a rule on read-back, no rejected forms, and no part limits; the 730-occurrence cap is in the end-user help only. An official page uses `BYMONTHDAY=-1`, so that form at least is accepted.
- An exception is an instance resource named by `recurringEventId` plus an immutable `originalStartTime`; a skipped day is a cancelled instance that carries only its id, its parent and its original start, and clients must keep it for the life of the parent. A plain list without `singleEvents` returns cancelled instances even with `showDeleted=false`; with `singleEvents=true` they disappear. A `syncToken` list forbids `showDeleted=false` and any time bound.
- "This and following" had a documented recipe (end the old series with UNTIL or COUNT, insert a new one) with the warning that the split "resets any exceptions happening after the target instance". Google removed the whole section from its guide between 2026-05-26 and 2026-09-17; the live page only warns against editing instance by instance. The old text is recoverable from the Wayback Machine (cited in the report).
- Nothing current says what happens to existing exceptions when a master's rule or start changes. A 2011 Google blog post says exception changes are "preserved even if the recurring event itself is updated"; a 2009 Workspace post says changing the time or frequency "resets all information in the series". Which is true today is a probe question, not a documented fact.
- Change detection: a per-event `etag` with `If-Match` (412 on mismatch), `syncToken` on `events.list` only (410 means wipe and list everything again), and push channels that expire after seven days and never renew. A client-chosen event id is base32hex, 5 to 1024 characters, unique per calendar, so an insert can be retried under the same id. `sendUpdates` has no documented default on patch, update or delete.

## The Floor: What the Open-Source Handlers Do

"handled" means correct in both directions; "refused" means not offered or rejected; "wrong" means data lost or misread. Everything below was read from current source; nothing was run. The report per handler names the file and function.

| Handler | Moved occurrence | Skipped | Field only | This and following | Rule or start change | Change detection | Change from Google |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thunderbird, Provider for Google Calendar (API v3, 139.9.7) | handled: PATCH to the instance id, changed fields only | handled: master PATCH resending the whole recurrence array with the EXDATE | handled: instance PATCH | refused: the button is hidden, the code throws "not implemented" (bug 320178, open since 2005) | wrong: exceptions are re-keyed locally, never at Google, then rebuilt from the stale cache | handled: syncToken, If-Match on every write, a prompt on 412, a full re-read on 410 | wrong: a changed master in a delta replaces the cached series and drops the exceptions it didn't resend |
| Thunderbird over CalDAV | handled: PUT of the whole resource with If-Match | handled | handled | refused: same hidden button | wrong: same local re-key | handled: sync token or ctag plus etags, a prompt on 409 or 412 | handled: the resource replaced whole |
| Evolution Data Server (CalDAV to Google; it has no API backend) | handled: PUT of the whole resource | handled: EXDATE, the override removed | handled | wrong: UNTIL (or a shortened COUNT) plus a new UID, but the old series keeps its later overrides past its new end | wrong: the master replaced, overrides never re-keyed (issue 1748) | wrong: PUTs carry no If-Match, so a concurrent change at Google is overwritten unnoticed, and any 400 counts as success | handled: the resource replaced whole |
| gcalcli (4.5.1) | handled: PATCH on the instance id | handled: DELETE on the instance id | handled | refused: deletes instances one by one | refused: no series edit | wrong: a full re-list every run, no etag, no token, no 410 or 412 handling | handled: it holds no state |
| Home Assistant with gcal_sync (9.2) | refused: no edits at all; reads handled | handled: instance patched to cancelled | refused | handled for a delete (UNTIL cut to the day before, one RRULE only); refused for an edit | refused: create with one RRULE only | handled: sync token, 410 wipes the store and lists again; no If-Match | handled: a read-only mirror of masters and exceptions |
| vdirsyncer (CalDAV only) | wrong: a whole-resource PUT of an edited series gets 409 (#963) | wrong | wrong | wrong: RANGE is undocumented at Google, the client would have to split | wrong | handled: etag re-list every run with If-Match; a 412 stops the collection; no sync-collection | handled: whole-item replace |
| KDE Akonadi Google resource | wrong: a new exception is POSTed as a new event; an existing one is PUT correctly | wrong: Google's cancellations are dropped (518138); timed EXDATEs are never written | wrong: as moved | wrong for an edit (the range is lost on that POST); handled for a delete | wrong: a full PUT drops timed EXDATEs and writes RDATE with a typo | wrong: `If-Match: *` on every write; a 410 re-list is applied as a delta, so stale items stay | wrong: cancellations lost, orphan local exceptions kept |

The floor, then, is lower than the issue assumed. No handler re-keys exceptions at Google when a series' start moves; the two that re-key do it locally and hand Google a different picture. Only Thunderbird's provider writes with an etag and handles a conflict. No handler carries a master's field change into exceptions that had inherited the old value. Only Evolution and Home Assistant do "this and following", both as a split, and Evolution leaves the stranded overrides behind. Two handlers lose cancelled instances outright. What to copy: Thunderbird's instance PATCH with only the changed fields, If-Match on every write with a conflict mapped to a choice, a 410 mapped to one full re-read, cancelled instances read back as skips; Evolution's and Home Assistant's split (UNTIL or a shortened COUNT, then a new series).

## The Reference: CalDAV and the Big Products

- **The standard** (RFC 5545) has two ways to say "this and following": an override marked `RECURRENCE-ID;RANGE=THISANDFUTURE` inside the same resource, or a split into a second UID, which RFC 5545 itself suggests where the range can't express the change and JSCalendar (RFC 8984) requires, with RFC 9253 adding the `RELTYPE=FIRST` and `NEXT` links between the halves. The standard doesn't say what an EXDATE naming an overridden instance means, and servers differ.
- **Servers.** Only Apple's CalendarServer applies RANGE when expanding; sabre/dav (Nextcloud, Baikal) stores it and says in its code it doesn't support it, Cyrus (Fastmail) and Radicale have no RANGE code, Google's CalDAV endpoint documents nothing about recurrence at all. None of them rewrites data when a master changes, except CalendarServer, which on every write drops an EXDATE that names an override and adds an RDATE for each override the rule no longer produces. Sync is uniform: strong etags with If-Match and 412, sync-collection with ctag as the fallback.
- **Clients never write RANGE into stored data.** Nextcloud's web calendar, Evolution, the Android calendar provider (so DAVx5) and by every secondhand account Apple Calendar all split with UNTIL and a new UID. On a split, Nextcloud drops the overrides after the point by design, Evolution leaves them in the old resource, Android doesn't carry them. On a master start change, Thunderbird and Nextcloud re-key overrides, EXDATEs and UNTIL by the delta; Evolution and Android don't, and the DAVx5 FAQ calls editing a master with exceptions "error-prone". No client pushes a master's field change into existing overrides, since each override stores its own copy of every field.
- **Google, Outlook and Apple themselves.** All three split for "this and following"; none stores a range. Google's new series is reported to take the old id plus `_R` and the split time. Outlook's dialog says changed occurrences "will be cancelled" and rejoin the pattern; the ActiveSync spec says a pattern change deletes every exception; Graph mails the master and each exception separately on a master change, which implies exceptions survive a field change. Google said in 2009 that a time or frequency change resets the series and a field change reaches every occurrence except a field the occurrence overrode, and hasn't restated either since. Google's undo lasts until the next action, and a "this and following" or "all" delete skips the 30-day trash. Nobody documents whether an undo mails guests again.

So the behavior Better-Cal already promises for local series (exceptions follow the drag, a field change reaches the days that inherited it, every edit undoable) is above every product and client surveyed. On Google that promise can only be kept by carrying the exceptions ourselves, which is what [the model's Google section](series-model.md#google-as-a-contract) planned; the survey confirms there is nothing at Google to lean on for it.

## What the Build Takes From This

- **Every write carries If-Match**, and a 412 is a conflict shown to the person, never an overwrite. The app stores Google's `etag` per row (master and each exception) from every poll and every write's answer; today it stores none. This is also what [Undo for Google](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/172) needs: an undo is a write toward the before state, refused the same way when the event moved on since.
- **A sync is a sync-token delta or a full list, never a full list applied as a delta.** A 410 lists everything and replaces the calendar's rows whole. A changed master arriving without its exceptions must not drop them (Thunderbird's read bug) and must not bring a cancelled day back (the risk the code map found in `GoogleSync::applyChanges`).
- **Exceptions are carried, not trusted.** Whatever the probe shows Google does with exceptions on a master change, the app sends each edited day the plan moved to its new instance and deletes the old, and sends each inherited field Google didn't carry, one call per edited day, then reads the series back and compares it to the plan, exception by exception, before writing local rows from Google's answer. A second sync afterwards must change nothing.
- **"This and following" is a split** (UNTIL on the old series, a shortened COUNT where the series is counted, then a new series), as the model already does locally, with the overrides after the split point re-sent on the new series instead of stranded or dropped, and with the Google-reported `_R` form of the new id noted but not relied on (the app chooses its own ids, which also makes a retry after a lost response safe).
- **A cancelled instance is a skip here and stays one**: read back as an EXDATE on the local series, kept across master changes, and never written back to Google as an EXDATE line unless the probe shows Google keeps both forms in step.
- **"This occurrence" sends only the changed fields** to the instance id, never a body carrying an empty `recurrence`, which the code map flags as what the app sends today.
- **A rule Google can't hold** (the clamped month end, a set position, anything the probe shows rewritten or refused) stays a refused reading on Google calendars, with the reason in the dialog, rather than a rule Google silently rewrites.

## The Probe

The probe runs on a throwaway calendar in a connected account and reads back what Google stored after each write, then polls once more and checks that nothing moves. It is a script, not a session: `tools/google-probe/` writes a report with the request, the answer and the second sync for every case, so it can be rerun when Google changes. Its cases, from the three reports' open questions:

1. **Rule forms.** Insert, then read back: `BYMONTHDAY=-1`; the clamped `BYMONTHDAY=28,29,30;BYSETPOS=-1`; `BYDAY=-1FR`; `BYDAY=MO,TU,WE,TH,FR;BYSETPOS=2`; a WKST; COUNT; UNTIL in UTC, as a local date-time and as a date on an all-day series; BYWEEKNO; BYYEARDAY; HOURLY with BYHOUR; EXRULE; an EXDATE matching no instance; different zones on start and end. For each: accepted or refused (with the error), and the exact text that comes back (order of parts, WKST, COUNT kept or rewritten, UNTIL precision).
2. **Each exception kind alone**, on a timed weekly series and on an all-day one: an instance moved to another date, an instance with its own time, a title-only edit, a cancellation (by DELETE on the instance and by PATCH to cancelled, compared), and one beyond a shortened end. After each: the instance id form, whether the master's `recurrence` gained an EXDATE for a cancellation, whether the master's etag or `updated` changed, and what the sync-token delta lists.
3. **Master changes over those exceptions**, one at a time from the same starting state: the start time of day; the length; the zone; the start date (same rule); the rule (a weekday changed, COUNT and UNTIL changed, the clamped form); a title, a description, a location, a status. After each: are the exceptions kept with their old `originalStartTime`, re-keyed, or dropped; did a field the exception had inherited take the new value; what did the delta list; and does a second sync change anything.
4. **The split.** Trim with UNTIL and trim with COUNT, then insert the new series, over exceptions before and after the cut: what the old series still holds (cancelled records included), what the new one has, whether the two share an `iCalUID`, and what the delta lists.
5. **Etags and conditional writes.** Stability across reads; whether a master change alters unmodified instances' etags and the reverse; If-Match on PATCH and DELETE of masters and instances, with the 412 confirmed; whether instances in a `singleEvents=false` list carry their own etag.
6. **Deletes and their reverse.** A deleted event's record with `showDeleted`; whether PATCH to `status: confirmed` brings it back under the same id or an insert with a client-chosen id is needed; the same for a cancelled instance; and whether an insert's chosen id survives.
7. **Sync.** How soon a write made here appears in the next sync-token delta (the echo the app relies on); whether a master changed at Google re-sends its cancelled instances in the delta; what an unbounded series does under `singleEvents=true` with a sync token, which allows no `timeMax`; the 730-occurrence cap through the API.
8. **Mail.** With `sendUpdates=none`, no mail for master and instance patches and a status-only change, checked against a guest mailbox the probe owns; the default when the parameter is omitted.
9. **Two live checks from the audit of closed issues**, while a calendar is at hand: a mirror upload that breaks and then finishes with Try again, and Adopt as local reversing a mirror.

Each case writes what Google answered; the probe makes no claims the read-back doesn't show. The answers go into [the model's Google section](series-model.md#google-as-a-contract) as the contract the build then implements, and into the harness's Google stand-in so the plan's occurrences and exceptions are checked against what Google actually stores.

**Run 2026-11-07** against a throwaway calendar: 66 cases, 877 requests, everything it made deleted after. [The report](research/google-series/probe-2026-11-07.md) has every request and read-back; the contract drawn from it is in the model's Google section. The short version: Google accepts every rule form the editor writes (and refuses a local-time UNTIL and HOURLY), resets every exception when a series' clock, zone, start day or rule changes, overwrites exceptions' own fields when a field or status changes for all events, keeps them on a length or bound change, cancels a skipped day as an instance record with no EXDATE, changes every etag of a series on any write and answers 412 to a stale If-Match, and brings a deleted event or a cancelled day back by a PATCH to confirmed under the same id. The mail group ran against a guest mailbox the same day: `sendUpdates=none` sends nothing for an insert, a master patch, an instance patch or a status change, and a patch with the parameter left out mails the guest an "Updated invitation".
