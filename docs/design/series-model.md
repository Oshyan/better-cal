# The Series Model

Where every occurrence of a repeating series goes when the series is edited, and what the person sees before it happens. The calls made here where more than one reading was defensible are listed, with what each decided against, in [decisions.md](decisions.md). Written before the 1.5 rewrite starts: first draft 2026-11-02, revised four times the same day, after each of four outside design reviews and my direction on them. This is the table the roadmap's "Decisions to make first" asks for. Every example is invented. The long worked examples, the full grid, the harness cases and the prediction cases are in [series-model-examples.md](series-model-examples.md).

## What This Is

One function decides where each occurrence goes, and every path that changes a series asks it: a whole-series edit, "this and following", a delete (this, following, all), an attendance answer, a Google calendar, Undo, and the saves of the editor and quick add. It takes the series as stored and the edit, and returns a **plan**: the series as it will be, in words and next dates, what happens to every edited and skipped day, any refusal with its reason, and, where the edit can reasonably mean more than one thing, the readings as choices, each with its own dates. The paths only write what a confirmed plan says.

Why one model: since 1.4.0 the series code has been fixed one reviewer's finding at a time, and each fix added a branch (The Paths on the Model lists them) whose next bugs came in the following round. patchAll and patchFollowing work out the same things separately, the Google paths work out less, and Undo restores whatever they wrote. The harness that shipped with 1.4.6 names twelve causes still open (cause 1 is fixed since); run at 976f49a (3,000 cases, seed 1) it has 2,011 failing checks, all under them (counts per cause in the [examples](series-model-examples.md#the-harnesss-causes-resolved)).

The first draft aimed to do the right thing automatically. The first review and my direction changed that: mathematical correctness isn't what a person expects. People think of the series they have in mind ("something on the 30th"), not of February, and their expectations come from calendars that mostly don't rewrite rules at all. So the model understands every consequence of a change but doesn't assume intent where it can lay out the reasonable readings. Every change to a series goes through one confirmation step that says what the series becomes and offers the readings as choices when there is more than one. Series changes are infrequent, so one extra step is a fair price, and it makes Undo useful: the person knew what the change would do before it did it. The later reviews found where the arithmetic still assumed the old behavior, where choices met badly and where a default didn't fit the request; each revision settles what they found, and the [examples](series-model-examples.md#the-reviewers-cases) say where.

Where one reading is clear, I'm keeping the higher bar over Thunderbird's and Nextcloud's fixed shift (move every exception by the same number of days and leave the rule alone): the rule follows the drag ("every Monday" dragged to Tuesday is "every Tuesday"), an edited day stays with the occurrence it belongs to, and the end moves too. Where another calendar's behavior is a reasonable reading ("on the 30th, skipping months without one"), it's one of the choices, in the words a person would use. Nothing is lost or added without the plan naming it.

## Vocabulary

- **Series:** a master row (`rrule` and no `recurrence_parent_id`) with its rule, start, zone (`tzid`), all-day flag, length, skipped days and edited days.
- **Rule:** the pattern (FREQ, INTERVAL, the BY parts, WKST) plus the bound. `Recurrence::pattern` is the rule without its bound.
- **Rule family:** the shape of a pattern that decides how it follows a drag. The rows of the table.
- **Member:** one part of a rule that produces occurrences: a listed weekday, day of the month, nth weekday, set position or month. A rule with no list has one member. Every occurrence is produced by one member, and an edit says what each member becomes.
- **Capability:** which edits a rule allows, worked out from its parts (Imported Rules, by Capability). Every family in the table allows all of them.
- **Occurrence:** a day the rule produces, named by its **key**: the UTC instant the expander (`Series\RuleIterator`, read by the window and the model alike) yields for it; for an all-day series, its date as a UTC midnight. The key is the occurrence's RECURRENCE-ID (RFC 5545 3.8.4.4) and Google's `originalStartTime`.
- **Raw occurrences:** what the rule produces with no skips applied. COUNT counts them, skipped ones included (RFC 5545 3.8.5.3).
- **Period:** what the rule repeats in: a day, a week (starting on WKST, Monday by default), a month or a year, all read in the series' zone.
- **Phase:** for an INTERVAL over 1, which periods the rule counts (every other week from its start's week). The start's period carries it, and for a weekly rule, WKST.
- **Exception:** a skipped day (an EXDATE in `exdates_json`) or an edited day (an override row keyed by `recurrence_instance_utc`). An exception belongs to the occurrence its key names, wherever it shows.
- **Own field, inherited field:** an edited day's field is its own when the person set it for that day; the row lists its own fields. Every other field is inherited and follows the series. Date and clock time are fields like the title (Edited Days and Their Own Fields).
- **Bound:** COUNT, UNTIL (inclusive; a date for an all-day series) or none.
- **Follow:** the rule is rewritten to name the day the dragged occurrence landed on, so every occurrence moves with it.
- **Realign:** what `alignedStart` does today (the start snaps to the rule's next day). The model never realigns an existing series; only a new one is aligned.
- **The day acted on:** the occurrence the edit was made from (`instanceStart`), the dragged one for a drag. For an edited day, the occurrence its key names.
- **Image:** the occurrence an old occurrence becomes: what its member's new form produces in its period, shifted. A **loss** is an old occurrence with no image; a **gain** is a new occurrence that is no old one's image (every 31st moved to the 1st gains the months without a 31st).
- **Δ:** whole days from the day acted on to the day it was dropped on, each read in its own zone (old zone before, new zone after). **k:** the months (or years) between their months (or years). **δ:** for a list, the change of the dragged occurrence's day within its month, counted as its member counts (by number, or from the end): the 15th and the last day, its last day of June (the 30th) dragged to 29 July (the third-last), is δ = -2, so every member + δ is the 13th and the third-last day and the dragged day lands where it was dropped.
- **Reading:** one way to understand an edit where more than one is reasonable. Readings come in groups (how the rule reads, which bound to keep, what edited days do); the plan offers each group as a choice with one reading preselected. Two readings merge only when their whole plans are the same (Finding the Readings).
- **Plan, preview, commit:** the model's answer for an edit; asking for it without writing; writing it once confirmed.

## Preview and Confirm

This is the one place the app asks before an undoable change. Elsewhere the toast's Undo is the better answer (ConfirmDialog.js says so), but a series change moves days the person can't see, and Undo alone tells them something went wrong but not what.

### What Goes Through It

- A drag or resize of an occurrence with "This and following" or "All events".
- An editor save with "This and following" or "All events", whatever it changes (dates, rule, end, title, reminders, calendar).
- A move to another calendar (dropped on the sidebar) with following or all.
- Delete following, delete all.
- Attendance and relationship with following or all.

What needs no dialog: any "this event" change (a drag, resize, editor save, delete or answer for one day, and "Use series value"), every change to an event that doesn't repeat, a copy, and Undo. The drop chip keeps its four buttons; "This event" acts at once as now, and the two series choices read "This + following…" and "All events…", since they open the dialog.

### The Plan

The plan is data; the client writes the sentences in the user's format, from one module of templates reviewed as prose. The server sends rules as their parts (each rule's text, its start in its zone, its zone, all-day), bounds as kinds and numbers (none, a count, an end date, with the bound before the change beside it), the next dates as instants with whether the series goes on past them, the counts, every day as its key, new key, the dates it shows on before and after, outcome, kept and takes, and every choice as its id, its data and its options' data; refusals and notes are codes with their data. Its `words` fields stay for API callers and are never read by the panel. Every template is in web/src/lib/planwords.js, and every date and time goes through web/src/lib/dates.js, so the user's locale and 12- or 24-hour setting apply everywhere in the panel; the examples below are in American order and 12-hour only because that is one user's setting.

What the panel shows, top to bottom, every block only when it has something to say. The panel is docked in one place (The Dialog, Desktop and Phone), and its body is two columns: each block has a label in a left column of one width (104 pixels), in the app's small uppercase section-label style, and its content on the right. No block starts at another indent; what reads as a list is rows, separated by 6 pixels, and blocks are separated by a hairline. Every row is sentences a person would say, never field names or code words.

1. **Header:** line one, the action in bold ("Move all events") with a small outlined "Change scope" button at the right, like every other small button in the app: it cancels the preview (the dragged block goes back) and returns to the scope chooser, the drop chip at the drop point after a drag, or the editor's Apply to select after a save. The verb comes from the kind of change: Move for a drag, Rename for an editor save of the title alone, Change for a resize, any other save or a zone change. Line two, the calendar's color disc, then the series' name in single quotes in the body weight, then "on" and its calendar ("'Sample practice' on Practice"); a long name is cut with an ellipsis on one line and shown in full on hover. When the edit was started from a day that has something of its own, a third line in the secondary weight names it: "Started from 'Sample practice 6' on Fri, Dec 11."; an ordinary day gets no line.
2. **CHANGE:** the change sentence names only what changes, with the rest as context, while the frequency and interval stay: "From Tuesday to Wednesday each week, at 7:00 PM, with no end, as before.", "From the 31st to the 30th each month, at 6:00 PM, skipping months without one, with no end, as before.", "From 9:00 AM to 10:00 AM every Monday, with no end, as before.", "From the first and third Monday to the second Wednesday and third Monday each month, at 7:00 PM, with no end, as before." Only when the frequency itself changes does it give both whole rules ("From weekly on Tuesday at 7:00 PM to monthly on the 15th at 7:00 PM, …"). The end reads "with no end, as before", "with no end (was 6 events)", "ending Nov 1, as before", "ending Nov 1 (was Oct 31)", "for 6 events, as before" or "for 10 events (was 6)". A change of start alone: "Starts Mon, Feb 1, 2027 (was Fri, Jan 1). Still monthly on the 1st at 4:00 PM, for 6 events, as before."; when the days don't change at all, "Still weekly on Tuesday at 7:00 PM, with no end, as before." An editor save's changed fields come first, one sentence each ("Title from 'Choir' to 'Chorus'."). The readings of the rule, the bound choice and the zone choice sit under it, each one sentence of reason ending in a question, then its options, each on its own line with its label. A reading's dates run from the day acted on until they include its first date that differs from the other reading's, so the lists show the difference ("Tue, Mar 30, 2027 · Fri, Apr 30 · Sun, May 30 … Sun, Jan 30, 2028 · Tue, Feb 29"), and every reading whose days before the day acted on differ from the series as it is today, the preselected one included, says so under its dates: "also changes earlier days, from Mon, Nov 2". "This change can mean two things:" with "Move every Monday to Friday" and "Shift the whole weekly pattern"; "The series is set to 6 events, and the day you moved would fall after the sixth. Keep the end date, or keep the count?" with "Keep the end: 10 events, ending Nov 1" and "Keep 6 events; the moved day becomes an event of its own"; "The series moves to Berlin time. Keep the clock time, or the same moment?" with each option's local times. The adoptions, one line per sentence kind (Changing the Series from an Edited Day): a day, "This day was moved earlier to a date of its own. Should its new day become the series' day, or every event move the same way?" with "Friday becomes its day: weekly on Friday" and "Every event moves a day later: weekly on Wednesday"; a time, "This day has a time of its own. Should its new time become the series' time, or every event move by the same amount?" with "8:00 PM becomes its time: every event at 8:00 PM" and "Every event moves half an hour later: 7:30 PM, this one at 8:00 PM"; a length, "This day has a length of its own. Should its new length become the series' length, or every event change by the same amount?" with "2 hours becomes its length: every event 2 hours long" and "Every event lengthens by half an hour, this one 2 hours long". The preselected option is first. A refused change shows its reason in place of the sentence; a refused reading is a disabled option with its reason, and the rest of the plan stands.
   For a split ("this and following", delete following, an answer from a day on), CHANGE starts with the old part, from the plan's `split`: "Events before Tue, Jan 19 stay as they are, now ending Jan 12 (2 events).", then the part's change sentence as for all events. A delete following has no part: its CHANGE is "Deletes Tue, Jan 19 and every event after it.", its edited days rows saying "'…' on <date> is deleted." and its removed days the skips that go with it; it has no COMING UP and no AFFECTS. The header reads "Move this and following", "Delete this and following", and the action button the same.
3. **COMING UP:** the next three events as they will show, on one line: "Wed, Nov 11 · Wed, Nov 18 · Wed, Nov 25 …", the ellipsis whenever the series goes on past them and none when it ends with them, never a control. They are listed from where the day acted on lands, or from where the request put it when that is earlier (a day at its own date, a zone kept at its moment); a start moved alone names no day, so they are listed from the new start; and they are read from the plan's own writes: the master's expansion, its skips, every edited row at its new key and place, and any row the plan creates, so they are what the series shows after the commit. The expansion is read from the series' start, since a rule's days follow from its start and its count, and on only as far as the list needs, so a series however old lists from that day. An edited day counts by the day it shows on, wherever its key is: one on a date of its own before that day isn't next, one keyed earlier but shown after it is.
4. **AFFECTS:** "All events, with no end. 11 were changed earlier (title), 1 was removed." or "13 events, ending Jan 20, 2027. 11 were changed earlier (title, location and 2 others), 1 was removed."; the parenthesis names the fields the changed days have of their own, in the editor's words, most common first, at most two named and the rest counted (from the days' `kept` lists); a change of the end or the count alone (the days themselves unchanged) says instead what it becomes, what it was and what stays: "Ends Jul 1, 2027 (was Oct 1). The 6 events before it are unchanged." / "10 events (was 6). The 6 events before the new ones are unchanged."; the second sentence only when a day is edited or skipped. The number is the plan's. Then one sentence for each class of days the change loses or adds (Bounds, Losses and gains by class), from its examples and its rate: "No 31st in some months, so Feb 15, Apr 15 and Jun 15 have no event, and 5 in every 12 months after them.", "Jan 4 and Jan 11 are before the new start.", "Nov 15 and Dec 15 are after the new end.", "Adds Mar 1, May 1 and Jul 1, and 5 in every 12 months after them."; a bounded series says its count instead of a rate ("Feb 15, Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Nov 15 and 2 more have no event").
5. **EDITED DAYS:** every day changed earlier, as a row. A row says what was done to the day earlier and what happens to it now, and of its fields only one that changes ("takes the new reminders", "is renamed to 'Chorus'"); what stays is never said. A day is named by its own title, or the series' when it has none of its own, and what it has of its own follows its name in a parenthesis only when it is more than its title: "'Sample practice 10', with its own title and location, on Tue, Jan 5, 2027 moves to Wed, Jan 6.", but "'Sample practice 1' on Tue, Nov 3 moves to Wed, Nov 4." A day at a time of its own after the change has an outcome of its own, so its row states that time: "'Study group' on Tue, Jan 19 stays at 1:00 PM.", and with its own date too, "'Study group' stays on Fri, Jan 22 at 1:00 PM."; a time equal to the series' new time is never said. A day moved earlier: "'Sample practice 6' was moved earlier to Fri, Dec 11.", then its question right under it, "Keep it there, or move it with the rest?", then "(•) Keep it on Fri, Dec 11" "( ) Move it to Sat, Dec 12". A day past the new end: "'Recital' on Sun, Oct 31 falls after the new end." / "Keep it as an event of its own, or delete it?" / "(•) Keep it on its own" "( ) Delete it". A day with its own title under a rename: "'Rehearsal' on Tue, Apr 13 has its own title, 'Rehearsal'." / "Keep its title, or give it the new one too?" / "(•) Keep 'Rehearsal'" "( ) Rename it to 'Chorus'". A day that becomes its own event: "'…' on <date> becomes an event of its own on <date>." A day left with nothing of its own (the outcome `rejoins`): "'…' on <date> goes back to the series; nothing of its own is left." A deleted day: "'…' on <date> is deleted." Every choice in the plan is drawn exactly once, by its own id, on the row of the days it is about, and what it selects is what the commit sends. A day's row lists every choice about that day alone, one under another: a day with its own title and location under a rename and a new place says "'Rehearsal' on Tue, Apr 13 has its own title, 'Rehearsal', and its own location." and then asks both questions. A choice about several days sits on the row of those days, with every other choice about the same days, whatever else each of them does; the row names what the days have rather than that they were changed ("2 days have their own titles ('Choir, extra rehearsal', 'Choir, concert') and locations." with up to three names and then "and N more"; "2 days were moved earlier to dates of their own."), then their range and its button, then each question and its radios. A day that does something of its own (a date or time of its own, becoming an event of its own, deleted, going back to the series) or has a choice about it alone has a row of its own, first, also when a choice about several days names it too. A choice about no day with a row (the day acted on, which is in Coming up) is a row of its own. The remaining days are grouped by their outcome alone: what a day keeps or takes never splits a group. The group says why its days are listed, and what all of them do: where they go, and what every one of them takes from the change, so a length change reads as one ("2 days that were changed earlier stay where they are and take the new length."): "10 days that were changed earlier move to Wednesday." then "Nov 3 to Jan 19, 2027 · [Show 10 days]", the button opening the days as rows of the ordinary form, set in a little; a group starts closed, and a group of one is its day's row. Field names are the editor's own ("location", never "place").
6. **REMOVED DAYS:** an occurrence removed earlier (a skipped day): "'Sample practice' on Tue, Dec 29 was removed earlier, so no event is added on Wed, Dec 30."; one whose date the new rule no longer has: "'Sample practice' on Tue, Dec 29 was removed earlier; the new pattern has no day there."; those before a start moved later, said once: "2 removed days before the new start are no longer needed." ("1 removed day before the new start is no longer needed."); a day the new rule would add at an edge keeps its own reason and question: "The new rule would add Fri, Jan 8 at the start." / "Add it, or leave it out?" / "(•) Leave it out" "( ) Add it".
7. **NOTE:** a clock time that doesn't exist or happens twice on some day ("There is no 2:30 AM on Sun, Mar 14 in Los Angeles, so that one is at 3:30 AM."); on a Google calendar, that Google decides what happens to edited days and this can't be undone (Google, as a Contract).
8. **Cancel and the action:** two buttons, "Cancel" outlined and the action filled, saying what happens with the count when the plan has one: "Move 13 events", "Rename 13 events", "Change 13 events"; "Move all events" for a series with no end. A refused change's button is "Move only this one", the change applied to the one day.

**Later, not applied yet.** A day with its own date gets the keep-or-move question only under a whole-pattern reading, where every day shifts and "move it with the rest" has a clear meaning. Under a member reading (every Tuesday becomes Wednesday, which a drag of a single-weekday series is), a day moved earlier to its own date stays where it is with no question, and its row says so ("'Sample practice 6' was moved earlier to Fri, Dec 11 and stays there.").

**Dates.** Every date and instant in a plan has one role, and the server writes each role with one writer, so no value is read in a zone it wasn't written in. An occurrence key is the identity instant in UTC (`2027-04-14T02:00:00Z`), and so is the `start` of an anchored series, whose stored start is no occurrence. An identity day (a day's `day`) is the day an occurrence is about: its old pattern day in the old series' zone. A pattern day (a skip's `to`, a gain, the bound's `last`, an edge's dates, a reading's `earlier` with `earlierSeries`) is a day of one named series, old or new, and two series' days are never compared as days. A pattern instant (a reading's `dates` and `then`) is an occurrence of its rule as a list entry `{start, allDay, tz?}`, the form every coming-up entry and option list takes too. A placement (`before`, `after`, `ownDate`) is where an event is: an all-day row's date as itself, or a timed row's date and clock of one instant in one zone, with `tz` when that isn't the plan's zone; `ownDate` is in the row's own zone. A bound instant (`bound.until`) is the rule's UNTIL as written, read in its rule's zone, and a zone-qualified value (`rule.*.start`, a note's `at`) names its zone beside it, or is a date when it has none. A row's outcome is the server's comparison of its instant and kind before and after, never of its dates; the instant is where it starts, so a day that only grows or shrinks stays. The client writes dates from these: a date names its weekday where it is a day ("Wed, Nov 11"), not in an end or a range ("ending Nov 1", "Nov 3 to Jan 19, 2027"), and its year only when it isn't the year of the date before it in the sentence, or this year for the first. It never reads a key's UTC date: read so, Tuesday 7:00 PM in Los Angeles is a Wednesday, and days moved from Tuesday to Wednesday said "move to Thursday". **One zone.** The after side decides: when the series is timed after the change, the plan's `zone` is its zone and every timed value of both sides is written there, an all-day value as its date; when it is all-day after the change, `zone` is null, every all-day value is its date, and a timed day is written in its own zone on both sides with that zone named beside it, never the device's nor an occurrence's from the calendar's window, which carries none (a New York day at its own 00:30, seen from Los Angeles, "moves to Wed, Jan 13 at 9:30 PM").

### The Dialog, Desktop and Phone

**One panel, placed by the width it has.** Every plan, from a drag or from the editor, opens in the same place for the width it has: docked at the bottom right of its lane (below), 480 pixels wide, no taller than the window less 12 pixels above and below, with no dimming backdrop, wherever the lane holds it at that full width; the bottom sheet (Phone, below) wherever it doesn't. Its header and action row are pinned; the body between them scrolls inside it with the always-visible thin scroll bar and the edge shadows. It never moves to follow the dragged block: the block stays in the grid where it was dropped, drawn as pending, and the panel is where it always is. Its size and place are app.css's alone, by the layout the window is in (the renderer's job: SeriesDialog.js writes no style and measures nothing), so a window resized while it is open simply shows it in that layout's place. Enter confirms the preselected choices, Esc or Cancel cancels and puts the block back, a press outside only dismisses (web/src/ui/outside.js). For a Google series, which can't be undone yet, the focus starts on Cancel.

**The lane.** The panel lives in a lane: the window less what the editor drawer or the event panel covers on its right. app.css does it with one variable: each of those panels sets how far it reaches from the window's right edge (`--bc-sd-side`: the editor drawer `min(480px, 94vw)`, the event panel `min(480px, 100vw)`), and the lane (`.bc-sd-lane`, a fixed, size-contained box from the window's left edge to that point) is a container. Docked, the panel's right edge is 12 pixels left of the drawer's or panel's left edge, so neither covers the other. A container query on the lane picks the presentation: a lane narrower than the panel at its full width with its margins (504 pixels) shows the sheet, from the lane's left edge to the window's right edge, over the drawer. So the choice follows the width there is, never the kind of device: beside the drawer that is any window under 984 pixels (at 641 pixels the lane is 161 wide, where a docked panel was 137 wide and pushed its text and Cancel out of the window), and with nothing beside it a desktop window docks the panel down to the phone width. A phone has no room to dock in, whatever lies beside: at 640 pixels and below, and on a touch screen up to 500 high and 1,000 wide, the lane is no width, so the panel is the sheet.

**Sheet (phone, and beside a side panel in a narrow window):** the same panel across the bottom of the window, full width, up to two thirds of the screen, its header and action row pinned and its body scrolling with the drawn scroll rail. Every row is already full width, so the rows are the desktop's, at the sheet's sizes; the action buttons are thumb height. The sheet covers the editor's lower part, as it does on a phone, where the editor is full screen.

**Type.** The body is 13 pixels on the desktop and 15 on the phone; labels are the app's section labels (11 pixels, bold, uppercase, muted); the secondary lines (what a day keeps, a choice's reason, an option's dates) are the muted colour, never a smaller size. The series' name in the header is in the body colour, never the lightest text on the card.

### While the Dialog Is Up

The drop waits for the dialog. The dragged block stays where it was dropped, drawn as pending (dashed outline, no fill), and nothing else on the calendar changes, since drawing the others as moved would show a guess. The panel opens at once with what the client knows and "Working out the plan" in place of the rest, the action button disabled until the plan arrives (no timer: the answer fills it). Cancel puts the block back. Confirm sends the commit, draws the block solid, and refreshes the window from the server's answer. A 409 `series_plan_changed` shows the new plan in the same panel and a line saying the series changed; any other error puts the block back with the error toast.

The editor works the same way: Save asks for the plan with the form left open; Cancel returns to the form unchanged; Confirm saves and closes the drawer. Its save is quiet: the panel stays hidden while the plan needs no one (a direct plan, with no choice and no refusal) and that plan is committed at once. Hidden is a property of the plan, not of the save: every plan that arrives, from the preview or as a commit's 409 `series_plan_changed` or 422 `series_refused`, goes through the same rule, so a replacement plan that needs someone shows the panel, which then stays shown, and one that still needs no one is committed; the editor never waits on "Working out the plan…" for a plan it already has.

### Creating a Series

A new series needs no dialog, but it gets the plan's information where it's made. Quick add's draft card shows a line under its chips: the rule in words and the first three dates ("Monthly on the 30th, or the last day · Sat 30 Jan, Sun 28 Feb, Tue 30 Mar · 6 events"), and when the start moved to the rule's first day, "Starts Tue 2 Mar, the first Tuesday on or after the date you typed". The editor's Repeat row shows the same line while a rule is set. Each change of the repeat controls asks for a preview; a newer request replaces an older one, with no debounce timer.

### After: Toast and Activity

The toast stays short ("Choir · changed how it repeats", with Undo). The Activity entry carries the plan's summary, ownership changes included ("Two edited days became events of their own: 'Rehearsal' on 13 Apr and 'Rehearsal' on 11 May"). One builder writes it for every whole-series change and the part of a split: its reach ("all events", or "from Tue 19 Jan on"), the rule where it changes, each clock with its zone when the zone changes and the choice made ("keeping the clock time", "keeping the same moment"), then each field changed by its values ("title from 'Choir' to 'Chorus'"). Undoing it says what it put back the same way.

### The API

- `PATCH /events/:id` and `DELETE /events/:id` take `preview: true` (in the body, or `?preview=1` on DELETE) with the same fields, `scope` and `instanceStart` as the change itself. The answer is 200 `{plan}` and nothing is written, journaled or sent to Google. `POST /events/:id/attendance` and `/relationship` take it too, and `POST /events` with an `rrule` (a creation plan: words, aligned start, first occurrences, bound).
- The commit is the same request without `preview` and with `plan: {hash, choices: {group: optionId}}`, naming an option for every group the plan has. The server works out the plan again inside the write's transaction, with the series' rows locked, from the rows as they are and the choices sent. When its hash differs from the one sent, nothing is written and the answer is 409 `series_plan_changed` with the new `{plan}`, as proposals answer `proposal_changed`. A refused plan or reading is 422 `series_refused` with `{plan}`, its `refusals` saying why.
- The hash covers every column of the series' rows as read, their tags, people and the trip links naming them, the edit and the chosen options, so a preview made before any other change to the series (another tab, a feed poll, a sync, a tag edit that touches no timestamp) is refused at commit.
- **A series change sent without `plan`** (an agent through the API or MCP, an older client) is applied at once only when its plan says `"direct": true`, which it does exactly when the edit is field-only and has no consequence beyond those fields: scope all; only title, place, description, url, reminders, status, icon, tags, people or attendance changed; no rule, start, date, time, length, zone, all-day or bound change, of the series or of the day acted on; and no edited day owning a changed field, the one the request names included, so no group exists. Anything else, "this and following" included (a split changes a bound), is 409 `series_plan_required` with the plan, and the caller commits it with its choices. A preselected option is a default for Enter, not a choice: it's never taken as the caller's, and a commit missing a group is 409 again. The MCP tool for event edits learns to show the plan and commit it.
- `POST /quickadd` drafts carry `series` (the creation plan) when they have an `rrule`.
- The plan holds `hash`, `scope`, `direct`, `rule` (before, after and their words), `start`, `bound`, `next`, `groups` (each with its options and the one selected), `days` (every exception: key, new key, outcome, words), `losses`, `gains`, `refusals`, `notes` and `summary`. One, as JSON, is in the [examples](series-model-examples.md#a-plan-as-json).

Each option carries its own words, next three dates, bound, and `differs` (the first dates where it parts from the preselected option, however far out); the rest of the plan is for the selected options, and picking another asks for a new preview. The server's words (one sentence generator in PHP, kept in step with web/src/lib/repeat.js by a shared table tested on both sides) are for the Activity entry and an agent; the panel writes its own sentences from the plan's data (The Plan), in the user's format, and never reads them.

## Edited Days and Their Own Fields

### Own Fields

An edited day records which of its fields are its own. A series-wide change reaches every field an edited day doesn't own; its own fields stay. Equal values say nothing about intent: a day renamed "Rehearsal" in a series later renamed "Rehearsal" still owns its title, and the next rename of the series leaves it alone.

The fields: `date` (the day it shows on), `time` (its clock time and zone), `length`, `allDay`, `title`, `location` (with its coordinates and provenance), `description`, `url`, `reminders`, `status` (planned or maybe), `attendance`, `tags`, `people`, `icon`. Not per day: the calendar (an edited day moved to another calendar becomes an event of its own, [#186](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/186)), the uid and the rule.

What owning a field means for the dates: an own `date` is that date, kept when the series' days move (the plan offers to move it this once, keeping its distance from its occurrence; it stays its own either way); an inherited `date` is its occurrence's day, wherever that goes. An own `time` is that clock time in that zone; an inherited one is the series' clock time. So a day moved to Thursday with its clock time untouched follows a later change of the series to 20:00. Read structurally: on its occurrence's day an inherited time is its new key's instant as the expander gives it; on a date of its own it is the time it shows moved by the series' clock change, the delta every inherited value takes, so an edit that changes no clock leaves it where it is (a rule several times a day included, whose times are not one clock); a day of the other kind until now, all-day in a series made timed, has no time to move and takes the series' clock.

Storage: a new column, `override_fields JSON NULL`, on override rows: the list of its own field names, `null` on masters and on events that don't repeat. Rows keep holding every field's value, own or inherited, so everything that reads events (the window, CalDAV, ICS export, outfeeds, search) reads rows exactly as today. Only writers consult the list: a series-wide write sets each edited row's inherited fields in the same transaction, the plan lists those writes, and Undo snapshots them. Per-field null meaning "inherit" fails where null already means something (no place, default reminders).

What writes the list: a "this event" edit adds the fields it changed, even when the value typed equals the series' (the person chose it); "Use series value" removes a field and copies the series' value back; an edit with scope all from that day clears the fields it sets (the person meant the series' value). A row also exists for what refers to it: a day in a trip (a trip with stops in it, or a stop of another trip, an `event_links` row naming it either way) owns its trip, read from its links and never stored in the list, so while a link names it the day keeps its row, the plan says it keeps its trip, and a field given back to the series leaves the row in place. The same holds for everything else that names a row, by one table of what refers to it (`Series\OwnFields::REFERENCES`): a duplicate decision (an `event_duplicates` pair linked or dismissed; a possible pair is the scan's proposal, which it makes again) and plugin data (`event_plugin_data`), each listed in the day's `kept` by its kind, so a rejoin never cascade-deletes them. An edited day left with nothing of its own, no field and nothing that names it, is just an occurrence again, and its row is deleted (with its key in Undo). That is a write rule, the same for every writer: "Use series value" and a whole-series plan alike, where the plan names the day's outcome as `rejoins` and deletes its row in the commit, Undo putting the row, its tags, people and links back as they were. Rows written from elsewhere follow the rule below.

The migration (`063_override_fields.php`) adds the column and fills it for every existing override row by comparing it with its master: `date` when the row's local day differs from its key's, `time` when its clock time or zone differs from the series', `length`, `allDay`, `title`, `location` (text), `description`, `url`, `icon`, `status` and `attendance` when they differ, `reminders` when the normalized lists differ, `tags` and `people` when the sets differ. It changes no value, so nothing shows differently after it; it runs in batches and only on rows whose list is still null.

What the backfill can't know: a value set on purpose to equal the series' (it reads as inherited), and a value that differs only because a series-wide edit before 1.5 never reached the row (today none does). The backfill reads that title as the day's own, which keeps what it shows; the first series rename after the migration says so ("1 edited day keeps its own title: 'Choir' on 9 Mar. [ ] Give it the new title too"), so the guess is visible and fixed in one tick.

In the editor: an edited day shows a line under the title, "Edited day of Choir (weekly on Tuesday). Its own: date, title. Use series values", and each own field has a small reset icon, "Use series value (Choir)", as reminders have "Reset to default". Using it is a "this event" change, one Undo, no dialog. The event view's series line says it in short ("Repeats weekly · moved from Tue 9 Mar").

### Rows from Elsewhere

A feed poll, a Google sync or a CalDAV client's PUT brings an edited day's full values and no intent. The equality backfill is right once, for history the app never had; run on every write it would undo what the app knows (a day owns "Rehearsal", the series is renamed "Rehearsal", Google echoes both unchanged, and equality would call the title inherited). So the list follows from what actually changed, field by field, against the row as stored:

- **Unchanged** (the other end echoing what the app wrote or last read): the field keeps its ownership, whatever it is.
- **Changed at the other end:** its own when the new value differs from the master's value in the same sync; inherited when it equals it, which is a series-wide change the other app carried to the day, or the day put back to the series' value there.
- **A row the app hasn't seen before** (an edited day made elsewhere), or a field whose ownership is unknown: by equality with its master, as the backfill.

A person's local changes to such a row (attendance, tags, reminders) are its own and kept across polls, as today. The rule needs the rows the app writes to be what the other end echoes, so where the app doesn't carry inherited values there (Google before 1.5.3), it doesn't write them locally either.

### All-Day Is One of Them

The first draft's decision 8 (every edited day takes the series' all-day flag) is reversed. `allDay` is a field like the others: a day that inherits it follows the toggle, and a day deliberately timed in an all-day series (allDay and time its own) stays timed at its own time when the series is toggled either way. The editor shows it ("Its own: all-day off, 09:00"), and the plan names it ("1 edited day keeps its own time: 'Sample Hall visit' on 13 Apr at 09:00"). An own length has no meaning across a change of kind by inheritance: a day that owns its length but inherits its all-day flag changes kind with the series, and its length (seconds, or whole days) can't be carried into the other kind. The model keeps the length it can, and the harness checks no own length on such a day.

### Changing the Series from an Edited Day

A drag from an edited day says two things: how the series changes, and where this day ends up. There is nothing to translate: every edit is measured against the occurrence acted on as it shows, and an edited day is simply an occurrence that shows somewhere of its own. Let K be the day's key (its occurrence), S where it shows (its row, or the series at K when it has none), and S' where it was put.

1. **Measure the displayed change.** Δ is the whole days from S to S', each read in its own zone; the clock change is from S's clock time to S''s; the length change likewise; the all-day flag and the zone are changed or not, from S to S'. The same S and S' come from a request to the day's own row and from one to the master naming K: the key names the occurrence, `start` and `end` say where it was put, and nothing downstream knows which route the request took.
2. **The series takes each delta.** The family rules run with K as the day acted on and Δ as its days moved; the series' clock moves by the clock change, its length by the length change, and its all-day flag and zone change only when the occurrence's did. Everything about the series (its rule, start, bound, the other exceptions) follows from that, as for any drag.
3. **The day is an exception like any other.** Its key goes to its image, its new key is the new series' key there, and it lies inside the new series or gets an outcome of its own. What the request set on it is its own value now, kept as every exception's own values are: it shows at S', its `date` and `time` are its own where S' isn't what its image would give it, and the fields sent are its values, so it takes them (and they stop being its own where the series takes the same value). Cut off by the bound the plan keeps, it becomes an event of its own where it was put, never deleted, since it is where the person just put it; cut off by a bound the person sent, it is a day outside like any other, with the outside choice (The Day Acted On Comes First). The other edited days keep what they own.

That reading, **the series moves the same way**, always exists. The other adopts this day, and the date and the clock time are adopted separately, since wanting the new 20:00 doesn't mean wanting the exceptional date:

- **This becomes the series' day:** the family rules run from K to S''s day, so the series' day is where this one now is, and its own date is cleared. Offered whenever this day's date is its own and that drag would be allowed, across a week or month boundary too, its effect on the start and end on its row; when that drag would be refused, the row is disabled with that refusal's reason.
- **This becomes the series' time:** the series takes S''s clock time, and this day's own time is cleared. Offered when the drag changed the clock time and this day's time is its own.

- **This becomes the series' length:** the series takes S''s length, and this day's own length is cleared. Offered when the change gave the day another length and its length is its own.

Each is its own group (`day`, `time`, `length`) beside the same-way reading for that part, shown only when the change moved that part and the day owns it. Adopting is one rule for every part: the delta is measured from the occurrence instead of from where the day shows, which is the same-way delta plus the day's own offset for that part (its own date less its key's day, its own clock less the series', its own length less the series'); nothing else differs (`Model::ADOPTIONS`, built 2026-11-05). Preselected, by part and by operation: the day when adopting moves every event by less than one period of the rule's frequency (open decision 1), the time always, and the length when the value was typed into the editor (a typed value names the value wanted, "set the series to two hours"), the same way for a drag (a resize is a delta). A zone or all-day change is its own reading, so neither offers an adoption.

Measured in days on the occurrence, the first review's "Book club" (its 1 Jan moved to 31 Jan, dragged to 1 Feb) moves every event a day or adopts the 1st, never a key moved a month ([examples](series-model-examples.md#from-an-edited-day)).

"This and following" from an edited day is measured the same way and applies the result to the part from K on. The split is by key, so an edited day keyed after the split but shown before it goes with the following part, and the plan says so: "Includes 'Rehearsal', moved to Fri 6 Mar, which belongs to Tue 9 Mar." A delete, an answer or a relationship with all events or this and following, sent to an edited day's own row, is the series' change at K too, preview and commit alike: one routing for every series change from an edited day, whatever day the request names. From an edited day moved to another calendar, "This and following" stays unavailable ([#188](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/188)).

## Mapping, Defined

### The Function

Given the old series (rule, start, bound, zone, all-day flag, length, edited days with their own fields) and the edit (the day acted on, where it was seen, where it was dropped, its new clock time, zone, all-day flag and length, the fields, any rule sent, and the chosen readings), the model returns a plan. In order:

1. **A sent rule that is unchanged is no rule.** The sent rule is unchanged when its pattern equals the stored one and its bound names the same end. Patterns are compared after dropping INTERVAL=1, reading every list part as the set it is (RFC 5545 3.3.10: a BYDAY, BYMONTHDAY, BYMONTH or any other list sent in another order is the same list, in a rule the drag table names and in an imported one alike), and reading a weekly BYDAY of the start's weekday alone, a monthly BYMONTHDAY equal to the start's day (a yearly BYMONTH and BYMONTHDAY equal to the start's) as bare, since a bare rule's one member is its start's. WKST counts wherever RFC 5545 3.3.10 makes it count, a WEEKLY rule with INTERVAL over 1 and BYDAY, or a YEARLY rule with BYWEEKNO, and is ignored in comparing anywhere else; it is never dropped from a rule that is written. Bounds are equal when the COUNTs are, or when both UNTILs have the same last raw occurrence at or before them (the editor writes 23:59:59 of the end date in the event's zone; the model writes the occurrence itself). "At or before" is read at the coarser of the two forms: an UNTIL that is a date reaches every occurrence on that day or earlier, and an instant against an all-day series reaches the days up to its own day in the zone it was written for. So the stored end sent back in the other kind's form, as the editor writes it beside an all-day toggle (its last day as a date, or 23:59:59 of it), is the same end, never a new one. So it no longer matters that the editor sends the rule on every save ([#199](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/199)). A sent rule with the same pattern and a different bound changes only the bound. A different pattern is a new rule.
2. **The capability is checked.** The rule's capability decides which parts of the edit it takes; anything else is a refusal with its reason.
3. **The change is measured, once, against the occurrence acted on as it shows.** The key names the occurrence (`instanceStart`, whichever row id the request came through); where it shows is its edited row when it has one (its own date, time, zone, length and all-day flag), the series' values at its key when it hasn't; a request naming no occurrence acts on the series' own values at its first one, and what it moves is the series' start: put on a day the stored pattern gives, it is a start (Bounds), every occurrence its own image; put anywhere else, the pattern changes too, and the first occurrence moves there as a drag. Every part of the edit is a delta from there: Δ, k and δ, the clock change, the length change, the all-day flag and the zone (changed or not, never an absolute value passed through), each content field against the occurrence's own value where it owns the field and the series' where it inherits it, tags and people the same way, a series column (the trip flag, which no row owns) against the series' value, the rule and its bound against the stored ones. Setting a field back to the series' value from a day that owns another is a delta on that field like any other. The series takes each delta, never the acted key's values as absolutes: a later time of a rule several times a day, or a day after a skipped hour whose clock the expander shifted, lengthened and nothing else, changes the length and nothing else.
4. **The readings are found** (Finding the Readings).
5. **For the chosen readings, everything is mapped.** The rule follows. Every exception's key goes to its image (Correspondence, by Member), looked up among the new rule's raw occurrences. The start and bound follow from the images (Bounds). Losses and gains are found over the series' whole span (No Loss, Proved per Family). Two old occurrences never share an image, and every mapped exception lies inside the new series or has an outcome of its own (an event of its own, or deleted, as chosen).
6. **The plan** is the result: the series' new columns, every row write, the words, the next occurrences, every exception's outcome, the losses and gains, the refusals, the groups.

### The Day Acted On Comes First

An accepted drag keeps its destination after every bound and choice is applied. Most readings do by construction (the day's image is an occurrence, and the start is the earliest image), but a kept count can end first: "Cello lesson", monthly on the 31st, 6 events from 31 Jan, its 31 Oct dragged to 1 Nov, becomes every 1st from 1 Feb, and six of those end on 1 Jul (probed).

A reading that would leave the day acted on out is offered rewritten to keep it as an event of its own, "Keep 6 events + this one on its own: 1 Feb to 1 Jul, and 1 Nov separately" ("6 in the series + 1 separate"), like an edited day after the end but with no delete choice. One that can't keep it even so is disabled with its reason. The rule is for drags and for the bound the plan keeps; a COUNT or UNTIL the person sent is their own statement, so a day acted on that it cuts off is an ordinary day outside the bound and takes the outside group's choice, kept on its own or deleted (Bounds: Start, End and Days Outside). The other readings are untouched ("Changes 6 events to 10, ends 1 Nov", probed), and "keep the end" is preselected over the split one (Bounds).

### Finding the Readings

The drag table's row, the bound rules, the zone change, edited days with their own date or values for the changed fields, an edited day's adoption, and days outside the new series each give a group. Two readings in a group merge only when their plans are the same: the same occurrences for as long as the rule could run (the walk below, past the bound, not just the series' span), the same start and bound, and the same outcome for every exception, its new key and its own fields. So:

- **The same dates for now, apart later:** both shown, each saying where they part. The clamped and the skipping 30th from 30 Mar with a COUNT of three both give 30 Mar, 30 Apr and 30 May; extended to twelve they part on 29 Feb 2028 (probed). The rows say "the same 3 dates; if extended, 28 or 29 Feb" and "the same 3 dates; if extended, none in February".
- **The same dates, exceptions attached differently:** both shown, each with the affected days' outcomes.
- **The same for all time:** merged. The clamped 31st and the last day; any month-end reading of a series every twelve months in March.
- **One member moved by whole periods:** a list member dragged whole weeks (or months, or years) keeps its days, yet moving it and shifting the whole pattern stay two readings, checked like any others. Mondays and Wednesdays from Mon 4 Jan, its Wed 6 Jan edited, Mon 11 Jan dragged to Mon 18 Jan: **move every Monday a week later** starts on Wed 6 Jan, the edited day staying; **shift the whole pattern a week later** starts on Mon 11 Jan, the edited day moving to Wed 13 Jan (probed). When no rule holds the moved member's images (every third week, Mondays moved two weeks; probed), that reading is disabled with the reason. The list preselection (open decision 1) applies.
- **The series' own form:** a rule the person chose (a clamped 30th, "every 31st", a from-the-end day) is transformed as itself, and that reading comes first. It's never replaced by a simpler rule because the two give the same dates today.

- **The old pattern with its days shifted.** A new pattern sent from the editor is offered the drag's mapping when it is what a drag of the old rule makes. Which drag: the members in their order, each one's first occurrence dragged to each day of its period from the period's first day, and the first of those drags with an enabled reading whose days, from the old start, are the new pattern's days. That earliest drag within a member's period is the one direction the reading gives; a plan that maps by another drag is a finding.

A group left with one reading isn't shown as a choice.

### Correspondence, by Member

Every old occurrence is named by its period and the member that produced it. The transformation says what each member becomes (the dragged member alone, or every member, moved by Δ, by δ or to the landed nth weekday) and by how many periods it shifts (k; under a one-member reading only the moved member shifts). The image is what the member's new form produces in that shifted period.

Members are never found by sorted position: with the 1st and 15th, every 1st moved to the 20th, an edited 15th stays on the 15th, where by position (the old second to the new second) it would land on the 20th.

- **Daily and weekly:** a moved member's occurrence goes to its local day + Δ in the new zone; an unmoved member's stays. Shifting every listed weekday and the week start by Δ keeps each occurrence's place in its week and its week's place in the interval, so this is exact for every daily and weekly row, every other week included.
- **Monthly:** an occurrence is what its member produces in its month, found under the old pattern (the rule without its bound, counted from the month's first day, so a key near the end still finds its member); its image is what the member's new form produces in the month k on. With an INTERVAL over 1, a member moved into a month the interval skips is refused, since the rule can't hold it there.
- **Yearly:** the same, by year and k years. A month list moves as a whole, since a drag across the year is refused.
- **Other capabilities:** same days only.
- **When the member's new form produces nothing** in the target period, there's no image: a loss. Only the skipping month-end readings lose occurrences among the rules the app writes, and they say so.

Two members never produce one day in one period: a moved member landing on another is refused, and the walk (No Loss) checks every period for it, which catches pairs that coincide only sometimes, like the fourth and the last Monday in a month with four (sabre yields one occurrence for both, probed: `BYDAY=4MO,-1MO` gives 22 Feb 2027 once).

Every new key is looked up among the new series' raw occurrences as the expander the app persists and shows yields them (the app's own, over the series as it will be stored, with no display cap), never computed by adding seconds or days to an instant, so a key is always an occurrence: an old key's new key is the new series' key on its image day, at the old key's place among its own day's keys (one key a day for every rule but those several times a day), or the key itself when the new day has it, since a new pattern keeps the keys on its days; a key at that place which another key of the old day keeps as itself is that key's, so no two old keys share one, and a rule several times a day replaced by one with fewer keys a day keeps the keys the two share while an edited day at a time the new day lacks falls outside. The model has one source for days and instants, that expansion; its own arithmetic is for members, periods, phases and images (what corresponds to what) and for words, and the expander, which every expansion reads, yields only a rule's own days: a day its BY parts don't name (a numbered weekday counted in its month or year) is never generated, so it is not counted. Everything that asks whether a key is an occurrence reads that same expansion with the same clock reading: the check that a request's `instanceStart` names an occurrence expands the stored series from its stored start, as the model does, so a day the expander yields (a gap day at its shifted clock) is one the request can act on, and a series several times a day is read without a window budget refusing its later days. When nothing the expansion depends on changes (the rule, the stored start, the zone, the all-day flag), the new series is the stored one and each key maps to itself by construction, with nothing expanded again; a rule the expander can't read can't give new keys, so every edit that needs them is refused and a field change keeps every row as it is. A clock time that doesn't exist on some day, or happens twice, follows the policy in Zones, DST and All-Day Days.

**The day acted on is an exception like any other.** Its key goes to its image under the reading, its new key comes from the expansion as every key's does, and it lies inside the new series or gets an outcome: it is named among the days, counted in the bound, and in the losses only where no outcome names it. What makes it first is the design's own priority rule, that an exception keeps its own values: what the request set on it (where it was put, the fields sent) are its values now. So "the dragged day stays where you put it" is that rule applied to it, a count that would end before it splits it off as an event of its own, and "keep the end" is preselected because it keeps it in the series. Under a new rule the request's placement only sets where the rule starts, so the day acted on has no place of its own there (Every Kind of Edit): it is an ordinary exception, kept where its key is a day of the new rule and otherwise one of the days outside, with the outside choice like any other edited day.

**Its row.** The day acted on is an exception whether or not it had a row: with none, its row is the occurrence as it showed. Its placement is where the request put it, and, as for any row, a placement field is its own where it isn't what its image gives: when the expander's clock for its image differs from where it was put (its image falls on a day whose clock a skipped hour shifts, or at the other instant of a clock that happens twice), it owns its time and shows where it was put. Owning a field makes it an edited day, so the plan creates its row (a `createEdited` write: the series as written, keyed on its new key, its placement and its own list), the one row a whole-series plan creates inside the series. With nothing of its own it stays an occurrence and gets no row.

**What the edit's kind is for.** The plan names the edit (a drag, a time change, a bound, a new rule, fields only) for its words and for `direct`, and for nothing else: capability comes from the capability table over the deltas and the rule's parts, the readings from the drag table over the days delta and the family.

### Naming the Landed Day

- **The fifth weekday is the last.** The 29th to 31st are the fifth of their weekday, which most months lack: a positive count landing there is written `-1`, and a count from the end landing on the fifth from the end is written `1`. This is what the editor's buildRrule writes. One reading: it's what "the second Tuesday" dragged to the last week means, and the dates show it.
- **The month-end readings.** A monthly day landing on the 29th, 30th or 31st offers: **on the 30th, skipping months without one** (`BYMONTHDAY=30`, what Google, Apple and Thunderbird mean by "monthly on day 30"); **on the 30th, or the last day in shorter months** (`BYMONTHDAY=28,29,30;BYSETPOS=-1`; for the 29th, `BYMONTHDAY=28,29;BYSETPOS=-1`); and, when the landed day is its month's last, **on the last day** (`BYMONTHDAY=-1`). For the 31st, the clamped reading is the last day for all time, so they merge into the last day. A yearly day landing on 29 February offers **only in leap years** (`BYMONTH=2;BYMONTHDAY=29`) and **the last day of February** (`BYMONTHDAY=-1`). All probed: sabre expands the clamped 30th as 28 Feb 2027 and 29 Feb 2028, and the clamped 29th the same.
- **No boundary.** The first draft refused the 29th and 30th and took the 31st as the last day; with the readings in front of the person, nothing is refused.
- **From the end.** A day counted from the end, dragged to the nth-last day of its month, offers **the nth-last day** and **that day by number** (with the month-end readings on the 29th to 31st). The from-the-end reading is offered for the last to the seventh-last day, the forms the editor and the series sentence can name; past that, "the 20th day from the end" isn't how anyone thinks of a date, and only the number is offered.
- **Written, not inferred.** A series the person wrote as "every 31st" (`BYMONTHDAY=31`, or bare from a 31st, which RFC 5545 reads the same way) stays that: the model never rewrites it into the last day, a drag by whole months keeps it, and the editor shows it as "on day 31, skipping months without one". The rule behind it: when the dragged member's own form gives the landed day in the landed month, it stays as written and is the one reading (the 30th dragged by whole months is offered no clamp, the clamped 30th no skipping reading). A day counted from the end is the exception its row names: it keeps both readings, the nth-last day and that day by number. A reading picked in the dialog is written in its own form and shown in its own words from then on, so every rule a drag can write is one the editor can show and change.
- **The editor's monthly choices** gain the forms ("on day 30, or the last day in shorter months", "on day 30, skipping months without one", "on the last day", and "the second-last day" to "the seventh-last day" for a series already counting from the end). web/src/lib/repeat.js learns to describe the clamped forms, `-2` to `-7`, and yearly rules with BYMONTH, which today read as "Repeats".
- **Bare rules** are read as their start's day (or month and day) and written back bare when the new rule names the new start's day, which is the same rule for all time.

### Bounds: Start, End and Days Outside

A drag can keep at most two of three things: the number of events, the end, and every old occurrence's image. The model names the conflict instead of picking. The start and the end come from the whole transformation, not from the old start's and end's images alone.

- **Start:** the earliest image of any old raw occurrence, skipped ones included, so it is always an occurrence of the new rule. Usually that's the old start's image, but not when a moved member crosses another: Mondays and Wednesdays from Mon 4 Jan, every Monday moved to Thursday, start on Wed 6 Jan (from Thu 7 Jan, the old start's image, 6 Jan would be lost; probed). Nor when the old start has no image: a series from 15 Feb dragged to "the 30th, skipping months without one" starts on 30 Mar, and the plan says 15 Feb's event is dropped.
- **A start moved alone.** A request that names no day moves the series' start, a bound like the end. When the new start is a day the stored pattern gives, nothing about the days changes: every occurrence is its own image, the series starts there, and the days before it are outside it, as days past a shortened end are. A COUNT counts from the new start; when that changes where the series ends, the bound group offers keeping the count (preselected for a COUNT) or the end (preselected for an UNTIL). When the new start is a day the pattern doesn't give (a Monday series started on a Tuesday), the pattern has to change, and the first occurrence is dragged there. A day named is always a drag: it moves, and its exceptions with it, so dragging the first session shown two weeks later moves every session.
- **Phase:** the start carries it. Every image lies in an old period shifted by the same k (for a weekly rule, in a week its WKST keeps together), so the earliest image's period is one the old rule counted, and an INTERVAL over 1 counts the same periods from it, even when the first period lost everything. The start is the earliest raw image, not the first visible one: a skipped start stays the start, with its EXDATE.
- **A COUNT the person chose is kept by default.** When the drag changes how many periods have an occurrence, the bound group offers **keep 6 events** (the COUNT stays, counted from the new start; the series ends where the sixth falls) and **keep the end** (recounted from the new start through the latest image). The bound line says what each does: "6 events, ends 1 Jul (was 31 Oct)" and "Changes 6 events to 10, ends 1 Nov", and "6 in the series + 1 separate" when an edited day leaves it. A count that would end before the day acted on keeps it separately (The Day Acted On Comes First), and then "keep the end" is preselected: it keeps the dragged day in the series with nothing split off, though it shows more events (Cello lesson: 10, against 6 + 1 separate), which its line says plainly. "Keep N" stays preselected only when the dragged day is among the N.
- **An UNTIL is kept as the end by default:** it becomes the latest image of the old raw occurrences at or before it, written as that occurrence (its instant in UTC for a timed series, its date for an all-day one; never an instant the rule doesn't produce; a rule yields only its own days, so an UNTIL on a 1st it doesn't name is no occurrence, cause 13). When the old last occurrence has no image (monthly on the 15th ending 15 Feb, dragged to the 30th skipping months without one), the end is the latest image there is, 30 Jan, and the bound line says why: "Ends 30 Jan (was 15 Feb): February has no 30th". When the count changes, the group offers "keep N events" too.
- **No display cap.** The expander stops at 1,001 occurrences, and the shipped movedUntil and followingRrule took that cap for the end, cutting a long series short or miscounting a split (Water plants and Stretch in the examples). The model works out a bound's last occurrence and a split's remaining count without the cap: it reads sabre's iterator itself, over the actual span, with no limit but a sanity one (400,000 occurrences).
- **No bound:** gains and losses are named ("adds every February, which had no 30th"), with nothing to choose.
- **How far an unbounded series is read.** From the start through the latest day the change names (the day acted on, where it landed, the start, every skipped and edited day, and the new start, when the earliest image falls after the rest), then one whole cycle of the rule's periods after that day's period, then on by the change's reach. Nothing in it is a chosen number, and nothing in it is an expansion: the old occurrences, their images and the new rule's days are the rules' own arithmetic over periods, a few thousand steps at most, as No Loss is.
  - **One cycle, per family:** the counted periods after which every day of the old pattern, its image under the reading, every loss and every gain come round again, so that whatever one cycle shows is all there is. Daily and weekly: one period (n days, n weeks), since shifting listed weekdays and the week start is the same one-to-one map in every period. Monthly: 4,800 / gcd(n, 4,800) of its periods, lcm(n, 4,800) months, a whole number of 400-year cycles of the calendar, after which month lengths, leap days and weekdays all repeat; yearly: 400 / gcd(n, 400) of its periods. A member by day of the month depends on the period's length alone and would repeat sooner, but an nth weekday or a set position depends on the period's first weekday too, and nothing is gained by telling them apart: one cycle for the family, and its length in days is the span every rate below is taken over.
  - **The reach** is the furthest any image of the cycle's occurrences moves, which is the furthest any image moves at all. Old occurrences are read through the cycle plus twice the reach and gains looked for through the cycle plus the reach, so every new day looked at has its preimages read. A rule outside the drag table has one reading, every day its own image, and is read through the days named alone.
  - Built 2026-11-06 ([#205](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/205)): the plan reports losses and gains by class, never as a list of every day, so a whole cycle costs what a walk of its periods costs. The first draft listed every day and took the model's tests from 5 seconds to over 4 minutes and out of memory.
- **Losses and gains by class.** A loss is an old occurrence with no day in the new series, and it is lost for one of three reasons; the reason, with the member it concerns, is its class. `noday`: its member's new form gives nothing in its period (the 31st in a month without one, the fifth Tuesday, 29 February in a year without one), named by that form. `start`: its image falls before a start moved later. `end`: its image falls past the new end. A gain is a day of the new rule that is no old occurrence's image; its class is the new member that gives it. Each class reports its first five days as examples, its count (every one of them for a bounded series, and for a `start` class always, since the days before a moved start are finite whatever the end; null otherwise, as the bound's events are) and, for a series with no end, its rate: how many fall in one cycle over the cycle's length in calendar periods, reduced ("5 in every 12 months", "3 in every 4 years"). Everything repeats with the cycle, so the count over one is the same wherever it is taken and the rate holds for all time; a class with none in the cycle (every loss before a moved start) has no rate. A gain at an edge is not among them: it is the edge choice (Gains at the edges). The panel says each class as one sentence under Affects (The Plan).
- **A bound sent with a drag** (Ends changed in the same save) is applied to the new series as sent: a sent COUNT counts scheduled occurrences from the new start, and skips the change generates (gains left out at an edge) are not counted against it, as under "keep N events"; skips the person made are counted, as RFC 5545 counts them. A sent UNTIL ends the new series there.
- **Gains at the edges.** A first or last period can gain a day: one only partly in the old series (Mondays and Wednesdays from Wed 6 Jan, every Monday moved to Friday, gain Fri 8 Jan; probed), or one a member left by moving whole periods (Swim relay in the examples). A member moved k whole periods can leave gains in each of the first or last k periods, not only one: a Monday-to-Friday series with its Mondays moved two weeks later leaves the first two Mondays of the new rule with no session behind them, and both are left out by default. What makes a gain one at an edge is structural, not a count of periods: it is the image, under the reading, of a day the old pattern gives outside the series (in a period the old rule counts, before its start or after its end). The images of the days just outside the series are the edge, however far the delta reaches, so a member moved more than a period, and the old end's continuation days that land inside the new span, are found by the same rule. A gain that is no such image (a day the member's new form gives where the old gave none, the 1st gained from every 31st) is an ordinary gain, named and kept. The plan names it and offers "Don't add Fri 8 Jan" or "Add Fri 8 Jan". I decided this is a choice, and "Don't add" is preselected whenever leaving the day out keeps every old occurrence's image with nothing else lost or split off: the gain comes from fitting the moved days into a rule, not from the request, and "move Mondays to Fridays" means the same sessions on other days. Otherwise "Add" is.
  - **Without it is a generated skip:** an EXDATE on the gained day, written with the plan. The start can't do it: the gain comes after the earliest image, which stays the start and carries the phase. A gain past the new end needs nothing.
  - **COUNT counts skipped days** (RFC 5545 3.8.5.3): Wednesday and Friday, COUNT=6 from Wed 6 Jan, shows five without Fri 8 Jan (probed). Under **keep N events**, COUNT is raised by one for each skip this change generates before the Nth occurrence, so the series keeps its N scheduled occurrences. A day skipped before the change is still one of the N and isn't replaced by a later one: five Mondays and Wednesdays from Wed 6 Jan with 13 Jan already skipped become `COUNT=6` without 8 and 13 Jan, the same four sessions, never a fifth on 27 Jan, and the bound line says "5 scheduled occurrences, 1 skipped" (probed; Pottery in the [examples](series-model-examples.md#7-a-day-already-skipped)). Under **keep the end**, the recount through the latest image includes every skip, and the bound line says how many show.
  - **One plan per combination:** the edge and bound choices are worked out together, each option's row showing its result with the other choices as selected; combinations with equal plans merge (without the gain, keeping the count and the end often agree, and the bound group goes away).
  - **Afterwards it is a skipped day like any other:** in the skipped line, mapped by its member under later edits, removed by Undo with the rest of the plan.
- **Nothing left.** The day acted on always has an image (it lands where it was dropped), so a drag alone never empties a series. A new rule or end that would leave no events ("every 30 February", an end before the start) is refused: "This would leave the series with no events. Delete it instead?" The refusal belongs to the reading whose plan leaves nothing: a new rule read as the old days shifted, or a drag sent with a new end read as the whole pattern shifted, can start after that end where the other reading doesn't, and then that row is disabled with this reason and the other is preselected; the edit is refused only when every reading leaves nothing.
- **Days outside the new series.** An edited day whose image falls outside the new bound, whose occurrence has no image at all (its month has no 30th under the skipping reading), or whose day a new rule doesn't produce gets a choice: **keep it as an event of its own** (preselected: no edit is lost; it stays where it shows, with its fields, tags and people, on the series' calendar) or **delete it**. **A skip belongs to the occurrence it removed.** Mapped by its member like any exception, it moves with its occurrence while the new series still has that occurrence. When the new series no longer has it (its image falls before a start moved later or past a shortened end, or the new rule has no day for it), the skip goes with it: it is dropped and named ("2 removed days before the new start are no longer needed"), never carried onto a real day the person never removed. So a course whose first two sessions were removed and whose start is then moved to the third session's date has no skips left and all its sessions from the new start. A day that becomes an event of its own is named in the plan, the Activity entry and Undo.

### No Loss, Proved per Family

A four-year check misses sparse intervals (every 61 months on the 15th, dragged to the skipping 30th, loses 15 Feb 2032; probed), so the check is a proof for each family:

- **Daily and weekly:** shifting every listed weekday and the week start by Δ is a one-to-one map of days, so nothing is lost, gained or merged, whatever the interval. Moving one member is one-to-one unless it lands on another listed day, which is refused; only a first or last period can gain (Bounds).
- **Monthly and yearly** (by day, nth weekday, set position, lists): an occurrence is lost when its member's new form produces nothing in its target period, and two members merge when they produce one day; both depend only on the period's length and the weekday it starts on. The Gregorian calendar repeats exactly every 400 years (146,097 days, a whole 20,871 weeks): month lengths, leap days and weekdays all come round again. A series every n months revisits the same place in that cycle after 4,800 / gcd(n, 4,800) periods, a yearly one every n years after 400 / gcd(n, 400). So the model walks the series' periods from its start to its bound, or for an unbounded series, that many periods (never more than 4,800); finding no loss or merge there proves there is none anywhere. It is arithmetic on month lengths and weekdays, a few thousand steps at most, not an expansion.
- **What it reports:** losses and gains by class, each with its first days and, for an unbounded series, how often ("drops 15 Feb 2032, then 1 in every 12 months"; Bounds, Losses and gains by class). Run past a bound, the same walk tells apart two readings with today's dates in common.

The walk also proves images distinct (each member keeps its own day in its period, no two members share one, and periods shift by a constant) and every exception's image inside the new series or given an outcome. These are proofs about Gregorian dates; the time-of-day promises rest on the capabilities (Zones, DST and All-Day Days).

### Imported Rules, by Capability

Rules from other apps can say more than the families above. **A rule shaped like a row of the drag table is that family, whoever wrote it, with all its edits;** the parts below restrict only what no row covers. An nth-weekday list is a row with any weekdays and counts 1 to 4 or -1 to -4 (`1MO,3TU,-1FR`), unless two members can name one day in some month (`4MO,-1MO` in a month with four Mondays, which the walk finds) or it adds BYSETPOS or BYMONTHDAY. Every rule a drag, a reading or the editor writes is in a row, since an edit that would make an overlap is refused, so ordinary editing never turns a series into an import: `1MO,3MO` dragged to `1MO,3TU`, saved and reloaded, drags again to `1WE,3TU` (probed).

Each part of a rule says which edits it allows, and a rule allows an edit only when every part does, so a restriction always wins: `BYWEEKNO` with one fixed `BYHOUR` takes a new time (the hour rewritten) and refuses a drag that moves days. A refused edit says why and offers "Only this one".

| Part of the rule | Days move (drag, following drag, zone keeping the moment) | Time of day | All-day toggle | Zone keeping the clock |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What the drag table names; DAILY with BYDAY and INTERVAL 1, whose days move as a weekly list's while the rule stays written as DAILY, its clock read as every spelling's is (a gap day shifted, every later day at its own clock) | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| BYHOUR and BYMINUTE, one value each | Yes | Yes, the new hour and minute written into the rule (a rule that says 09:00 can't move to 10:00 otherwise) | Yes: made all-day, the rule drops them, since an all-day series has no clock for them to name; made timed, they are written from the new time | Yes |
| Several times a day: BYHOUR or BYMINUTE lists; HOURLY, MINUTELY and SECONDLY | Refused | Refused: there is no one time to change | Refused: several occurrences a day would collapse onto one date key | Refused (Zones, DST and All-Day Days) |
| BYWEEKNO (with its WKST, always kept) | Refused: a week number means a different day after a shift, depending on the week start | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Other day sets: BYYEARDAY, BYMONTHDAY with BYDAY ("Friday the 13th"), WEEKLY with BYMONTH, DAILY with BYDAY and an interval, nth lists whose members can name one day, BYSETPOS lists outside the set-position row | Refused | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Not understood: anything the expander can't read (RSCALE, X- parts, a value outside RFC 5545's grammar, a part its table marks N/A for the frequency) | Refused | Refused | Refused | Refused |

Every part allows a new length, a new bound, a split and the fields, except the not-understood parts, which allow field changes only: in a rule the expander can't read, the model finds no occurrence before a split, no count left and no valid restart, so a split and a new bound are refused, saying so ("This repeat came from another app in a form Better-Cal can't read, so only its details can change here"). Delete all stays. A refused series edit leaves "This event" open wherever the day shows.

### Zones, DST and All-Day Days

A series' days are its days on its own wall clock: every local day is read in the series' zone.

**A zone change makes one of two promises,** and the dialog says which. **The same clock time in the new zone** (preselected, what the editor sends): every occurrence at 18:00 Berlin time, so every instant moves. **The same moment as the day acted on:** that occurrence keeps its instant (18:00 Monday in Los Angeles is 03:00 Tuesday in Berlin), the series repeats at 03:00 Berlin time on Tuesdays, and since the two places change their clocks on different weeks, the dialog lists the occurrences that don't keep their instants (probed: "18:00 in Los Angeles, except 19:00 on 15 and 22 Mar and 1 Nov"). Nothing about it is a drag: every row keeps its own instant, and the series' days are its old days read on the new zone's calendar, every member alike (Monday in Los Angeles at 18:00 is Tuesday in Berlin, for every Monday). Combined with a drag, the moment is the wall clock where the day was put, read on the clock it showed on: Monday 18:00 in Los Angeles dragged to Wednesday 18:00 and given Berlin is Wednesday 18:00 Los Angeles time, and that drag is measured in Los Angeles, the zone both its ends are read in (Δ = 2 days, the clock unchanged), with its readings as any drag's; then the zone change carries the result to Berlin, where Wednesday 18:00 in Los Angeles is Thursday 03:00. No day number read in one zone is ever subtracted from one read in the other. The carry is the whole-pattern shift by the days the instant moves on the calendar (here one), the drag table's preselected reading for that shift where a family offers several (a monthly day crossing into the next month); a family with fixed days refuses it with its reason, as it refuses a drag. Verified by `server/tests/series-model.php`, "Zone moment:" (2026-11-04).

**A clock time that doesn't exist** on some day (inside a spring-forward gap): the occurrence is at the same clock time plus the gap, 02:30 becoming 03:30. RFC 5545 3.3.10 says such instances are ignored and not counted; I'm keeping them, because a weekly event that vanishes one week a year is worse than one an hour later, and it's what 3.3.5 says for a single DATE-TIME (03:30 on 14 Mar 2027 in Los Angeles). The cost: a strict client reading the app's ICS or CalDAV may leave that day out, and end a COUNT series an occurrence later. The key is the instant the occurrence has. The policy is stated here and the expander implements it, for every spelling of the rule: a plain daily rule, one naming its hour in `BYHOUR` and one listing all seven weekdays express no different intention, since another calendar or serializer picks the spelling, so each keeps the gap day at the shifted clock and every later day at its own, the same where the clocks move by half an hour (02:15 becomes 02:45 that day only). The expander reads every wall clock by this one rule (`Series\Wall`), with no state carried from one day to the next, and the keys the library's readings wrote before 1.5.0 (a `BYHOUR` rule with no occurrence on the gap day, a weekday list that kept the shifted clock, [#201](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/201)) are moved onto it by migration 064.

**A start that would fall in the skipped hour** (a drag, time or zone change whose first day is that day, or a new series whose first day, aligned to its rule, is that day) is stored one period earlier, on the rule's latest day before it where the clock exists, as a generated skip, with COUNT raised by one under a count. Whether a day can hold it is the pattern's question, not the bound's: the bound is the same either way, and read from the start in the gap it would end by the shifted clock (an UNTIL at the last day's clock losing that day), which is what storing it earlier prevents. The storage holds only an instant and a zone, and the expander takes every day's clock from the start, so a start stored as 03:30 would move the whole series an hour; stored a period earlier, the first day shows at 03:30 and every later one at 02:30, and every sentence names the first day shown, never the stored one. Every count the person sees counts the events shown, never the stored day: the API's rule says the shown COUNT and a COUNT sent is the shown one (the editor's "Ends after" reads it), and the writer adds the stored day back. The same holds for the start: every start the API reads for a series is its first shown day, at the series' length (`Events::shownSpan`, beside the COUNT's `shownRrule`), and the stored anchor stays in the rows, ICS and CalDAV, where DTSTART with an EXDATE on it is the standard's own spelling of the same series. A start typed into that hour is that clock: the editor and quick add send the clocks typed (`typed` in the contract), since 02:30 typed there is the instant of 03:30, and the server takes the series' clock from the clock typed, so a new series typed to start there is stored by this same rule (1.5.1; before it the server received the shifted instant and stored a 03:30 series). The end typed with it moves with the start: its clock is moved by the push the gap gave the start, then read by the same rule (`Wall::typedEnd`), so 02:30 to 03:30 typed there is an hour long, 03:30 to 04:30 that day and 02:30 to 03:30 on every other day, and a start that exists has no push ([#222](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/222); before it the two clocks were read alone, so that hour was refused as ending at its start and 02:30 to 04:30 made an hour-long series).

**A clock time that happens twice** (in the fall-back hour): the first, the earlier instant in daylight time, as RFC 5545 3.3.5 says for a DATE-TIME (01:30 PDT on 7 Nov 2027), on the start's day and every later one alike. One rule holds for a start there too: the start is stored at that first instant, and every expansion of a stored series iterates from the instant it is stored at, never from its wall clock read back (PHP reads a repeated wall clock as the later instant, so the window's expander, which wrote DTSTART as a wall clock with its zone, started an hour after the model's). A start in the repeated hour is its first instant in the window, the model and the instance check alike. A series stored at the later instant (sent with that offset, or written elsewhere) is read the same way, from the instant it is stored at: the model's expansion and the instance check start where the window's does, so its first occurrence is the key the window shows, and the stored instant is kept while the start's wall clock doesn't move. The same rule holds for every instant the model is given, never only the start: **an instant given stays that instant**, and an equal day, clock and zone are not an equal instant where the hour repeats. A wall clock is read as its first instant only where no instant names it. So an edited day keeps the instant it shows at while its day, clock and zone don't move (a rename leaves a day at 02:30+01:00 there, never at 02:30+02:00), an inherited time on its occurrence's day is its key's instant, "Use series value" for the time puts a day back on its key, and ownership is read in instants: a day on its key's day at the other of the two instants (put there by a drag, by "this event", or arriving so from elsewhere) owns its time, and the day acted on, put on one instant where its image's key is the other, is an edited day of its own time at the instant it was put, for the length it was given.

**The expander is the app's own, written from RFC 5545 3.3.10's table.** One expander (`Series\RuleIterator`) reads every rule everywhere: the calendar's window, the model, the check that a request names an occurrence, mail admission's estimate and the CalDAV server's time ranges and `expand` (from 1.5.1, through the window's own expansion); sabre/vobject stays for reading and writing iCalendar text, and for a free-busy-query's busy times. A rule is a walk over the periods of its FREQ, stepped by INTERVAL from the start's own: a year, a month, a week from WKST, a day, a wall-clock hour, or a minute or second of elapsed time. Each period's set is a function of the rule, the start and the period's number alone, so the walk carries nothing from where it began, and a window from any day jumps straight to its period and reads what the walk from the start reads there. A period's days are those every day part admits: on a whole period an expand and a limit both keep the members that match, and where no part names a day the start's own is taken (a weekly rule's weekday, a monthly rule's day of the month, a yearly rule's month and day, a BYWEEKNO rule's weekday). A numbered weekday counts in the month for MONTHLY, and for YEARLY with BYMONTH, and in the year otherwise; BYWEEKNO's week 1 is the first week from WKST with four days of the year, a negative week counted from the year's last, and its period is that numbering's year; BYYEARDAY's 60th day is 29 February in a leap year and 1 March in the others. Clock parts follow the table too: for DAILY and coarser, BYHOUR, BYMINUTE and BYSECOND expand each day into wall clocks, the start's hour, minute or second where a part is absent, so a weekly rule with BYHOUR=3;BYMINUTE=15 is at 03:15 whatever the start's minute, and a rule naming 03:00 in a zone whose clocks move by half an hour stays at 03:00; HOURLY limits by BYHOUR and expands its minutes and seconds, stepping its hours on the wall clock; MINUTELY limits by BYHOUR and BYMINUTE and expands its seconds, and SECONDLY limits by every part, both stepped in elapsed time from the start's own minute or second. Every wall clock becomes an instant by the one rule above (a gap day at the clock plus the gap, a repeated clock at its first instant), and two clocks on one instant are one occurrence. BYSETPOS picks from each period's whole set in time order, for every frequency, so a yearly rule's set is the year's, not each month's. The start is always the first occurrence and the first counted; a generated instant before it is none. COUNT bounds the generated set, before skips; UNTIL bounds every generated instance, inclusive, a date UNTIL on a timed series read as the end of that day in its zone; a day a month or year doesn't have (30 February) is never generated, so never counted. What the table marks N/A for the frequency (BYWEEKNO outside YEARLY, BYYEARDAY in DAILY, WEEKLY or MONTHLY, BYMONTHDAY in WEEKLY, a numbered BYDAY outside MONTHLY and YEARLY or beside BYWEEKNO), a value outside the grammar, or COUNT with UNTIL, is a rule the expander can't read: refused where a rule is written, field changes only where one is stored. A rule ends only by its COUNT, its UNTIL, the end of year 9999, or a walk that proves it has no more: no day its day parts admit in the Gregorian calendar's 400-year cycle, or a whole cycle of periods (those 400 years together with the interval's phase, so an interval of 401 years still finds its year) without an occurrence. Daily on 29 February, three times, is 29 Feb 2028, 2032 and 2036. Any other limit of the walk (its periods looked at for one occurrence, which only a clock limit no step lands on reaches, or the model's occurrences kept) stops with an error, never as the rule's end, and the plan is then the one for a rule the expander can't read. A rule is written in its series' own form (RFC 5545 3.3.10) by every writer: UNTIL a date for an all-day series and a UTC date-time for a timed one, and no clock parts (BYHOUR, BYMINUTE, BYSECOND) on an all-day series, so a series made all-day loses them; clock parts stored on an all-day series are read as absent.

**Several times a day can collide.** With one time a day, a gap occurrence moved forward can't land on another. With several it can: `FREQ=DAILY;BYHOUR=2,3;BYMINUTE=30` in Los Angeles yields one occurrence on 14 Mar 2027, 03:30 PDT, where other days have two (probed). Only imported rules repeat several times a day, so rather than new identity machinery they refuse every series-wide move of their days, time or zone, saying why. So no edit merges two occurrences; the collision a stored rule has on its own expands as today, and "this event" edits it by its key. A field-only edit of any series (a rename, a new length, a new bound, with no day, time, zone or all-day change) keeps every stored key, shown time and skip exactly as stored, whatever the rule: every delta is zero, so each key's image is itself (its place among its day's keys, for a rule several times a day), and a rule several times a day keeps its count and each of its exceptions.

The plan notes every day of the series among the dates it shows, or within a year ahead, whose clock falls in a gap or an overlap, each with its own note: the scan runs from coming up's own first day through the later of a year on and the last date coming up shows, read from that first day however many occurrences come before it. An overlap's note is about the series' own instant that day, which an instant given keeps (the stored start on its day, the expander's key on later ones), never the first by default: it says which of the two it is and what tzdata says of it, "02:30 happens twice on 31 Oct: this is the second, in standard time", leaving out the clause after the comma where neither instant is in daylight time (a plain change of offset). The time zone cross-check (tools/tz-harness) expects these instants in its skipped and repeated hours instead of accepting every difference there.

All-day series are stored in UTC (0.9.16), so their local day is the UTC date; a timed series made all-day takes the dates of its days in its old zone.

### What the Harness Proves

Its invariants, revised: **0** (the start is the earliest image, a raw occurrence and a day of its rule, the yearly nth weekday included); **1** (rule follow, bound, time and zone as sent), its oracle reading the drag table's readings, each case picking one reading per group, the preselected one in half the cases; **2** (exceptions attached, none past the end, or given their outcome); **3** (member preserved: each exception's image is what its member's new form produces in its period, shifted, whatever the members' sorted order); **4** (own fields unchanged, inherited ones equal to the series' new values, an own date kept unless the case chose to move it); **5** to **7** (following split, Undo identity, delete following) with the outcomes above; **8** (Google: until 1.5.3, only edits that keep the rule text are offered there, with no outcome claimed for edited days; from 1.5.3, the series read back from a Google stand-in, and again after a second sync, gives the plan's occurrences and exceptions).

It gains: a preview writes nothing; the commit's rows equal the plan; a commit after any other change to the series is 409 with nothing written; no loss, merge or day outside except as a chosen reading names it; a refusal changes nothing; UNTIL is an occurrence, the latest image, and a bound or split past the 1,001st occurrence is exact; a gained occurrence carries no exception; an edit sent with the stored rule gives the same plan as without it; an edit from an edited day lands where dropped (#189); no edited row is keyed off its rule; a rule refuses an edit exactly when one of its parts does; readings merge only when their plans are equal; a plan-less call applies only when `direct`; an unchanged sync echo changes no ownership; the day acted on shows where it was dropped under every combination offered; a generated skip lies on a gained day, and under keep N the series has N scheduled occurrences, earlier skips among them; every rule written reads back as a drag table row with all its edits.

## The Paths on the Model

The model is a pure function (a `SeriesModel` with no database: it reads the master, its edited rows and the edit, and returns a plan of row writes or a refusal). A writer applies a confirmed plan in one transaction; the Google path sends one; Undo snapshots one. Nothing else in Events.php works out where an occurrence goes.

- **patchAll:** writes the plan: the master's columns, every edited row's key, times and inherited fields (keys are unique per calendar and uid, and a new key may be one another row holds until the commit: so every row that leaves the series, deleted or made an event of its own, or that changes its key lets go of it first, and only then are the new keys set), the skipped days, tags and people, and the rows that become events of their own or are deleted.
- **patchFollowing:** a split and a whole-series plan of the part from the day acted on (`Series\Split`, 1.5.1). The old master gets its UNTIL at its last occurrence before the split, in its own form, and only the skips before it; the part is the old pattern started on the day acted on (stored a period earlier when that day's clock is in a skipped hour, as every start is), with the rest of COUNT counted in raw occurrences without the cap, the old UNTIL, the skips and edited rows keyed from the split on, and it is planned as a whole series under the edit, so its rule is written by the one writer every edit uses (BYHOUR in step with its start, no clock part on an all-day series). A rule sent with it is compared with the series' rule as shown, never the part's: unchanged it is no rule, a changed UNTIL is the part's end (one before the split day leaves nothing and is refused), and a changed COUNT is the series' whole count, with that many from the split day as the other reading ([decisions](decisions.md), A split's sent rule). The commit inserts its master (a new uid, the old one's tags and people, the target calendar if the edit moves calendars), re-parents the rows, and writes the part's plan on it. From the first day it is patchAll.
- **deleteFollowing:** the split's old part; the edited rows from the split on are deleted. From the first day it is deleteAll.
- **deleteEvent this:** an EXDATE and the row removed, as now.
- **setAttendance and setRelationship following or all:** a split with no fields (following), then the answer on the master and every row, in one Undo group. The choice for days with their own answer is not offered yet (the answer reaches them all, as for all events).
- **An edit from an edited day:** the controller routes scope all or following on an override row to its master, with its key as `instanceStart` and the body as sent. Nothing is translated on the way: the model finds the day acted on by its key and measures from where it shows, as it does for a request sent to the master naming that key.
- **Undo:** a series edit snapshots every row the plan writes, before and after, with the tags, people and links of rows created, deleted or re-linked. apply() deletes the rows the edit created, parks the key of every row whose calendar, uid or key changed, then restores the rest whole (invariant 6). One entry per action; a refusal or a preview records none.
- **Every other writer keeps the own list, from 1.5.0,** even where its planning arrives later: import, feed polls, Google sync, invitation updates by mail, a CalDAV client's PUT, and Undo::apply. Each carries `override_fields` over on rows it rewrites or re-parents and sets it on rows it creates (by what the edit changed, or by Rows from Elsewhere), never leaving it null to be guessed later.
- **The editor:** sends only what changed (#199), though the server no longer depends on it, and asks for the plan whenever the scope isn't "This event only".
- **Quick add and create:** a new series starts on its rule's first occurrence on or after the start asked for, and its creation plan says so. A created rule is taken as written: "every fifth Friday" stays a fifth Friday.

Gone from Events.php with 1.5.1: alignedStart, dayDelta, seriesKeyShift, occurrencesIn, movedUntil, followingRrule, movedOverride, exdatesFrom, patchFollowing, deleteFollowing, googlePatchFollowing and googleDeleteFollowing; from Recurrence.php: shiftByday, shiftBymonthday, setUntil, splitUntil and daysToFirstByday. parkInstanceKeys stays, shared by the writer and Undo. RepeatParser::firstOn and matches stay for quick add.

### Google, as a Contract

Sending Google the plan isn't the same as Google ending up with it. Google decides its instance ids (series id plus `originalStartTime`), what becomes of its exception instances when the master's start or rule changes, whether a series-wide field change reaches them, whether invitees are notified, and how a skipped day is stored (as a canceled instance). I hardly use Google sync, so the order is the simple, honest one.

**Until 1.5.3, a Google calendar gets only changes that keep the rule text.** On a Google series the dialog offers "this event" and the edits that send only the master with its rule as written: a time of day, a length or a zone keeping the clock for all events; a drag that doesn't rewrite the rule (a daily or bare weekly series, or a whole number of weeks, for a list only as the whole-pattern shift); a field change for all events, to the master alone as today; Ends changed; delete this and delete all. These are a temporary allowance, not outcomes the model promises: what Google does with edited days when the start, clock time, zone or end changes is unverified until the 1.5.3 probe, so their plans list no outcome for edited days (the day acted on included: a count that would end before it splits nothing off there) and say instead: "Google decides what happens to edited days, and Better-Cal hasn't checked what it does yet. This can't be undone." Everything new is unavailable there, its reason in place of the reading: a drag that rewrites the rule, a time change that writes a new hour into a BYHOUR or BYMINUTE rule (the text is what Google keeps, whatever moved; it is compared as its parts, each list a set, so a list written back in another order keeps it), any other reading, a split ("this and following", delete following), a series-wide field carried to edited days (the words are in the examples' Google). That is less than today's code tries there (google-parity in the harness); a missing option is better than a plan the app can't keep.

**1.5.3 brings Google onto the model.** First the probe, on a test calendar: change a master's start, clock time, zone, end and rule with edited instances on it and read back what Google kept, testing each kind of exception on its own (a moved date, an own time, a title-only edit, a cancellation, and one beyond a shortened end); rename the master and see whether its exception instances take the title; write the clamped rule and `BYMONTHDAY=-1` and read them back. Then:

- **What the app sends:** the plan's master (rule, start, end, the mapped skips), and wherever the probe says Google strands them, each edited day the plan moved (a patch to its new instance id, the old one deleted) and each inherited field Google didn't carry.
- **Acceptance is the read-back and the sync after it.** The app reads the master and the instances holding the plan's exceptions back and compares them with the plan, each exception by `originalStartTime` with its own fields; then a second sync must change nothing, no ownership moved and no old exception back. Google may store the rule in another form, so the comparison is of the occurrences it gives as well as its text. Local rows are written from what Google returned, never from the plan. A difference goes into the Activity entry and a Review notice ("Google kept 'Rehearsal' on Tue 13 Apr; the plan had Wed 14 Apr").
- **Recovery.** A transaction can't span several requests, so the app first records a pending row with the plan and its steps, and, before any step that can remove an exception at Google, that exception's complete state (fields, own list, tags, people, links). Every step is safe to retry: an insert carries an event id the app chose, so a retry after a lost response finds the first attempt, and a step is checked against a read-back before it's retried. On a failure the app stops, writes what Google has, and Review lists the event as "Half done at Google" with **Finish** (a new plan from Google's state toward the same target) and **Put back** (the steps done, reversed). Put back first compares each event's etag with the one recorded after its step; an event changed since, at Google or here, is left as it is and named rather than overwritten.
- **Undo for Google series ([#172](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/172))** is the same machinery: the before side of a plan sent back as a target, after the same etag check.

### Floating Times

A floating series ([#167](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/167)) is one row of the table, not a new kind: a series whose wall clock has no zone. What the model needs, decided: a way to know a series is floating; its keys stored as its wall-clock times written as if UTC (the storage #167 leans toward), so arithmetic on them is plain UTC arithmetic with no DST; and a zone change between floating and pinned keeps every occurrence's wall clock (Δ = 0), the "same clock" promise with no second reading. The rest (storage flag, window, reminders, Google) is for #167.

## Open Decisions

The first draft's eleven decisions are settled above, and calls without a plan are the `direct` rule. The three below were settled on 2026-11-02 as recommended, and gains at the edges became a choice (Bounds). After the fourth review I moved two preselections (a gain at an edge left out, the end kept when a count would split off the dragged day) and kept earlier skips out of a count's compensation (Bounds). What remains open is only what the Google probe in 1.5.3 turns up.

1. **Which reading is preselected.** This decides what Enter does, and since people tend to accept a default, it matters more than that sounds. Settled as recommended, the second reviewer agreeing with the first three; the prediction cases can still move any of them:
   - **A month-end drag:** "on the 30th, or the last day in shorter months", and for a yearly 29 February, the last day of February. It loses nothing and fits "near the end of each month"; "skipping months without one", what other calendars do, is second. A day counted from the end keeps counting from the end first; a rule the person chose keeps its own form first.
   - **A list:** "Move every Wednesday to Thursday". Dragging Wednesday's class to Thursday in a Monday, Wednesday and Friday series most likely means Thursday instead of Wednesday, not Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday.
   - **A day moved on its own:** "Keep Fri 26 Mar". One class moved for an appointment most likely stays moved when the timetable changes.
   - **From an edited day:** adopting the day when it moves every event by less than the rule's period (a week, a month or a year), since then it reads as "this is the day now" (the Sunday moved to Monday and dragged on to Tuesday); when it would move every event a whole period or more (the Book club), the same way first. Adopting the time whenever it's offered. The threshold is a judgment and stays one: the dialog never states it, it shows each reading's resulting schedule, and that is what the person picks from.
2. **The whole plan on the calendar behind the dialog,** drawn as ghosts while the dialog is up. Settled: not in 1.5.0; add them if the dialog's dates turn out to be misread.
3. **What 1.5.3 does with the probe's answers.** Settled, pending the probe itself: carry exceptions and inherited fields ourselves wherever Google strands them (one call per edited day), and offer the clamped reading on Google only if Google stores and expands it as written, otherwise disable that row there, saying why.
