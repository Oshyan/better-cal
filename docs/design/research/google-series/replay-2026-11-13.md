### 1. An edited day is hidden at Google where its two forms meet at midnight UTC (behaviour, new; 7 scenarios; a fault a person meets)

`google-identity-all-day-to-timed-at-midnight-utc` (request 15), `-timed-to-all-day-at-midnight-utc` (15), `-timed-to-all-day-from-the-edited-day-in-auckland` (15), `-wide-auckland-adjacent-weekdays-tag-own-time-edit-then-renames` (18), `-wide-daily-all-day-to-16-los-angeles-a-day-moved-onto-a-skipped-date-keeps-the-skip` (20), `-wide-tokyo-weekly-own-time-tag-edit-then-poll-and-undos` (16), `seed7-case14` (15).

The request: the read the issue 256 fix sends before an edited day's step, `GET /events/<series>_<day>`, on the day's id in the series' new form. The stand-in answers 200 (the day's instance); Google answers 404 `notFound`.

What it is: in all seven, the day is an edited day (its own notes and place, or its own time or title), the change is a switch between timed and all-day, and the series' clock falls at 00:00 UTC (00:00 UTC itself, 13:00 Auckland, 09:00 Tokyo, 16:00 Los Angeles in winter). There a day's timed id and all-day id name the same instant (`<series>_20270208T000000Z` and `<series>_20270208`). The switch drops the day's record under the old form's id, as always; Google then hides the day in the new form altogether. The probe of the same morning shows it without the app: in 3.21 (timed at 00:00 UTC to all-day, a renamed and a cancelled day), `events.instances` lists 8 days of 10 after the switch, the renamed and the cancelled day missing; 3.25 (the mirror) loses the same two; 3.19 (10:00 Los Angeles, keys apart) keeps all ten. The day the app asks for is the right day: the id's date is the day's date in the new form, not off by one. It is not a day Google holds a live record for, and not an ordinary day either: it is gone from the series. Round 3 saw a `PATCH` to such an id answered 412, not 404. Probe 3.48 explains it: 412 is Google's answer to any conditional write on an id with no resource; the same write with no If-Match is 404.

What the app does now: `dayCondition` reads a 404 as "Google has no such day" (as for a day past the 730-occurrence cap), returns no etag, and `run()` passes over the step. The edited day's own values are not sent; the read-back then holds no record at that key, and the Activity entry and Review notice name the difference ("Google kept ... as an ordinary day"). In each of the seven, at Google the edited day stays hidden (gone from Google Calendar), and here the next store shows it as an ordinary day with its own notes, place or time lost. Not silent (a notice names it), but the day is lost at Google and its values are lost here.

Before the fix: the released code sent the same step without a read, on the etag chained from the step before. Round 3 shows Google's answer: 412. The change stopped half done; Finish sends the step again on the master's etag, which the hidden id does not carry either, so Finish most likely stops the same way every time (not run live). Wrong in the old way, and stuck.

Who meets it: a repeating event whose time is midnight UTC (09:00 in Tokyo, 13:00 in Auckland, 16:00 or 17:00 in Los Angeles, 19:00 or 20:00 in New York, midnight in London in winter: ordinary times in some places), with a day given its own notes, place, title or time, then switched between timed and all-day for all events. The switch itself is uncommon; the times are not.

What the probe settled (3.48 to 3.51): after the switch the day's old record is dropped, both of its ids answer 404, `events.instances` leaves it out, and a person in Google Calendar doesn't see it. No write tried brings it back: a PATCH with the master's etag, with the dropped record's or with none, a POST naming the series and the day, a DELETE of the dropped record (410) and a PATCH making it live (400). The deleted day is hidden the same way. It is the instant: 09:00 Tokyo meets it as midnight UTC does (3.50). With no edited or deleted day every day survives (3.51). So a day's own values or later changes can't be written to Google after such a switch, including "make the day's changes again afterwards".

The rule (proposed, not made): on a Google calendar, a switch between timed and all-day is refused before anything is sent when an edited or deleted day of the series sits at an instant that is also a day of the series in the new form. The app knows this from its own rows: the keys of the master's edited rows and skips, against the new form's occurrence keys. A deleted day is covered too: it is hidden at Google, which looks right, but no record names it any more, so the app would read it back as an ordinary day here. The third probe run settled the way out (3.63 to 3.65): moving the event an hour first, then switching (or, the mirror, making it timed at another time and then changing the time), keeps every day; making the days ordinary first doesn't, since a record at the series' values is hidden too, so `run()` also checks its first read. The sentence is in [the probe page](probe-2026-11-13.md#third-run-2026-11-13t205640-0100).

### 2. A kept day loses its own notes on a master change that does not send them (behaviour, new; 3 scenarios; a fault a person meets)

`seed7-case4` (request 13, a link set for all events), `google-identity-daily-dragged-a-day-lands-on-the-next-instant` (13, a daily series' start moved a day later along its pattern), `google-identity-zone-change-at-the-same-moment-pending` (13, Los Angeles to Denver at the same moments).

The request that caused it: in each, the master `PATCH` before the read (its body: a link and the rule; a start and end and the rule; a start and end in the new zone and the rule), none of which names the description. Each keeps the series' records (3.5, 3.17, 3.32). What Google did: the edited day's record is kept, with its own place (and its own title, where it had one) unchanged, and its own notes gone (`description` absent, where it was "Own notes for the day."). The stand-in keeps them. The series was created by the app with an empty description, as the app creates every series without notes; whether that is why (Google writing the series' empty notes into each record) is not known.

What the app does next: the read-back shows the day's description gone. `rebase` reads that as a change made at Google since, in a field this change does not touch, so it keeps Google's value (none), sends no step for the day, and names nothing. The store then writes the day here without its notes. A person loses a day's own notes, silently, when they set a link for all events, move a series' start along its own pattern (drag the first day a week later), or change its zone at the same moments, on a series with no notes of its own. Setting a link for all events on an event with a day's own notes is ordinary.

What the probe settled (3.52 to 3.61): on a series with no notes, every master PATCH that does not send `description` clears each kept record's own notes (a link, the start along its pattern, the zone at the same moments, the title, the length, a longer COUNT: six of six). On a series with notes the three kinds tried keep them. Sending the series' empty notes with a link kept them (3.61, one case). A day's place is never touched; its title is overwritten only by a title sent (3.55), which the app already predicts and sends again.

The rule (proposed, not made): after a master write that doesn't send the notes, to a series with none, the prediction (`afterMaster`) has every kept record's notes cleared, so the read-back shows it as the change's own effect and the edited day's step sends its notes again, as it already does for a title the write sends.

### 3. Every day of a series left skipped deletes the series (behaviour, new; 1 scenario)

`seed7-case2` (request 12): a rule change (weekly on Mondays in some months, until 8 November) that leaves the series one day, 8 November, which was already deleted. Google answers the master `PATCH` with `status: cancelled`: the whole series is deleted. The stand-in keeps it live. The app then reads the series as deleted at Google and stores that: the whole event is gone, here too. The planner refuses a change that leaves no events (`empty`), but counts a deleted day as one. Rare: a change that leaves only days already deleted. Probe 3.62 confirms it without the app: the master `PATCH` is answered `cancelled`, and a later `PATCH` to `confirmed` is taken but leaves it cancelled, so the series can't be brought back. The rule (proposed, not made): a plan that leaves no day undeleted is refused as leaving no events, as one that leaves no day at all is.

### A. A cancelled day is listed without `showDeleted` (behaviour, new; 26 scenarios)

A deleted day has no such row, so the update touches nothing, and the app carries on correctly. The stand-in should list such days, in that short form; the same answer shape matters for any later reader of that list.

### E. A write to a day with no record went on the wrong etag (replay artifact; fixed)

`google-identity-wide-auckland-adjacent-weekdays-tag-own-time-edit-then-renames` (request 17) and `google-identity-wide-tokyo-weekly-own-time-tag-edit-then-poll-and-undos` (15). The full request in the Tokyo one: `PATCH /events/<series>_20270208?sendUpdates=none`, If-Match the master's etag as the stand-in's answer to the switch gave it, body `{"summary": "Sample series", "description": "", "location": "Sample Hall", "status": "confirmed", "start": {"date": "2027-02-08"}, "end": {"date": "2027-02-09"}, "source": null}` (a day's step after the series was made all-day). The stand-in answered 200, the day as an all-day record; Google answered 412 "Precondition check failed.". The day's all-day id holds no record in the stand-in's read-back just before, so the app took the series' etag. The stand-in gives every resource of a series one etag; the replay mapped that value to the etag of the last resource in Google's read-back that carried it, a dropped record's, which Google keeps per resource. So the replay sent Google another etag than the app would have. Fixed: an etag is mapped by the resource it was sent for, then by its series, and only then by value. With the fix this write goes on the series' etag on Google too; whether Google then takes it is round 3's to show (the probe of 2026-11-13 found such a write taken in 3.36 and refused in 3.37).

What the app would do with each answer: on 200, the step is done and the switch goes on to the next day. On 412, `GoogleWriter` raises `google_conflict`, the series change stops half done and is listed in Review with Finish and Put back.

## Round 1: the Answer to an Insert

Evidence: `workfolder/google-replay/replay-2026-11-13T093625+0100.json` (every scenario's first divergence, with the request and both answers), checked against the probe of the same morning, `workfolder/google-probe/probe-2026-11-13T091712+0100.json` (3,244 event resources in Google's answers, calendar in America/Los_Angeles).

What the replay reported, after its normalizing: `description` and `recurrence` in 46 scenarios (a timed series or single with a location), `description` alone in 1 (a series with a location and a rule), `description` and `location` in 1 (the split's series, no location), and `description`, `location`, `recurrence` and `reminders` in 1 (an all-day single). The app's insert body sends `description: ""`, `location: ""` when it has none and `recurrence: []` on a single event; the stand-in stored the body and echoed it.

What Google does, as rules:

- **A field sent empty is not in the answer.** `description: ""`, `location: ""` and `recurrence: []` sent on an insert come back absent (all 49 first divergences). Of 3,244 resources in the probe's answers none holds an empty string in summary, description or location, and none holds a null anywhere, though the probe sent `source: null` seven times.
- **Every resource carries `reminders`.** With none sent: `{"useDefault": true}` on a timed event, `{"useDefault": false}` on an all-day one (the replay's all-day single; in the probe, 422 all-day resources, every one false, and 2,652 timed ones true). The replay hid the timed case, since it ignored `{"useDefault": true}`.
- **Every resource carries `eventType: "default"`** (3,244 of 3,244), cancelled ones included. The replay hid it.
- **A `dateTime` is written with the calendar's offset, not the event's zone.** A series in UTC at 18:00Z comes back `2027-05-03T11:00:00-07:00` with `timeZone: "UTC"` kept (the replay's `app-series-deleted-whole`); in the probe, every `start`, `end` and `originalStartTime` `dateTime` carries -08:00 or -07:00 whatever its `timeZone` (UTC 94, America/Denver 42, America/Los_Angeles 2,686). The replay hid it, since it compared instants.
- Also in Google's answer and not compared, by design: `updated` with milliseconds (`.761Z`; the stand-in writes `.000Z`), `created`, `etag`, `htmlLink`, `creator` (the account) and `organizer` (the calendar). These differ between any two runs.

### Errors

From the probes of 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-13: a 400 "Invalid start time." has reason `invalid`; a 404 "Not Found" `notFound`; a 410 "Resource has been deleted" `deleted`; each in `{"error": {"errors": [{"domain": "global", "reason", "message"}], "code", "message"}}`. **A PATCH's 412 is worded differently from a DELETE's:** "Precondition check failed.", reason `failedPrecondition`, with `"status": "FAILED_PRECONDITION"` (four in the 2026-11-13 probe); a DELETE's 412 is "Precondition Failed" (probe of 2026-11-07, 5.5 and 5.7). Not yet seen live, and left as the stand-in had them: the reason of a DELETE's 412, of a 409 on an id taken (the documentation says `duplicate`) and of a 400 for a refused rule.

### Where each difference went

The stand-in should answer as Google does; the replay's normalizing is only for what differs between any two runs (ids, etags, times of writing). So:

- **The stand-in** (`FakeGoogle::shapedAnswer` and `shaped`, applied to every answer on its way out, the store unchanged): a null field, an empty description or location and an empty recurrence are left out; `reminders` is added when none is held, true on a timed event and false on an all-day one; `eventType: "default"` and `kind` always; every `dateTime` in start, end and originalStartTime is written with the calendar's offset at that instant; errors carry the reasons above, and a PATCH's 412 Google's wording.
- **The replay** no longer ignores `reminders {"useDefault": true}`, `eventType "default"` or a `dateTime`'s offset, and compares `kind`. It still ignores `etag`, `updated`, `created`, `sequence`, `htmlLink`, `creator`, `organizer` and the order of an RRULE's parts.

### What the product does with it

The database suite against the reshaped stand-in: 21 assertions fail in four files that all pass before it (`sync-form-recovery`, `google-series-recovery-held`, `google-series-identity-recovery`, `google-series-identity`). Every one is a Put back after a change between timed and all-day stopped after its master write, refused with 409 `google_changed_since` ("changed at Google since"). With the `reminders` shaping alone taken out they pass again; the offsets and the empty fields change nothing in the product's tests. The reading: Put back's "nothing changed since" compares every field of the series as Google gives it, `reminders` among them, against what the stopped step left as the app expects it; on a switch the stand-in's answer now carries another `reminders` (true on the timed form, false on the all-day one), which the app did not send and does not expect, so it reads the series as changed at Google. For a switch from timed to all-day this is what Google is shown to do (no all-day resource in the probe has `useDefault: true`), so on Google, Put back after such a step should be expected to refuse in the same way: a product finding, reported, not adjusted. For a switch from all-day to timed Google keeps `false` (below), so those failures come from the stand-in not yet modelling that, and on Google this direction would not trip on `reminders`.

### Behaviour, not shape: handed back

These are what Google keeps or changes, not how it writes an answer, so they are left to the worker on leftovers and rule changes:

- **`reminders.useDefault` stays false after an event is switched to all-day and back.** In the probe, 170 timed resources carry `{"useDefault": false}`, all in cases where the event or the day was all-day at some point (3.23 to 3.26, 3.29, 3.30, 3.36 to 3.40); none in any other case. The stand-in's shaping gives a timed event true whenever it holds no reminders, so after a switch back it answers true where Google answers false. Expected to show in the next round in a harness scenario that switches back.
- **A day's old all-day id under a timed series answers as a cancelled stub** (probe 3.39, steps 21, 27 and 33, `GET <id>_20270308`): `summary: "CANCELLED"`, `created: "0000-12-31T00:00:00.000Z"`, the calendar as creator, no description, location or private properties. Part of issue 256's leftovers.
