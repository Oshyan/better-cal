# The Series Model

Where every occurrence of a repeating series goes when the series is edited, and what the person sees before it happens. Written before the 1.5 rewrite starts: first draft 2026-11-02, revised the same day after an outside design review and my direction on it. This is the table the roadmap's "Decisions to make first" asks for. Every example is invented. The long worked examples, the full grid, the harness cases and the prediction cases are in [series-model-examples.md](series-model-examples.md).

## What This Is

One function decides where each occurrence goes, and every path that changes a series asks it: a whole-series edit, "this and following", a delete (this, following, all), an attendance answer, a Google calendar, Undo, and the saves of the editor and quick add. It takes the series as stored and the edit, and returns a **plan**: the series as it will be (rule, start, bound, zone, all-day flag), the rule in words, the next few occurrences, the bound in words ("6 events, ends 1 Nov", or "changes 6 events to 10, ends 1 Nov"), what happens to every edited and skipped day, any refusal with its reason, and, where the edit can reasonably mean more than one thing, the readings as choices, each with its own dates. The paths only write what a confirmed plan says.

Why one model: since 1.4.0 the series code has been fixed one reviewer's finding at a time (month lines, the last day, an UNTIL left behind, lists, nth weekdays, "this and following" dropping edited days, two edited days colliding on a drag). Each fix added a branch (rebaseSeriesEdit's rule shift, alignedStart's snap, seriesKeyShift's ordinal mapping and its `$sameDays` escape, movedUntil, the split day's own patch), and each round's next bugs came from the branch the round before had added. patchAll and patchFollowing work out the same things separately, the Google paths work out less, and Undo restores whatever they wrote. The harness that shipped with 1.4.6 named twelve causes still open after that (a thirteenth, the key collision, is fixed since). Run again at 976f49a (3,000 cases, seed 1, 192 seconds) it has 2,011 failing checks in 378 signatures, every one under the eleven keys it lists for those twelve causes: stale-skips 639, keep-snap 378, google-parity 342, editor-rule 249, rule-change-orphans 139, biweekly-list 133, plain-rule 59, count-span-or-month-multiple 36, yearly-nth-start 22, until-type 10, until-first-phantom 4.

The first draft aimed to do the right thing automatically. The review and my own direction change that aim. Mathematical correctness isn't the same thing as what a person expects. People think of the series they have in mind ("something on the 30th"), not of February, and their expectations come from the calendars they use most, which mostly don't rewrite rules at all. So the model still understands every consequence of a change, but it doesn't assume intent where it can lay out the reasonable readings instead. Every change to a series goes through one confirmation step that says what the series becomes, and offers the readings as choices when there is more than one, with the same Confirm and Cancel either way. Series changes are infrequent, so one extra step is a fair price, and it makes Undo useful: the person knew what the change would do before it did it, so if it isn't what they wanted, they know how.

Where one reading is clear, I'm keeping the higher bar over Thunderbird's and Nextcloud's fixed shift (move every exception by the same number of days and leave the rule alone): the rule follows the drag ("every Monday" dragged to Tuesday is "every Tuesday"), an edited day stays with the occurrence it belongs to, and the end moves too. Where another calendar's behavior is a reasonable reading ("on the 30th, skipping months without one"), it's one of the choices, in the words a person would use. Nothing is lost or added without the plan naming it.

## Vocabulary

- **Series:** a master row (`rrule` and no `recurrence_parent_id`) with its rule, start, zone (`tzid`), all-day flag, length, skipped days and edited days.
- **Rule:** the pattern (FREQ, INTERVAL, the BY parts, WKST) plus the bound. `Recurrence::pattern` is the rule without its bound.
- **Rule family:** the shape of a pattern that decides how it follows a drag. The rows of the table.
- **Capability:** which edits a rule allows. Every family in the table allows all of them; rules from other apps may allow fewer (Imported Rules, by Capability).
- **Occurrence:** a day the rule produces, named by its **key**: the UTC instant the expander (`Recurrence::sabreExpand`, sabre/vobject) yields for it; for an all-day series, its date as a UTC midnight. The key is the occurrence's RECURRENCE-ID (RFC 5545 3.8.4.4) and Google's `originalStartTime`.
- **Raw occurrences:** what the rule produces with no skips applied. COUNT counts them, skipped ones included (RFC 5545 3.8.5.3).
- **Period:** what the rule repeats in: a day, a week (starting on WKST, Monday by default), a month or a year, all read in the series' zone.
- **Ordinal:** an occurrence's place. For a daily or weekly rule, its distance in days from the day acted on. For a monthly or yearly rule, its period and its position among the rule's raw occurrences in that period (first, second, ...), counted over the whole period.
- **Exception:** a skipped day (an EXDATE in `exdates_json`) or an edited day (an override row keyed by `recurrence_instance_utc`). An exception belongs to the occurrence its key names, wherever it shows.
- **Own field, inherited field:** an edited day's field is its own when the person set it for that day; the row lists its own fields. Every other field is inherited and follows the series. Date and clock time are fields like the title (Edited Days and Their Own Fields).
- **Bound:** COUNT, UNTIL (inclusive; a date for an all-day series) or none.
- **Follow:** the rule is rewritten to name the day the dragged occurrence landed on, so every occurrence moves with it ("every Monday" dragged to Tuesday is "every Tuesday").
- **Realign:** what `alignedStart` does today when a rule can't follow: the rule is kept and the start snaps to the next day the rule names. The model never realigns an existing series; only a new series is aligned to its first occurrence.
- **The day acted on:** the occurrence the edit was made from (`instanceStart`), the dragged one for a drag. For an edited day, the occurrence its key names.
- **Image:** the occurrence an old occurrence becomes. A **loss** is an old occurrence with no image; a **gain** is a new occurrence that is no old one's image (every 31st moved to the 1st gains the months without a 31st).
- **Δ:** whole days from the day acted on to the day it was dropped on, each read in its own zone (old zone before, new zone after). **k:** the months (or years) between their months (or years). **δ:** for a list, the change of the dragged occurrence's day within its month.
- **Reading:** one way to understand an edit where more than one is reasonable. Readings come in groups (how the rule reads, which bound to keep, what edited days do); the plan offers each group as a choice with one reading preselected. Readings that give the same dates are merged into one.
- **Plan, preview, commit:** the model's answer for an edit; asking for it without writing; writing it once confirmed.

"Readings" are the choices the dialog offers; which one is preselected is open decision 1 where the row doesn't say. ## Preview and Confirm

This is the one place the app asks before an undoable change. Everywhere else the toast's Undo is the better answer (ConfirmDialog.js says so), but a series change moves days the person can't see, and Undo alone leaves them knowing that something went wrong but not what. So every series change gets one step that shows its plan.

### What Goes Through It

- A drag or resize of an occurrence with "This and following" or "All events".
- An editor save with "This and following" or "All events", whatever it changes (dates, rule, end, title, reminders, calendar).
- A move to another calendar (dropped on the sidebar) with following or all.
- Delete following, delete all.
- Attendance and relationship with following or all.

What needs no dialog: any "this event" change (a drag, resize, editor save, delete or answer for one day, and "Use series value"), every change to an event that doesn't repeat, a copy, and Undo. The drop chip keeps its four buttons; "This event" acts at once as now, and the two series choices read "This + following…" and "All events…", since they open the dialog.

### The Plan

What the dialog shows, top to bottom, every line only when it has something to say:

1. **What and which:** the action and the series' title ("Move all events · Choir"), with a small switch between "This and following" and "All events", and a link "Only this one" that applies the change to the one day instead. Changing the switch asks for a new plan.
2. **The rule:** before and after in words on one line, "Weekly on Tuesday at 19:00, now weekly on Wednesday". When nothing about the days changes, "Repeats weekly on Tuesday, as before".
3. **Readings,** when the edit has more than one: a group of radio rows, each with its words, its next three dates and its consequence on the same row ("On the 30th, skipping months without one · 30 Jan, 30 Mar, 30 Apr · skips every February"). The preselected one is first.
4. **Next:** the next four events as they will show, the moved day among them, edited days marked ("Fri 26 Mar, its own date").
5. **The bound:** "No end, as before", "6 events, ends 30 Jun", "Changes 6 events to 10, ends 1 Nov", with the bound choice beside it when the count and the end disagree.
6. **Edited and skipped days,** one line each, by what happens to them: moves with its event ("'Rehearsal' Tue 13 Apr moves to Wed 14 Apr, keeps its title and place"), stays ("Tue 23 Mar, moved to Fri 26 Mar, stays there"), becomes an event of its own, is deleted. Skipped days in one line ("2 skipped days move with theirs: 30 Mar to 31 Mar, 20 Apr to 21 Apr"). Days that fall outside the new series come with their choice (keep as events of their own, or delete). After three lines, "and 4 more" opens the rest.
7. **Ownership changes,** in a sentence of their own: "Two edited days become events of their own: 'Rehearsal' on 13 Apr and 'Rehearsal' on 11 May."
8. **Other choices,** when they apply: days moved on their own (stay on their dates, or move with the series), own values for the fields changed ("2 edited days keep their own title: 'Rehearsal' 13 Apr, 'Dress rehearsal' 11 May. [ ] Give them the new title too"), the zone reading.
9. **Notes:** a clock time that doesn't exist or happens twice on some day ("No 02:30 on 14 Mar in Los Angeles: that one is at 03:30"), "At Google; can't be undone" until 1.5.2.
10. **Cancel and the action,** the button saying what it does: "Move all events", "Save for all events", "Delete 8 events".

A refused change shows its reason in place of the rule ("This repeat came from another app and counts weeks of the year, so its days can't follow a drag. Move only this one, or open the series to choose its days."), with "Only this one" and Cancel. A refused reading is a disabled radio row with its reason; the rest of the plan stands.

### The Dialog, Desktop and Phone

Desktop, after a drag: a card anchored beside the drop point, where the drop chip was, with no dimming backdrop, so the week or month stays readable behind it. After an editor save: the same card over the drawer's footer, the form still visible above it. About 520 pixels wide, dense rows, dates in short form, the reading rows on one line each. Enter confirms, Esc or Cancel cancels, and a press outside only dismisses (web/src/ui/outside.js). For a Google series, which can't be undone yet, the focus starts on Cancel, as ConfirmDialog's does.

Next     Wed 10 Mar · Wed 17 Mar · Fri 26 Mar (its own date) · Wed 7 Apr

         Tue 23 Mar, moved to Fri 26 Mar: stays there        (•) Stay  ( ) Move with the series

Phone: a bottom sheet over the bottom bar, as tall as its content up to two thirds of the screen, scrolling inside with the drawn scroll rail. The title line holds the title and the switch; the rule takes one or two lines; reading rows are full width with their dates on a second line; edited days show two lines before "and 3 more". Cancel and the action sit at the bottom, in thumb reach. The calendar above it keeps the dropped block in view: the sheet opens with the drop point above its top edge where the screen allows, and otherwise lists "This day: Wed 10 Mar at 19:00" first.

Drag and editor differ in their first lines: a drag's plan leads with where the dragged day lands; an editor save lists each changed field on one line ("Title: Choir, now Chorus" · "Reminder: 1 hour before, now 30 minutes") before the rule and the days.

### While the Dialog Is Up

The drop waits for the dialog. The dragged block stays where it was dropped, drawn as pending (dashed outline, no fill), and nothing else on the calendar changes, since the other occurrences haven't moved yet and drawing them as moved would show a guess as the result. The card opens at once with what the client knows (the title, the scope, where the dragged day landed) and "Working out the plan" in place of the rest, with the action button disabled until the plan arrives; there is no timer, the plan's answer fills it. Cancel puts the block back. Confirm sends the commit, draws the block solid (the optimistic step moveEvent does today), and refreshes the window from the server's answer. A 409 `series_plan_changed` reopens the card with the new plan and a line saying the series changed since it was shown; any other error puts the block back with the error toast, as now.

The editor works the same way: Save asks for the plan with the form left open; Cancel returns to the form unchanged; Confirm saves and closes the drawer.

### Creating a Series

A new series has no edited days to move, so it needs no dialog, but it gets the plan's information where it's made. Quick add's draft card shows a line under its chips: the rule in words and the first three dates ("Monthly on the 30th, or the last day · Sat 30 Jan, Sun 28 Feb, Tue 30 Mar · 6 events"), and when the start moved to the rule's first day, "Starts Tue 2 Mar, the first Tuesday on or after the date you typed". The editor's Repeat row shows the same line while a rule is set, for a new event and an existing one. Each change of the repeat controls asks for a preview; a newer request replaces an older one, with no debounce timer.

### After: Toast and Activity

The toast stays short: the title and what was done ("Choir · changed how it repeats"), with Undo. The Activity entry carries the plan's summary, ownership changes included: "Changed how Choir repeats (all events): weekly on Tuesday, now on Monday and Thursday. Two edited days became events of their own: 'Rehearsal' on 13 Apr and 'Rehearsal' on 11 May." Undoing it says what it put back the same way.

### The API

- `PATCH /events/:id` and `DELETE /events/:id` take `preview: true` (in the body, or `?preview=1` on DELETE) with the same fields, `scope` and `instanceStart` as the change itself. The answer is 200 `{plan}` and nothing is written, journaled or sent to Google. `POST /events/:id/attendance` and `/relationship` take it too, and `POST /events` with an `rrule` (a creation plan: words, aligned start, first occurrences, bound).
- The commit is the same request without `preview` and with `plan: {hash, choices: {group: optionId}}`. The server works out the plan again inside the write's transaction, with the series' rows locked, from the rows as they are and the choices sent. When its hash differs from the one sent, nothing is written and the answer is 409 `series_plan_changed` with the new `{plan}`, as proposals answer `proposal_changed`. A refused plan or reading is 422 `series_refused` with `{plan}`, its `refusals` saying why.
- The hash covers the series' rows as read (master, edited rows, skips, with their `updated_at`), the edit as sent, and the chosen options, so a preview made before any other change to the series (another tab, a feed poll, a Google sync) is refused at commit.
- A series change sent without `plan` (an agent, an older client): open decision 4.
- `POST /quickadd` drafts carry `series` (the creation plan) when they have an `rrule`.
- The plan holds `hash`, `scope`, `rule` (before, after and their words), `start`, `bound`, `next`, `groups` (each with its options and the one selected), `days` (every exception: key, new key, outcome, words), `losses`, `gains`, `refusals`, `notes` and `summary`. The one for the mockup above, as JSON, is in the [examples](series-model-examples.md#a-plan-as-json).

Each option in a group carries its own words, next three dates and bound, so the dialog can show every reading's dates side by side; the rest of the plan is for the selected options, and picking another asks for a new preview. The words come from the server (one sentence generator in PHP, kept in step with web/src/lib/repeat.js by a shared table of rules and sentences tested on both sides), so the dialog, the Activity entry and an agent read the same sentence.

## Edited Days and Their Own Fields

### Own Fields

An edited day records which of its fields are its own. A series-wide change reaches every field an edited day doesn't own; its own fields stay. Equal values say nothing about intent: a day renamed "Rehearsal" in a series later renamed "Rehearsal" still owns its title, and the next rename of the series leaves it alone.

The fields: `date` (the day it shows on), `time` (its clock time and zone), `length`, `allDay`, `title`, `location` (with its coordinates and provenance), `description`, `url`, `reminders`, `status` (planned or maybe), `attendance`, `tags`, `people`, `icon`. Not per day: the calendar (an edited day moved to another calendar becomes an event of its own, [#186](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/186)), the uid and the rule.

What owning a field means for the dates: an own `date` is that date, kept when the series' days move (with a choice in the plan to move it with the series, keeping its distance from its occurrence); an inherited `date` is its occurrence's day, wherever that goes. An own `time` is that clock time in that zone; an inherited one is the series' clock time. So a day moved to Thursday with its clock time untouched follows a later change of the series to 20:00, which the first draft's in-place or moved distinction couldn't say.

Storage: a new column, `override_fields JSON NULL`, on override rows: the list of its own field names, `null` on masters and on events that don't repeat. Rows keep holding every field's value, own or inherited, so everything that reads events (the window, CalDAV, ICS export, outfeeds, search) reads rows exactly as today. Only writers consult the list: a series-wide write sets each edited row's inherited fields in the same transaction, the plan lists those writes, and Undo snapshots them. Per-field null meaning "inherit" fails where null already means something (no place, default reminders).

What writes the list: a "this event" edit adds the fields it changed, even when the value typed equals the series' (the person chose it); "Use series value" removes a field and copies the series' value back; an edit with scope all from that day clears the fields it sets (the person meant the series' value). An edited day left with no fields of its own is just an occurrence again, and its row is deleted (with its key in Undo). Rows that come from elsewhere (a feed poll, a Google sync, a CalDAV client's PUT) carry full values and no intent, so their list is worked out by equality with their master each time they're written, the same rule as the backfill; a person's own local changes to such a row (attendance, tags, reminders) are added to it and kept across polls, as those fields are today.

The migration (`063_override_fields.php`) adds the column and fills it for every existing override row by comparing it with its master: `date` when the row's local day differs from its key's, `time` when its clock time or zone differs from the series', `length`, `allDay`, `title`, `location` (text), `description`, `url`, `icon`, `status` and `attendance` when they differ, `reminders` when the normalized lists differ, `tags` and `people` when the sets differ. It changes no value, so nothing shows differently after it; it runs in batches and only on rows whose list is still null.

What the backfill can't know: a value set on purpose to equal the series' (it reads as inherited, and will follow the next series-wide change), and a value that differs only because a series-wide edit made before 1.5 never reached the row. Today none does: rename "Choir" to "Chorus" and last week's moved Tuesday is still "Choir". The backfill reads that title as the day's own, which keeps what it shows; the first series rename after the migration then leaves it alone, and its plan says so ("1 edited day keeps its own title: 'Choir' on 9 Mar. [ ] Give it the new title too"), so the guess is visible and fixed in one tick. The Activity journal can't settle it either way without changing what a row shows, so it isn't consulted.

In the editor: opening an edited day shows a line under the title, "Edited day of Choir (weekly on Tuesday). Its own: date, title. Use series values", and each own field's control has a small reset icon beside it, titled "Use series value (Choir)", the way reminders have "Reset to default" now. Using it is a "this event" change, one Undo, no dialog. The event view's series line says the same in short ("Repeats weekly · moved from Tue 9 Mar"), so a person sees which day is special without opening the editor.

### All-Day Is One of Them

The first draft's decision 8 (an all-day toggle gives every edited day the series' flag) is reversed. `allDay` is a field like the others: a day that inherits it follows the toggle, and a day deliberately timed in an all-day series (allDay and time its own) stays timed at its own time when the series is toggled either way. "The editor can't show it" was a limit of today's editor, not what people want; the editor now shows it ("Its own: all-day off, 09:00"), and the plan names it ("1 edited day keeps its own time: 'Sample Hall visit' on 13 Apr at 09:00").

### Changing the Series from an Edited Day

A drag from an edited day says two things: how the series changes, and where this day ends up. The first draft measured from where the day showed but applied the family rules from its key, which disagree once the day has moved. The model translates first, then applies the family rules, then places the day.

Let K be the day's key (its occurrence), S where it shows, and S' where it was dropped.

1. **Measure the displayed change.** Δ is the whole days from S to S' in the series' zone; the clock change is from S's clock time to S''s; the length change likewise.
2. **Translate.** The change is applied to the occurrence: the family rules run with K as the day acted on and K + Δ (at the new clock time) as where it landed. Everything about the series (its rule, start, bound, the other exceptions) follows from that, as for any drag.
3. **Place the day.** The day being dragged takes the explicit change: it shows at S', its `date` and `time` are its own if S' isn't where its occurrence's image falls, and it takes the edit's fields. That is the difference between this day and the others: the others keep their own fields (preservation), this one gets what the person did to it (the explicit change).

That reading, **the series moves the same way**, always exists. A second, **this becomes the series' day and time**, is offered when K and S' are in the same period (the same week for a daily or weekly rule, month for a monthly one, year for a yearly one): the family rules run from K to S' itself, so the series lands where this day now is, and the day's own date and time are cleared (it's in place again, its other own fields kept). Across a period it isn't offered, since it would shift every occurrence by whole periods. Which one is preselected is open decision 1.

Monthly, the review's example: "Book club", monthly on the 1st at 19:00 from 1 Jan, its 1 Jan moved to 31 Jan. Dragged from 31 Jan to 1 Feb with "All events". Δ is one day, so the occurrence moves from 1 Jan to 2 Jan: monthly on the 2nd from 2 Jan. January's event shows on 1 Feb, its own date; February's is on 2 Feb. The second reading isn't offered (1 Jan and 1 Feb are in different months). The dialog says it plainly: "Monthly on the 1st, now on the 2nd · Next: Mon 1 Feb (January's, its own date) · Tue 2 Feb · Tue 2 Mar", with "Only this one" a click away for the person who meant only January's. The literal reading the review warned about (the key moved a month to 1 Feb, the day then shown 30 days later on 3 Mar) is not a reading at all: the change is measured in days and applied to the occurrence before any family rule runs.

Weekly: "Choir", weekly on Tuesday at 19:00 from 5 Jan, its Tue 9 Mar moved to Thu 11 Mar. Dragged from Thu 11 Mar to Fri 12 Mar with "All events". The same way: the occurrence moves a day, so weekly on Wednesday from 6 Jan, and this day shows on Fri 12 Mar, its own date, keyed on Wed 10 Mar. This becomes the series' day (Tue 9 Mar and Fri 12 Mar are in the same week): weekly on Friday from 8 Jan, and this day is in place on Fri 12 Mar. Both readings are shown, each with its next dates.

Clock time: the same series, with Tue 16 Mar at its own 19:30 and Tue 6 Apr at its own 18:00. The 16 Mar event is dragged to 20:00 the same day, "All events". The same way: the series moves half an hour, to 19:30, and this day is at 20:00, still its own. This becomes the series' time: the series is at 20:00 and this day's own time is cleared. Either way the 6 Apr event stays at its own 18:00, and the dialog says so.

"This and following" from an edited day translates the same way and applies the result to the part from K on. The split is by key (open decision 11 of the first draft, now settled), so an edited day keyed after the split but shown before it goes with the following part, and the plan says so: "Includes 'Rehearsal', moved to Fri 6 Mar, which belongs to Tue 9 Mar." From an edited day moved to another calendar, "This and following" stays unavailable ([#188](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/188)).

## Mapping, Defined

### The Function

Given the old series (rule, start, bound, zone, all-day flag, length, edited days with their own fields) and the edit (the day acted on, where it was seen, where it was dropped, its new clock time, zone, all-day flag and length, the fields, any rule sent, and the chosen readings), the model returns a plan. In order:

1. **A sent rule that is unchanged is no rule.** The sent rule is unchanged when its pattern equals the stored one and its bound names the same end. Patterns are compared after dropping INTERVAL=1, sorting BYDAY lists, and reading a monthly BYMONTHDAY equal to the start's day (a yearly BYMONTH and BYMONTHDAY equal to the start's) as bare. WKST counts wherever RFC 5545 3.3.10 makes it count, a WEEKLY rule with INTERVAL over 1 and BYDAY, or a YEARLY rule with BYWEEKNO, and is ignored in comparing anywhere else; it is never dropped from a rule that is written. Bounds are equal when the COUNTs are, or when both UNTILs have the same last raw occurrence at or before them (the editor writes 23:59:59 of the end date in the event's zone; the model writes the occurrence itself). So it no longer matters that the editor sends the rule on every save ([#199](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/199)). A sent rule with the same pattern and a different bound changes only the bound. A different pattern is a new rule.
2. **The capability is checked.** The rule's capability decides which parts of the edit it takes; anything else is a refusal with its reason.
3. **The change is measured.** Δ, k and δ from the day acted on to where it was dropped; from an edited day, translated first (above).
4. **The readings are found.** The drag table's row, the bound rules, the zone change, edited days with their own date or values for the changed fields, and days outside the new series each give a group. Readings that give the same dates over the series' whole span are merged into the one with the simplest rule; a group left with one reading isn't shown as a choice.
5. **For the chosen readings, everything is mapped.** The rule follows. Every exception's key goes to its image, looked up among the new rule's raw occurrences. Losses and gains are found over the series' whole span (No Loss, Proved per Family). Two old occurrences never share an image, and every mapped exception lies inside the new series or has an outcome of its own (an event of its own, or deleted, as chosen).
6. **The plan** is the result: the series' new columns, every row write, the words, the next occurrences, every exception's outcome, the losses and gains, the refusals, the groups.

### Correspondence, by Family

- **Daily and weekly:** an occurrence's image is the new rule's occurrence on its local day + Δ in the new zone. Shifting every listed weekday and the week start by Δ keeps each occurrence's place in its week and its week's place in the interval, so this is exact for every daily and weekly row, every other week included. Under "this day moves", only the dragged day's occurrences take + Δ; the others keep their days.
- **Monthly:** an occurrence is the nth raw occurrence of its month under the old pattern (the rule without its bound, counted from the month's first day, so a key near the end still finds its place). Its image is the nth raw occurrence of the month k on under the new pattern. A list keeps its order under δ (the pattern reading) or keeps every member but one (this day), so n names the same member; a single-day or single-nth rule has one a month, so n is 0.
- **Yearly:** the same, by year and k years. A month list keeps its order because a drag across the year is refused.
- **Other capabilities:** same days only.
- **When the nth doesn't exist** in the target period, there's no image: a loss. Only the skipping month-end readings lose occurrences among the rules the app writes, and they say so.

Every new key is looked up among the new rule's raw occurrences (sabreExpand), never computed by adding seconds or days to an instant, so a key is always an occurrence. A clock time that doesn't exist on some day, or happens twice, follows the policy in Zones, DST and All-Day Days.

### Naming the Landed Day

- **The fifth weekday is the last.** The 29th to 31st are the fifth of their weekday, which most months lack: a positive count landing there is written `-1`, and a count from the end landing on the fifth from the end is written `1`. This is what the editor's buildRrule writes. One reading: it's what "the second Tuesday" dragged to the last week means, and the dates show it.
- **The month-end readings.** A monthly day landing on the 29th, 30th or 31st offers: **on the 30th, skipping months without one** (`BYMONTHDAY=30`, what Google, Apple and Thunderbird mean by "monthly on day 30"); **on the 30th, or the last day in shorter months** (`BYMONTHDAY=28,29,30;BYSETPOS=-1`; for the 29th, `BYMONTHDAY=28,29;BYSETPOS=-1`); and, when the landed day is its month's last, **on the last day** (`BYMONTHDAY=-1`). For the 31st, the clamped reading and the last day are the same dates and merge into the last day. Each reading shows its next dates, so the February without a 30th is in front of the person, not in a rule. Over a span with no short month (a COUNT of three, every twelve months in March), the readings give the same dates and merge into the plain day. A yearly day landing on 29 February offers **only in leap years** (`BYMONTH=2;BYMONTHDAY=29`) and **the last day of February** (`BYMONTHDAY=-1`). All probed: sabre expands the clamped 30th as 28 Feb 2027 and the clamped 29th as 28 Feb 2027 and 29 Feb 2028.
- **No boundary.** The first draft refused the 29th and 30th and took the 31st as the last day, two neighboring dates on opposite sides of an invisible line. With the readings in front of the person, nothing is refused.
- **From the end.** A day counted from the end, dragged to the nth-last day of its month, offers **the nth-last day** and **that day by number** (with the month-end readings on the 29th to 31st). The from-the-end reading is offered for the last to the seventh-last day, the forms the editor and the series sentence can name; past that, "the 20th day from the end" isn't how anyone thinks of a date, and only the number is offered. This replaces the first draft's "last seven days" heuristic, which chose silently.
- **Written, not inferred.** A series the person wrote as "every 31st" (`BYMONTHDAY=31`, or bare from a 31st, which RFC 5545 reads the same way) stays that: the model never rewrites it into the last day, a drag by whole months keeps it, and the editor shows it as "on day 31, skipping months without one". A reading picked in the dialog is written in its own form and shown in its own words from then on, so every rule a drag can write is one the editor can show and change.
- **The editor's monthly choices** gain the forms: for a start on the 29th or 30th, "on day 30, or the last day in shorter months" and "on day 30, skipping months without one"; for a start on its month's last day, "on the last day" too; for a start in the last week, "on the second-last day" to "the seventh-last day" when the series already counts from the end; plus the weekday choices as now. web/src/lib/repeat.js learns to describe the clamped forms, `-2` to `-7`, and yearly rules with BYMONTH, which today read as "Repeats".
- **Bare rules** are read as their start's day (or month and day) and written back bare when the new rule names the new start's day.

### Bounds: COUNT, UNTIL and Days Outside

A drag can keep at most two of three things: the number of events, the end, and every old occurrence's image. The model names the conflict instead of picking.

- **A COUNT the person chose is kept by default.** When the drag changes how many periods have an occurrence (a day the 29th to 31st moved to a day every month has, or the reverse), the bound group offers **keep 6 events** (the COUNT stays; the series ends where the sixth falls) and **keep the end** (recounted from the new start through the image of the last occurrence). The bound line says what each does: "6 events, ends 1 Jul (was 31 Oct)" and "Changes 6 events to 10, ends 1 Nov".
- **An UNTIL is kept as the end by default:** it becomes the image of the last raw occurrence at or before it, written as that occurrence (its instant in UTC for a timed series, its date for an all-day one; never an instant the rule doesn't produce, since sabre yields a monthly UNTIL on a 1st at the series' time as a phantom occurrence, cause 13). When the count changes, the group offers "keep 4 events" too.
- **No bound:** gains and losses are named ("adds every February, which had no 30th"), with nothing to choose.
- **Days outside the new series.** An edited day whose image falls outside the new bound (a kept COUNT that now ends sooner, or Ends shortened in the editor) gets a choice: **keep it as an event of its own** (preselected: no edit is lost; it stays where it shows, with its fields, tags and people, on the series' calendar) or **delete it**. Skipped days outside are dropped and counted ("1 skipped day after the end is forgotten").
- **Edited days a new rule doesn't produce** get the same choice. A day that becomes an event of its own is named in the plan, the Activity entry and Undo.
- **Start:** the image of the old start, always an occurrence, so no series opens on a day off its own pattern.

### No Loss, Proved per Family

The first draft checked for losses over four years from the day acted on. That misses sparse intervals: a series every 61 months on the 15th from 15 Jan 2027 next falls on 15 Feb 2032, and dragged to the 30th, the skipping reading loses it, five years out (probed: it runs 30 Jan 2027, 30 Mar 2037). The check is now a proof for each family:

- **Daily and weekly:** shifting every listed weekday and the week start by Δ is a one-to-one map of days, so nothing is lost, gained or merged, whatever the interval. "This day moves" is one-to-one unless the day lands on another listed day, which is refused.
- **Monthly and yearly** (by day, nth weekday, set position, lists): an occurrence is lost when its target period lacks the nth raw occurrence of the new pattern, and that depends only on the period's length and the weekday it starts on. The Gregorian calendar repeats exactly every 400 years (146,097 days, a whole 20,871 weeks): month lengths, leap days and weekdays all come round again. A series every n months revisits the same place in that cycle after 4,800 / gcd(n, 4,800) periods, a yearly one every n years after 400 / gcd(n, 400). So the model walks the series' periods from its start to its bound, or for an unbounded series, that many periods (never more than 4,800); finding no loss there proves there is none anywhere. It is arithmetic on month lengths and weekdays, a few thousand steps at most, not an expansion.
- **What it reports:** for a bounded series, every loss and gain by date; for an unbounded one, the first few and how often ("drops 15 Feb 2032, then 1 in 12"). Past the walk nothing new can happen, since the cycle has come round.
- **The clamped and last-day readings lose nothing** by construction; the walk confirms it.

The same walk proves the other two promises: images are distinct (within a period the mapping keeps order, and periods shift by a constant), and every exception's image lies inside the new series or has an outcome.

### Imported Rules, by Capability

Rules from other apps can say more than the families above. The model doesn't refuse them wholesale or accept them blindly; each has a capability, and each capability allows the edits that keep its meaning. A refused edit says why and offers "Only this one".

| Capability | Rules | Days move (drag, following drag, a zone change keeping the moment) | Time of day | All-day toggle | Length, zone keeping the clock, bound, split, fields |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Follows | Every family in the drag table; DAILY with BYDAY and INTERVAL 1 is read as the weekly list | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| One fixed time | BYHOUR and BYMINUTE with one value each | Yes | Yes, writing the new hour and minute into the rule (a rule that says 09:00 can't move to 10:00 otherwise) | Yes: made all-day, the rule drops them; made timed, they are written from the new time | Yes |
| Several times a day | BYHOUR or BYMINUTE lists; HOURLY, MINUTELY and SECONDLY | Yes for DAILY and coarser, every time of the day moving with its day; HOURLY and finer refuse | Refused: there is no one time to change | Refused: several occurrences a day would collapse onto one date key | Yes |
| Week numbers | BYWEEKNO (with its WKST, always kept) | Refused: a week number means a different day after a shift, depending on the week start | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Other day sets | BYYEARDAY, BYMONTHDAY with BYDAY ("Friday the 13th"), WEEKLY with BYMONTH, DAILY with BYDAY and an interval, nth lists over several weekdays, BYSETPOS lists outside the set-position row | Refused | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Not understood | RSCALE, X- parts, anything sabre rejects | Refused | Refused | Refused | Fields, bound and split only |

A refused series edit leaves "This event" open: the one day can still move.

### Zones, DST and All-Day Days

A series' days are its days on its own wall clock: every local day is read in the series' zone.

**A zone change makes one of two promises,** and the dialog says which. **The same clock time in the new zone** (preselected, what the editor sends when a zone is picked): every occurrence at 18:00 Berlin time, so every instant moves. **The same moment as the day acted on:** that occurrence keeps its instant (18:00 in Los Angeles on a Monday is 03:00 on Tuesday in Berlin), the series repeats at 03:00 Berlin time on Tuesdays (a drag by that Δ), and since Los Angeles and Berlin change their clocks on different weeks, other occurrences don't keep their old instants. The dialog lists those, for a Monday 18:00 series from 1 Mar 2027 (probed): "In Los Angeles that's 18:00, except 19:00 on 15 and 22 Mar and 1 Nov, between the two places' clock changes."

**A clock time that doesn't exist** on some day (inside a spring-forward gap): the occurrence is at the same clock time plus the gap, 02:30 becoming 03:30. RFC 5545 3.3.10 says such instances are ignored and not counted; I'm choosing not to omit them, because a weekly event that vanishes one week a year is worse than one that's an hour later, and it's what 3.3.5 says for a single DATE-TIME in a gap, what sabre gives (probed: every Sunday 02:30 in Los Angeles is 03:30 on 14 Mar 2027), and what Google and Apple show. The cost: a strict client reading the app's ICS or CalDAV may leave that day out. The key is the instant the occurrence has, so it is still an occurrence.

**A clock time that happens twice** (in the fall-back hour): the first, the earlier instant in daylight time, as RFC 5545 3.3.5 says for a DATE-TIME and sabre gives (probed: Sunday 01:30 in Los Angeles on 7 Nov 2027 is 01:30 PDT, 08:30 UTC).

The plan notes either when it falls among the dates it shows, or within a year ahead. The time zone cross-check (tools/tz-harness) tests the policy directly: its cases in skipped and repeated hours expect these instants from Better-Cal's expansion, instead of filing every difference near a DST change as accepted. The oracles may still disagree there, with each other and with it; Better-Cal's own answer is fixed.

All-day series are stored in UTC (0.9.16), so their local day is the UTC date; a timed series made all-day takes the dates of its days in its old zone.

### What the Harness Proves

Its invariants, revised: **0** (start is the first raw occurrence and a day of its rule, the yearly nth weekday included); **1** (rule follow, bound, time and zone as sent), its oracle reading the drag table's readings, each case picking one reading per group, the preselected one in half the cases; **2** (exceptions attached, none past the end, or given their outcome); **3** (ordinal preserved); **4** (own fields unchanged, inherited ones equal to the series' new values, an own date kept unless the case chose to move it); **5** to **7** (following split, Undo identity, delete following) with the outcomes above; **8** (Google parity: Google is sent the plan that was stored, and from 1.5.2 the series read back from a Google stand-in equals it).

It gains: a preview writes nothing; the commit's rows equal the plan, field by field; a commit after any other change to the series is 409 with nothing written; no loss, no merge and nothing outside except as a chosen reading names it; a refusal changes nothing and answers its code; UNTIL is an occurrence; a gained occurrence carries no exception; an edit sent with the stored rule gives the same plan as without it; an edit from an edited day, sent as the client sends it, changes the series as the translation says and lands the day where dropped (#189); no edited row is keyed on a day its rule doesn't produce; every capability refuses exactly what its row says.

## The Paths on the Model

The model is a pure function (a `SeriesModel` with no database: it reads the master, its edited rows and the edit, and returns a plan of row writes or a refusal). A writer applies a confirmed plan in one transaction; the Google path sends one; Undo snapshots one. Nothing else in Events.php works out where an occurrence goes.

- **patchAll:** writes the plan: the master's columns, every edited row's key, times and inherited fields (keys parked first, then set, the two passes from 1.4.6), the skipped days, tags and people, and the rows that become events of their own or are deleted.
- **patchFollowing:** the plan is a split and a whole-series plan of the part from the day acted on. The old master gets its UNTIL at its last occurrence before the split and only the skips before it. A new master gets a new uid, the target calendar if the edit moves calendars, the rest of COUNT, the skips from the split on, and the edit. Edited rows keyed from the split on are re-parented to it (uid and calendar with them) and mapped. From the first day there is no split: it is patchAll.
- **deleteFollowing:** the split's old part as above; the edited rows from the split on are deleted. From the first day it is deleteAll.
- **deleteEvent this (and deleting an edited row):** no mapping: an EXDATE and the row removed, as now.
- **setAttendance and setRelationship following or all:** a split with no fields (following), then the answer on the master and on the rows that inherit it, and on those with their own answer only when the plan's choice says so, in one Undo group as now.
- **An edit from an edited day:** the controller routes scope all or following on an override row to its master, with the override's key as the day acted on and its own start as where it was seen. Scope this, or none, edits the row alone, as now.
- **Undo:** a series edit snapshots every row the plan writes, before and after (the masters, every edited row it re-keys, re-parents, rewrites, deletes or detaches, the events of their own it creates), with the tags, people and links of rows created, deleted or re-linked. apply() deletes the rows the edit created, parks the key of every row whose calendar, uid or key changed, then restores the rest whole: exact, as invariant 6 checks. One entry per action; a refusal or a preview records none.
- **The editor:** sends only what changed (#199), though the server no longer depends on it, and asks for the plan whenever the scope isn't "This event only".
- **Quick add and create:** a new series starts on its rule's first occurrence on or after the start asked for, for every family (at creation nothing is lost by moving it), and its creation plan says so. A created rule is taken as written: "every fifth Friday" stays a fifth Friday.

What goes from Events.php when this lands: rebaseSeriesEdit, alignedStart, daysChanged (no callers already), dayDelta, seriesKeyShift, occurrencesIn, movedUntil, movedOverride, followingRrule and exdatesFrom, plus the halves of patchAll, patchFollowing, deleteFollowing, googlePatchAll, googlePatchFollowing and googleDeleteFollowing that work out rules, starts, bounds and keys (each keeps only its write). From Recurrence.php: shiftByday, shiftBymonthday, setUntil, splitUntil, and daysToFirstByday (no callers outside one test). parkInstanceKeys stays, shared by the writer and Undo. RepeatParser::firstOn and matches stay for quick add.

### Google, as a Contract

Sending Google the plan isn't the same as Google ending up with it. What Google decides itself: its instance ids (series id plus `originalStartTime`), what becomes of its exception instances when the master's start or rule changes, whether it carries a series-wide field change to its exception instances, whether invitees are notified, and how it stores a skipped day (as a canceled instance). What the app sends: the plan's master (rule, start, end, the mapped skips), and, if the probe (open decision 3) says Google strands them, each edited day the plan moved, as a patch to its new instance id with the old one deleted, and each inherited field Google didn't carry.

**Acceptance is the read-back.** After the requests, the app reads the master and the instances holding the plan's exceptions back from Google and compares them with the plan: the rule and start, the canceled instances, and each exception by `originalStartTime` with its own fields. The local rows are written from what Google returned, never from the plan, since Google's copy is the true one. A difference from the plan goes into the Activity entry and a Review notice ("Google kept 'Rehearsal' on Tue 13 Apr; the plan had Wed 14 Apr"), with the same Finish and Put back as below.

**A change that takes several requests can fail halfway.** A transaction can't span them, so the app first records a pending row: the plan, its steps, which are done. The steps go in an order where every prefix loses nothing: a split inserts the new series before trimming the old one, so a failure in between shows days twice (visible, and fixable) rather than not at all; carried exceptions move after the master. On a failure the app stops, reads Google back, writes what Google has locally, and keeps the pending row. The event shows "Half done at Google" and Review lists it with what's done and what isn't ("'Choir' repeats on Wednesday at Google, but 'Rehearsal' on Tue 13 Apr is still on Tuesday"), with **Finish** (a new plan from Google's state as it is now toward the same target, shown in the dialog) and **Put back** (the steps done, reversed). A sync meanwhile writes Google's state as always; the row closes when Google matches the target or the person puts it back.

**Undo for Google series ([#172](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/172))** is the same machinery: the before side of a plan sent back as a target, after checking the event's etag hasn't moved (as undoPluginEdit does for plugin calendars). A deleted series comes back with a new Google id, and invitees may hear about it; #172 lists those.

Until 1.5.2, a Google-backed series goes through the dialog with the plan's rule, start, bound and dates, which the Google paths send in place of rebaseSeriesEdit's half, and the dialog says "Edited days are left to Google, and this can't be undone" instead of listing per-day outcomes it can't promise.

### Floating Times

A floating series ([#167](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/167)) is one row of the table, not a new kind: a series whose wall clock has no zone. What the model needs, decided: a way to know a series is floating; its keys stored as its wall-clock times written as if UTC (the storage #167 leans toward), so arithmetic on them is plain UTC arithmetic with no DST; and a zone change between floating and pinned keeps every occurrence's wall clock (Δ = 0, same days at the same clock time), the "same clock" promise with no second reading. Left open, for #167 rather than this model: whether that is a flag on the row or a new column, how the events window widens for floating rows, reminders on the device's last zone, and what a Google calendar gets (Google can't hold a floating time).

## Open Decisions

The direction and the review settle the first draft's decisions: 1 and 2 (the 29th to 31st) are the month-end readings; 3 (COUNT) is the bound rules, a chosen count kept by default; 4 (lists) is two readings; 5 (imported rules) is capabilities; 6 (edited days a new rule doesn't produce) is a choice, events of their own preselected and named; 7 (fields) is own fields; 8 (all-day) is reversed; 9 (from an edited day) is the translation; 11 (which days go with "following") is by key, named in the plan. Decision 10 (Google's exceptions) stays open, as 3 below. What's left:

1. **Which reading is preselected,** in three places. The dialog always shows every reading with its dates, so this decides only what Enter does. Recommended, each to be checked against the prediction exercise before 1.5.0 merges:
   - **A month-end drag:** "on the 30th, or the last day in shorter months", and for a yearly 29 February, the last day of February. It loses nothing and fits how people think of it ("near the end of each month"); "skipping months without one", what other calendars do, is second, with its skipped months on the row. A day counted from the end keeps counting from the end first.
   - **A list:** "this day moves". A person dragging Wednesday's class to Thursday in a Monday, Wednesday and Friday series most likely means Thursday instead of Wednesday, not Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday; the pattern reading is second. The reviewer supported moving members together, and that stays one click away, but as a default it changes days nobody touched.
   - **From an edited day:** "this becomes the series' day and time" when it's offered, since that's where the person put the event; otherwise the same-way reading is the only one.
2. **The whole plan on the calendar behind the dialog.** The grid could draw the plan's occurrences in the visible range as ghosts while the dialog is up. Recommended: not in 1.5.0. The dialog's dates first, the dragged block pending, nothing else redrawn; add the ghosts if the prediction exercise shows people misread the dates in the card.
3. **What Google does.** On a test calendar, before 1.5.2: change a master's start and rule with edited instances on it and read back what Google kept; rename the master and see whether its exception instances take the title; write the clamped rule and `BYMONTHDAY=-1` and read them back. Recommended: carry exceptions and inherited fields ourselves wherever Google strands them (one call per edited day), and offer the clamped reading on Google calendars only if Google stores and expands it as written; otherwise the dialog leaves that row out on a Google calendar and says why.
4. **A series change sent without a plan** (an agent through the API or MCP, an older client). Recommended: apply it with the preselected readings when the plan has nothing to choose and no consequence beyond moving days (no event of its own, nothing deleted, no count change, no loss or gain); otherwise 409 `series_plan_required` with the plan, so an agent sees the same choices a person does and commits one. The MCP tool for event edits learns to show the plan and commit it.
