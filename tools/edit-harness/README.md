# Series Edit Harness

Builds thousands of random repeating series with skipped and edited days, applies random edits to them as the client does (Events, the series plan API, Undo) against a migrated database, and checks what was written against the expander and against its own reading of the design ([series-model.md](../../docs/design/series-model.md), [api-contract.md](../../docs/api-contract.md) "Series Plans"). The design is the specification: nothing here takes the series model's word for what an edit should do. Run it after changing anything in the series model (`Domain/Series`, `SeriesPlans`), `Events`, `Recurrence` or `Undo`.

```bash
BC_TEST_DB=127.0.0.1:3306 ./tools/edit-harness/run.sh [--cases N] [--seed S] [--family F] [--case N] [--no-shrink] [--quiet]
```

It needs PHP with `server/vendor` installed and a MariaDB or MySQL server it may create databases on (`BC_TEST_DB` as host:port, `BC_TEST_DB_USER` and `BC_TEST_DB_PASS`, root with no password by default), exactly as the database suite does. Each run makes a database of its own and drops it. 3,000 cases take about five and a half minutes, and shrinking adds a minute or two; a seed names a run, so `--seed 1 --case 116` replays one case and `--family` limits the draw to one family. It writes `findings.json` here (ignored by git): every failing check with the case that produced it, and one shrunk reproduction per finding signature. `HARNESS_SLOW=seconds` names each case that took longer, and `HARNESS_PROFILE=1` adds the time spent in each phase to the summary. It runs from the command line only.

A seeded slice of 200 cases (seed 7) and the fixed cases run with the database suite on every push (`server/tests/db/edits.test.php`). It fails on any failure outside the known code paths below and lists each one with its signature and the case to replay, and it fails when the slice and the fixed cases together leave a required category of the plan's data checks unrun. The mutation runner follows it in CI (Mutations).

`--fixed` runs the fixed cases after the drawn ones (`--cases 0 --fixed` for them alone, `--fixed=name,...` for some; a full run of 1,000 cases or more takes them all), `--save-spec DIR` writes each case's complete spec as a fixed case is written (`--case N` to save one), `--findings FILE` names the findings file, `--model FILE` loads the series model from a copy instead of the tree (the mutation runner's), and `--find 'check|role'` names each case that ran a category of the plan-data checks, to pick a fixed case from.

## Files

`Base.php` (the seeded generator, Gregorian day numbers, local clocks), `Shape.php` (the harness's own reading of a rule and an evaluator of its days), `Truth.php` (the expander as ground truth), `Space.php` (the series and their exceptions), `Delta.php` (the edit vector), `Expect.php` (the oracle), `Invariants.php` (the checks), `PlanChecks.php`, `PlanPlacement.php`, `PlanNext.php` and `PlanUntouched.php` (the plan's data checks), `Driver.php` (one case through the domain code), `lib.php` (the known code paths, the runner, the report and the shrinker), `harness.php` (the command line), `cases/` (the fixed cases, complete specs; `cases/limitations/` and `cases/findings/` hold cases that pin a documented limitation or a finding and run apart), `mutations/` (the mutations, their runner and their fixed cases).

## The Space

The space comes from the design's tables, not from a list of named cases. Every rule family the drag table names is drawn: daily with any interval, DAILY with a BYDAY list, weekly bare, on one day, on a list of days and every other week on a list (with and without a week start), monthly bare (also every three months), on a day 1 to 28 (also every other month), on the 29th to 31st, the clamped month end (`BYMONTHDAY=28,...,n;BYSETPOS=-1`), a day counted from the end, a list of days, an nth weekday in both spellings (`2TU` and `TU;BYSETPOS=2`), a list of nth weekdays with mixed weekdays and directions, set positions over a set of days, yearly bare, on a month and day (29 February and the last day of February among them), on a month and nth weekday, and on a list of months with a day or an nth weekday. Every other row of the capability table is drawn too, stored as an import writes it: one fixed BYHOUR and BYMINUTE, several times a day (BYHOUR lists, HOURLY, MINUTELY), week numbers, the other day sets (BYYEARDAY, BYMONTHDAY with BYDAY, WEEKLY with BYMONTH, DAILY with BYDAY and an interval, an nth list whose members can name one day, BYSETPOS outside the set-position row) and rules the expander can't read (RSCALE, an X- part).

Every bound kind is drawn: none, a COUNT, and an UNTIL in the editor's form (23:59:59 of the last day in the series' zone), as the last start, or as a date for an all-day series. Every zone kind the generator has: northern DST on two different weeks (Los Angeles, Berlin), southern DST (Auckland), a half-hour zone (Kolkata), a zone whose clocks move by half an hour (Lord Howe: a 30-minute gap in October and a 30-minute repeated half hour in April), UTC, and all-day. Starts fall a few days before or after the zone's clock changes, on month ends and the turn of the year, on a leap day, or anywhere, at clock times that include one inside the spring gap and one inside the autumn overlap.

Edited and skipped days are placed before, at and after the day acted on, on the first and the last occurrence and on the first after a clock change. Each edited day owns a random set of fields (title, place, notes, link, reminders, status, tags, people, date, clock time, zone, length, all-day), each made as a "this event" edit; its own list must name what the edit changed. Some edited days are in a trip, chosen from the case's seed and number so the drawn space is untouched: a stop of a trip of its own, or, in a series made a trip first, a trip with a stop in it, each linked through Trips as the app links them. The day acted on is the first occurrence, a later one, an edited one, the first after a clock change, or the last.

## The Delta Vector

An edit is a vector of deltas, each drawn on its own: a date delta (zero in about half the cases, else from a few days to over a month either way), a clock delta (kept within the day), a zone change, a length delta, an all-day toggle (a day made timed gets a zone and a clock), the trip flag (the one series column), each field an edited day can own (title, place, notes, link, reminders, status, tags, people), the rule sent back unchanged (as stored, or as the editor writes it: INTERVAL=1 spelled out, a BYDAY list reordered, an UNTIL at 23:59:59) or changed to another pattern (another family, or the same weekdays shifted), and a bound change (a count, an end on one of its days, or none). Where the clock it is put at happens twice, the request names the second of its two instants in half the cases (again by the case's number), so a day put on either instant of a repeated hour is drawn. A field sent with the value the occurrence already shows (its edited row's own value, or the series') is no change. A bound sent equal to the stored one by the design's rule (the same count, or an UNTIL with the same last raw occurrence) is no bound change. A new pattern drawn that is the stored one by the design's comparison (lists as sets, a bare rule as its start's member, a weekly BYDAY of the start's weekday among them) is no new rule.

The scope is drawn too: all events, "this and following", delete this, delete following, an answer for all events, an answer from a day on. A whole-series edit goes through the plan API as the client sends it: to the master with the day acted on as `instanceStart`, or, when the day acted on is an edited day, to its own row in half the cases and to the master with its key in the rest. Either way `instanceStart` names the occurrence by its key K and every delta is measured from where that occurrence shows (S, its own date, clock and zone when it has them) to where it was put (S'), so a drag of an edited day is S' minus S applied to the series and every row, never S' minus K. The plan is previewed, one option is picked per group (the preselected one in half the cases, a random enabled one otherwise, previewing again after each pick), the preview must have written nothing, and the commit carries the hash and the choices; the same choices are replayed on redo. Then Undo, the edit again, and in some cases a second edit on top (a rename, a time change, a drag or a delete of one day) undone twice.

Shrinking works on the vector: it drops the second edit, then each edited or skipped day, then zeroes one delta at a time, then shortens the date delta, keeping each step that still fails the same invariant on the same path.

## The Oracle

Ground truth for which instants a stored series produces is the expander from the series' start through its bound (`Recurrence::sabreExpand`, read with no skipped days applied). A bounded series is read whole; an unbounded one over a window from its start (two years for daily and weekly rules, eight for monthly, twenty-five for yearly, twenty days for rules several times a day), and a day beyond the window is looked for where it is.

The harness has its own reading of every family in the drag table (`Shape`): its members (a weekday, a day of the month, a clamped day, an nth weekday, a set position, a month with one of those), their periods and the phase an interval counts. Its evaluator is checked against the expander on every series it reads (`H-evaluator`), so a disagreement shows as a harness defect rather than passing silently. It reads a series' days in its zone at its clock as the design states the expander's gap rule: a rule naming its hour (BYHOUR) has no occurrence on a day whose clock falls in a spring-forward gap, while every other rule keeps that day at the clock plus the gap. Images are of the pattern's days, gap days included, and the new series has those its own clock's gap doesn't leave out.

For a whole-series edit, `Expect` works out what the design says from the design's definitions, with no call into `Domain/Series`. It measures Δ (and, from an edited day, the move from where it shows to where it was dropped, in days in the series' zone, in clock time on the clock it shows on, and in length), finds the readings the drag table offers for the family and the landing (each with the design's refusal conditions, in the design's order, the first enabled one preselected), and for the reading the case chose maps every old raw occurrence by member: the member's new form evaluated in the period shifted by its k (a list of month days shifted as a whole moves every member by δ counted as the dragged member counts, by number or from the end, so the dragged day lands where it was dropped). The new start is the earliest image; the new days are the walk of the old pattern's periods in their new forms, before the start and after the end included; an UNTIL ends at the latest image, a kept COUNT counts from the new start; a new day within the images' span that is no image and whose source lies outside the old series is a gain at an edge, skipped under "Don't add" and not counted against a kept count; a skipped day goes to its image or is dropped; an edited day is keyed on its image or falls outside with the outcome chosen. A new pattern starts on its first day on or after the series' start moved by Δ and keeps the keys on its days; when it is what a drag of the old rule makes (a member's first occurrence dragged to another day of its period, the earliest such drag), that drag's correspondence is the other reading. A zone change has the two promises: the same clock in the new zone, or the same moment, where the wall clock it was put at is read on the clock it showed on and its deltas are measured from there as any others. A start whose clock falls in a spring gap is expected a period earlier, skipped.

Where the design offers readings, each reading's outcome is computed on its own: the plan must offer exactly the readings the design names (refusing those the design refuses, a reading that leaves no events among them, which a new rule or an end sent with a drag can), merge two only when their plans are the same (walked past the bound, up to 1,200 periods), and the persisted series must be the chosen reading's. The plan is never the expected value of anything except that the commit equals the plan.

A rule outside the drag table takes only the edits its capability allows, and they keep its days: the expected series is the expander's reading of the stored pattern from its start at the new clock, under the bound sent or the one it had.

## The Invariants

Every invariant is universal over the delta vector; none is keyed by an edit's name.

- **a** Every delta that is zero leaves its stored thing untouched on every row, read straight from the rows before and after: a field the edit didn't change, the tags and people, the trip flag, every row's zone and all-day flag without a zone or all-day change, every row's length without a length change; and an edit that sends no time, no rule and no bound keeps every key, shown time and skip exactly as stored, whatever the rule.
- **b** Every non-zero delta reaches every affected row: each changed field on the series and on every edited day that stays, except a day that owns the field and kept it (the `own*` choice); the day acted on takes it; the trip flag on every row; each edited day placed as its own list says (an own date kept, or moved by its occurrence's move when chosen; an own clock time kept in its own zone; an own length and all-day flag kept; the rest follows the series, and a day that owns none of them is at its key). A "this event" edit makes the fields it changed the day's own (`b-this`).
- **b-trip** The trip rule as an end state, read from the stored links after every edit: no live link has both its trip and its stop flagged, and no trip is its own stop; a refusal (`trip_nested`) writes nothing and is right only where the end state could nest (the flag sent to a whole series, from its first day, with a day in it that is a stop); the flag reaches every row of the series.
- **b-trip-links** A row exists for what refers to it: right after the edit, before Undo, every trip link the case made stands unchanged while its edited day's row does, and the row goes only when the edit deletes that day (a delete of it, or a plan's `deleted` outcome), never by going back to the series. The oracle's own lists include `trip` for a day a link names.
- **c** The persisted series equals the oracle's expectation for the options chosen (days, clock, zone, all-day flag, length, UNTIL written as the last occurrence when the start or pattern changed, skipped days, edited days, and no row for an edited day left with nothing of its own: every field it owned taken from the series, and for the day acted on a placement that is the series' at its new key), the plan offers the groups the outcome calls for (readings, bound, edge, outside, zone) with the design's preselections, and the plan's rule, start, bound and day outcomes equal what was persisted. The plan's data, field by field, is checked by the four checks below (The Plan's Data).
- **d** The day acted on shows where it was put (its instant under "the same moment"), unless the edit sent a new pattern or a new bound, which give it no special place.
- **e** A refusal writes nothing, a preview writes nothing, an edit the design refuses (its capability, every reading refused, nothing left) is refused, and one the design allows is not.
- **f** After Undo every row of events, event_tags, event_people and event_links the case made is byte-identical to before the edit (updated timestamps aside); the edit applied again gives the same rows under new ids; a second edit undone twice gives the state after the first, then before either.
- **g** The stored start is the first raw occurrence and a day of its rule, at creation and after every edit, on the series and on a part a split made.
- **h** Every skipped day and every edited day's key is a raw occurrence of its rule; none past a bounded series' end (`h-past-end`); one edited row per day.
- **i** (worked out for every other case, the same rows read as a Google calendar's) Until 1.5.2 a Google calendar is offered only the edits that keep the rule text, and then as the local plan with no outcome claimed for edited days; an edit that keeps the text (not a list's member reading, no all-day change, no new pattern) is offered.

"This and following" is checked against the same oracle (the old part keeps exactly the days before the split, its earlier edited days untouched and no skipped day from the split on; the new part is the part from the split dragged as a whole series with the design's preselected options); delete this, delete following and the answers have their own checks, with the same universal ones around them.

## The Plan's Data

Four checks read a committed whole-series plan against the rows stored after the commit, the rows before it and the oracle, never against another value of the plan and never through the model's writers. The zone is the harness's own: by the zone rule (series-model.md, One zone), the series' zone when it is timed after the commit, none when it is all-day; a timed placement is written in that zone, or in its row's own zone, named, when there is none; an all-day row is its date.

- **c-shape** Every field by its exact path and role, and no field the contract doesn't name: keys are UTC ISO ending in `Z`; identity, pattern and all-day values are valid dates; a placement's or an entry's offset is its zone's at that instant (`+00:00` is UTC, never required as `Z`), its date and clock one instant, its zone named exactly when it isn't the plan's; list entries are `{start, allDay, tz?}`, an all-day one at `T00:00:00+00:00`; `bound.until` is basic format; `earlierSeries` is old or new beside a date and null beside none; a reading option beside another carries `dates`, `then`, `earlier` and `earlierSeries`; `plan.zone`, `rule.to.tz` and each note's `tz` equal the derived zone. The plan refusing the whole change has its own shape: no `zone`, `acted` or `bound.from`, its start in the old zone, or a bare date for an all-day series.
- **c-placement** Every day by outcome. An edited row's `outcome` is the harness's comparison of its stored instant (its start) and kind before and after (`stays`, `moves`), `separate` when its row left the series, `rejoins` or `deleted` when it went, by whether the oracle keeps its day inside; `day` is its old key's day in the old zone; `ownDate`, when it owned its date before, is where it was in its own zone; `before` is the stored row before by the zone rule, `after` the row after (the standalone row for `separate`, the occurrence at its image for `rejoins`), with `kind` when it changes; `shows`, `to` and `at` are those dates and that instant. The day acted on with no row: before at its occurrence, after at the row created for it or the event of its own it became. Skipped days show on their identity day and go to the oracle's image; a generated skip is a stored skip on its day. A moved-day choice names the day the harness's own placement puts the day on for it, and the chosen one the day the commit put it on. `plan.acted` is there exactly when the day acted on has a row, with its key, day, before and own date. Losses and gains over the model's stated window (all of a bounded series; of an unbounded one, through the latest of the day acted on, the start and every exception's day, and a span on by frequency) with its exclusions: every listed one must be one, every one within the window listed. The bound's last days and UNTILs against the stored rules before and after; the rules' starts and `plan.start` against the stored masters. A reading's `dates`, `then` and `earlier` against the oracle's walk of that reading at the new clock (the rule's own hour when it names one); `earlier` compares the reading's events and the series' today event by event by instant and kind, never by day number (two zones' day numbers name different days), and names the earlier of the first pair that differs by its own series' day, its edge days included or not, unbounded or bounded, its days before the day acted on counted from its old day or from where it landed: the contract leaves those open, so each is worked out and a value is asserted where they all agree (Known, Accepted Differences). Notes both ways, by tzdata: every day of the stored series from the cutoff through a year on which its clock names no instant (a gap) or two (an overlap, whatever the size of the change: Lord Howe's is half an hour) must have its note, and every note must name such a day, at that occurrence's instant (an overlap's first), in the series' zone.
- **c-next** Coming up, assembled from what the commit stored: the series' occurrences (read on from the cutoff as far as it takes), its edited rows, and the event of its own the day acted on became (rows an outside choice kept on their own aren't in it, and under a new pattern the day acted on has no special place). The cutoff is the least of the landed day, the request's day, the acted row's day after and the created row's day, each in the display zone; the list must equal the set's first four in the order days show (by day, all-day first, then instant, then key), and `nextMore` whether a fifth exists. A rule the expander can't read lists nothing, and `nextMore` says whether it has no end. The chosen reading's `next` is the first three.
- **c-untouched** On model commits (the whole-series path), every row of the case and the two control events made before it (one in the case's calendar, one in another) by row and column: the series' placement columns and skips change only where the oracle changes them (its rule for a bound, its start, rule and skips for a change of time, days or pattern, its kind, zone and end where those change); an edited row that stays changes a placement column only where the harness's own placement of it (its image's key, its own date, clock, length and kind) gives another value than it had, and an own-list placement entry only where its kind changes; the day acted on takes where the request put it; a row the oracle has no image for is counted as unoracled; content columns, tags, people and own-list entries only where the request changed the field and the row takes it (its own list and the chosen options); a row leaves the series (and takes what leaving writes: its parent, key, uid, rule, skips, Google id, own list and source) or goes only where the oracle says it does; links stand; the calendars change only in the bookkeeping. The bookkeeping allowed anywhere, by name: `events.updated_at`, `calendars.synctoken` and `calendars.updated_at` (written with it), the `dav_changes` table, and the undo tables `mutations`, `mutation_rows`, `mutation_touches` and `mutation_calendar_refs`. Inserted rows outside the set (the row created for the day acted on, the event of its own it became), an event of another account, and label names the request doesn't name fail.

## Reporting

The summary ends with the tallies: failures outside the known paths, accepted ones by cause, failures of the plan's data checks on cases under a known path (counted on their own, never accepted), unoracled parts, skipped cases, and the executions of each plan-data check by role and outcome; the run fails when a required category (`Report::REQUIRED`) ran zero times (a full run of 1,000 cases or more, or the CI slice with the fixed cases). Every record carries its seed, case (or fixed case) and revision (the checkout's commit and the series model's content as loaded).

## Mutations

`mutations/list.php` names one mutation per entry of the plan's list: a small patch, as find-and-replace pairs each found exactly once, to a copy of one file, the check it must trip, and the fixed cases that show it. `mutations/run.php` runs the mutations' fixed cases unmutated (they must pass; one that fails is a finding about the tree, named in the baseline line), then each mutation with its copy loaded in place of the tree's, judged by the failures it adds to the unmutated run, record by record: it must add failures of the named checks and of no other. A patch's text must occur exactly once, so when the code it names changes the runner fails until the patch is retargeted (the `earlier` mutation names the model's detection of where two readings differ, which review D2 asks the model to change). The two Undo gate mutations patch `Trips.php` and run `server/tests/db/undo-trips.test.php` instead, which must fail. A check that doesn't fail its mutation is not a check: fix the check, not the mutation. `--search ID --seed S --cases N` lists the drawn cases a mutation trips, to save one as a fixed case (`run.sh --case N --save-spec DIR`). It runs in CI after the database suite.

```bash
BC_TEST_DB=127.0.0.1:3306 php tools/edit-harness/mutations/run.php [--only ID,...]
```

## Known Code Paths

The known list names code paths, never an invariant or an edit's name. The summary ends with `Clean: every failure is under a known code path.` when nothing falls outside them.

- **following-1.4.6**: "this and following", delete following and an answer from a day on stay on their 1.4.6 code until 1.5.1 moves them onto the model. The harness decides this from what it sent: the scope (following, delete following, attendance following) and a day acted on after the series' first. From the first day those edits are the whole series (`following@first` and the others), run on the model, and are checked as such; they are not under this path.

sabre/vobject 4.6.1 yields a monthly rule's UNTIL as an occurrence when it falls on the first of a month the rule doesn't give, at the series' time (a date for an all-day series). The app's rule iterator skips it instead, as it skips any instant outside a rule's own days, for the model and the window alike, so the harness reads the expander as it is and a phantom day would show as a disagreement with the oracle.

Every other failure is a finding about the application. A failure of a check of the plan's data (c-shape, c-placement, c-next, c-untouched) is never under a known path, on any path. Once 1.5.1 lands, delete `following-1.4.6` from `Known::PATHS` and from this list.

## Known, Accepted Differences

- Where the design leaves a choice open, the harness says so instead of guessing: "the old one with its days shifted" is read as the earliest drag within a member's period that makes the new pattern, the one direction that reading gives; an edited day that changes kind by inheriting the series' all-day flag keeps no expectation for its own length, which has no meaning across the two kinds; a clock that happens twice is its first instant where no instant names it, a start included, an instant given stays that instant (an edited day's, a key's, where the request put the day acted on), and every expansion runs from the stored instant; a DAILY;BYDAY series keeps the expander's shifted clock after a spring gap, which the design accepts, and a day acted on whose image the expander shows at another clock keeps where it was put as an edited day of its own time, which the plan creates and the oracle counts.
- The adoption readings for an edit from an edited day ("This becomes the series' day", "This becomes the series' time", Changing the Series from an Edited Day) are not computed: the oracle reads every such edit as "the series moves the same way". A plan that offered them would show as readings the design names but the harness doesn't (`c-readings`), so the first run after #189 lands needs them added to `Expect::readings`.
- A sent bound with a drag is applied to the new series as sent: a sent COUNT counts scheduled occurrences from the new start, with the skips the change generates not counted against it, as under "keep N events".
- The stored day of a start in a skipped hour (the series stored from the period before, that day skipped) is no day of the old series: the oracle reads the series from its first shown day, and no exception is placed on it.
- The day acted on is never drawn as an edited day that owns its all-day flag (one of the other kind than its series): a drag from it is measured on another clock than the series', which the design doesn't define. Fixed cases edit the fields of such a day, from its row and through the series, in both kinds (`cases/acted-*`).
- An all-day series stored the old way (before 0.9.16: local midnights of its days, its own zone) reads as UTC dates in the model and the harness alike, so in an eastern zone both read it a day early and their agreement proves nothing. `cases/limitations/legacy-all-day-series-at-local-midnights.json` pins what happens to one in Auckland today (its start off its rule, the occurrence asked for not found), and the database suite fails when that changes.
- The contract names no rule for which occurrence coming up lists as the day acted on, so an occurrence's entry is checked as `occurrence` or `acted`; a row created for the day acted on, or the event of its own it became, is `acted`, an edited row `edited`.
- A reading's `dates`, `then` and `earlier` are open three ways in the contract (its edge days included or not, its rule unbounded or its series bounded, "before the day acted on" from its old day or from where it landed): each value is asserted where every reading of them agrees and counted as unoracled elsewhere, and `dates` only where two readings are offered ("the other reading's" names one).
- Two edited days landing on one day by a drag (one moved to the day of another) is allowed by the app and by the harness.
- The oracle compares days and instants, not rule strings: a rule written in another equivalent form passes.
