# Google Probe Findings, 2026-11-13

Run 2026-11-13 against a test calendar in America/Los_Angeles, groups 3, 4 and 10: 56 cases, 1,161 requests. New since [the run of 2026-11-10](probe-2026-11-10.md): 3.36 to 3.47, 4.3 and group 10. The raw report stays out of the repository; this page gives its findings in general terms, with no event ids, addresses or calendar id. Etags are named E1, E2... within a case, in the order first seen.

**Repeated cases.** Every case run before (3.1 to 3.35, and 4.1 and 4.2 from 2026-11-07) gave the same finding as its last run, word for word once ids and retry counts are set aside.

## Back to old keys (issue 256)

Each case starts from a weekly series of four from 2027-03-01 (10:00 Los Angeles, or all-day), occurrence 2 renamed, occurrence 3 deleted. Every event is changed and then changed back, each change sent as the app sends it: the master PATCH with the If-Match of a list read before it, then occurrence 2 restored (its own title) and occurrence 3 cancelled, each PATCH with the If-Match the app chains (the read-back's master etag, then each answer's etag). After a refusal the remaining steps were sent as Finish sends them (a fresh list read, its master etag as the If-Match). After every request each id the two days ever had, in every form, was read by GET and in the `showDeleted` list.

**What Google does with a dropped record when the series returns to its keys (3.36, 3.37, 3.39, 3.40, 10.2, 10.3).** A record a master change dropped (cancelled, no `originalStartTime`, no `recurringEventId`, under its old id) keeps the etag it had when it was dropped while the series is in another shape. When a later master change makes its id a day of the series again, Google **revives it**: listed and read as a live record (`confirmed`, with `originalStartTime` and `recurringEventId`), at the series' values. The renamed day's title is gone and a deleted day is live again, so a day deleted before the first change shows again. Its etag is the master's after that write.

**Etags (3.36, 3.37, 3.40, 4.3, 10.2, 10.6).** One etag per series holds only until a day's record is written. A PATCH to an id with no record (an ordinary day) sometimes gave the master and the new record the same new etag (3.36 change 1, 3.40 change 1, 4.3, 10.6) and sometimes not: the record one, the master another (3.37 change 1). A PATCH to an id that already holds a record, live or revived, moved **that record's** etag alone; the master and the other records kept theirs (3.36, 3.37 and 3.40 change 2; 10.2). Once, after an ordinary day was cancelled, a record written earlier also took an etag of its own (3.36 change 1). So the etag one write answers with is not, in general, the etag the next day holds. That is the 412: the third request carried the second's answer etag, and its target held another. This replaces the reading of 5.1 to 5.3 (2026-11-07) that every resource of a series carries one etag: those cases wrote a master or an instance and then read, and never wrote a second day.

**What Google would have taken.** The etag the target id itself holds, as the `showDeleted` list (or a GET of the id) showed it just before the write: in every case resent that way, the write was taken.

**The rule this shows (for issue 256).** A write to one day of a series is conditional on that day's own etag, read just before the write, never on an etag chained from another resource's answer.

## Which rule changes keep a record (D1)

From group 3's first state (occurrence 6, a Monday, cancelled; occurrences 3, 4 and 5 edited), the rule alone, as the app sends a rule change.

| 3.43 a weekday added (BYDAY=MO,WE;COUNT=20) | PATCH master, the rule | 200 | Every record kept, the cancelled one included. 3.7 (BYDAY=MO,WE;COUNT=10) dropped the cancelled one only because 5 April is past that rule's tenth occurrence. |
| 3.44 the weekday changed (BYDAY=TU) | PATCH master, the rule | 200 | Every record dropped: none is on a day of the new rule. |
| 3.45 COUNT longer (12) | PATCH master, the rule | 200 | Every record kept. |
| 3.46 COUNT to an UNTIL after occurrence 7 | PATCH master, the rule | 200 | Every record kept. |
| 3.47 every fifth week (INTERVAL=5;COUNT=2) | PATCH master, the rule | 200 | The cancelled record (5 April, the rule's second day) kept; the three others, on weeks the rule no longer has, dropped. |

**The rule.** Through a rule change a record, cancelled or live, is kept exactly when its `originalStartTime` is still an occurrence of the new rule, and dropped otherwise. There is no separate drop of cancelled records on a change of the rule's days; the earlier reading of 3.7 was wrong.

## What this changes

- Issue 256 is the chained If-Match. A day's write must carry that day's own etag, read just before. It reaches a clock change and back (3.40) and even a first switch from all-day to timed (3.37), not only a switch back, and anything that returns a series to keys it had before: a bound taken back (10.2) and a split taken back (10.3) revive dropped records the same way.
- A dropped record comes back to life, at the series' values, when its id is a day of the series again. A day deleted before the change shows again, and an edited day's own values are gone. The app's steps that put them back are needed and are taken.
- Rule changes keep records by identity alone (D1): kept exactly when still a day of the new rule.
- One day of an all-day series can take a clock time (3.38); the refusal can go.
- A deleted master takes its records with it; made live again, it brings every record back, a day deleted before it included (10.4).
- A colour on one day is a record with a field only Google holds, and a label Google adds by itself; a master change keeps both (10.6).
