# Scale and limits

## Who it's for

Better-Cal is meant to replace a standard, individual, one-person Google Calendar. It isn't meant for enterprise use or for extremely heavy users (thousands of events a week, dozens of busy shared calendars, a whole team on one install). If you need that, feel free to fork it; the limits below are where you'd start.

For a sense of what "one person" means here: a normal multi-month view may contain thousands of occurrences, and the defaults leave substantial room above that while still bounding pathological inputs.

## Limits you might notice

- **1,000 occurrences per repeating series per request.** That covers a daily event across the widest window the app ever asks for (two years), so normal use never hits it. A series that does (something hourly, say) shows its first 1,000 occurrences in that range, and the app tells you which series was cut short instead of quietly showing fewer. API callers get the same report as a `capped` list of event ids.
- **Up to 700 days per app load.** The events API has a roughly two-year ceiling; the app keeps one requested window below 700 days and asks you to narrow a wider one instead of launching an unbounded set of parallel chunks.
- **About ten years either way of today in the scrolling views.** Month, week and day views scroll roughly ten years back and forward from today. Jumping to a date further out stops at the edge. Search and the API aren't limited this way.
- **Busy days in month view.** A day shows about seven events on a tall window (fewer on a short one or a phone), then "+N more"; trips and people's away times stack two deep per week before they overflow into the day's expanded list. Nothing is dropped, it just takes a click.
- **Outbound feeds hold 5,000 events.** A feed you publish covers the past year onward (plus every repeating series), up to 5,000 events. Past that, the oldest past events are left off first, never upcoming ones, and the feed's card in Settings says so; `BETTERCAL_LIMIT_OUTFEED_EVENTS` raises the cap. A feed made from a saved search holds the search's top 500 matches.
- **Dense event windows fail as a whole, not partially.** One operation may visit 5,000 repeating series, generate 10,000 occurrences, spend eight seconds on recurrence work, and admit 32 MiB of stored event text/JSON by default. If any aggregate limit is reached, the app asks for narrower dates or fewer calendars rather than presenting an incomplete window as complete.
- **Published feeds are at most 16 MiB.** Source rows and trip relationships are admitted before serialization, and generation stops with a small `413` response if the complete `.ics` would exceed its budget.
- **Repeating rules from outside** (feeds, imports, CalDAV, Google, email): an interval up to 1,000 and a COUNT up to 100,000. A rule past that arrives as a single event rather than a broken series.
- **Reminders:** up to five per event, at most four weeks ahead. Dense calendars are scanned in durable 250-event slices; completed slices send normally, continuation slices run immediately while worker time remains, and unfinished work resumes in the next worker run without starting a duplicate scan cycle. Reminder, ad-hoc notification and test-email attempts share persistent caps of 100/account/hour and 500/account/day, with recipient and installation ceilings too; a reminder scan additionally stops at 50 emails per cycle. Excess scheduled email is dropped and reported in System status; push continues independently. Explicit test email also retains its stricter five-per-ten-minute limit.
- **Plugins:** one `eventsWindow()` call returns at most 5,000 occurrences by default. A complete `syncEvents()` snapshot is at most 2,000 events and 8 MiB of scalar event data; a refusal or mid-sync error keeps the previous snapshot intact. Manual **Run now** admits at most 30 newly queued jobs/hour and 100/day, while at most 16 plugin job identities may be pending/running. Repeated requests for an already-active job coalesce instead of growing the queue.
- **Editing one repeat occurrence:** `instanceStart` must be a real, non-skipped occurrence generated by the series and within about 20 years of today. This does not constrain whole-series edits without an occurrence anchor. Existing detached provider exceptions remain addressable for compatibility.

## Limits you can change

Imports, subscribed feeds, CalDAV objects, incoming email, skipped occurrences, description length and filter work all have caps that protect the server from oversized or hostile input. Each has a sensible default for one person and can be raised in `.env.php`; [`.env.example`](../.env.example) lists them (the `BETTERCAL_LIMIT_*` settings) with their defaults. The main ones:

- An uploaded `.ics` file: 25 MiB and 20,000 events.
- One command-line Takeout migration: 1,000 new `.ics` files and 1 GiB in aggregate by default, with the normal per-file import limits still applied. Each calendar commits separately, so a failed file can be corrected and the command safely resumed without redoing completed calendars.
- One JSON API request: 1 MiB (operator-adjustable up to a 4 MiB hard ceiling).
- One ordinary ICS subscription: 20,000 events per poll (and 20 MiB, which isn't configurable).
- One Google calendar poll: 20,000 remote resources, 32 MiB of decompressed responses, 20 pages and 40 seconds, including any retry after Google expires a sync token. The event count is lowered automatically when PHP's `memory_limit` cannot safely carry the decoded changes, cached snapshot and database sync structures at once; the stored snapshot's actual text/JSON size is checked before it is loaded, so calendars with unusually large but valid descriptions fail cleanly too. A refusal keeps the last successful calendar and sync position, records the reason on the calendar, and lets the worker continue. `BETTERCAL_LIMIT_GOOGLE_SYNC_*` settings can raise these only to the hard ceilings in `.env.example`.
- One Google account inventory: 5,000 calendars, 5 MiB, 20 pages and 20 seconds, including its access-token refresh. `BETTERCAL_LIMIT_GOOGLE_CALENDAR_LIST_*` settings have hard ceilings as documented in `.env.example`.
- One CalDAV object: 1 MiB, with up to 500 changed occurrences. The declared length is checked first and the actual stream is read only up to the limit plus one byte, before parsing. Other CalDAV requests (REPORT, PROPPATCH, MKCALENDAR and the rest) may send at most 1 MiB, and a PUT one object plus 64 KiB; anything larger, declared or chunked, gets a 413 before Sabre reads it. Normal events, even with long descriptions, are much smaller. The web server may still buffer requests before PHP receives them.
- One incoming email: 5 MiB (operator-adjustable up to a 25 MiB hard ceiling); up to 200 events in an emailed invitation. Size is checked from IMAP metadata before display headers or bodies are decoded.
- Public email in aggregate: 100 new events or first-invitation candidates per rolling day, 25 per claimed sender, 500 future/repeating mail-created events, and at most 100 open emailed invitation decisions in Review; paid mail reading is 20 model calls per hour, 50 per day, and 20 per claimed sender/day. Separate account-wide limits cannot be reset with a new From, Message-ID, or event UID. Review shows one aggregate notice when a limit is reached; the original email remains in the mailbox. The open-decision ceiling is a hard cap so public mail cannot bury the Review list, though an operator may lower it. Changes to an existing invitation that wait in Review count toward the same daily and per-sender numbers, and one event keeps at most three waiting changes; a fourth replaces the newest. Emails that can't be read are listed one by one up to 20, then counted in one notice.
- Quick Add model assist: 120 calls per rolling hour and 500 per day account-wide, with smaller 30/hour and 150/day shares for each API token and at most four concurrent calls (two per token). Preview calls count because they use the same paid model; deterministic parsing remains available when capacity is reached. Quick Add text is limited to 4,000 characters.
- Plain-language prompt filters: 40 model batches per rolling hour and 200 per day account-wide, with 20/hour and 100/day for one subscribed calendar. One batch evaluates up to 25 events. Work above that waits and Review explains that unevaluated events remain visible until the queued checks run.
- One Gemini response is streamed into at most 1 MiB; larger responses are abandoned and the caller uses its existing fallback. This protects Quick Add, prompt filters, ranking, mail extraction and model-enabled plugins at their shared transport boundary.
- An event description: 65,535 characters, after which it's cut.

An import that runs into one of these says which setting raises it.

### ZIP imports and malformed dates

A ZIP may contain 10,000 entries, 1 GiB of expanded data across all entries, 1,000 admitted calendar files and 250,000 events across those files. Ignored files count toward entry and byte budgets too. The settings are `BETTERCAL_LIMIT_IMPORT_ZIP_ENTRIES`, `BETTERCAL_LIMIT_IMPORT_ZIP_TOTAL_BYTES`, `BETTERCAL_LIMIT_TAKEOUT_FILES` and `BETTERCAL_LIMIT_IMPORT_ZIP_EVENTS`; their ceilings are 50,000 entries, 4 GiB, 5,000 calendar files and 1,000,000 events. Existing per-file and admitted-calendar-byte limits still apply. These budgets leave room for an invented heavy-use export with 50 calendars, 50,000 events and about 100 MiB of expanded text, provided each calendar fits the host's memory budget.

The whole ZIP is checked before any calendar is created. Calendar contents are read twice, one file at a time: first to count events, then to import. The first read counts the bytes each calendar file actually unpacks to, not the size its archive header claims, against the admitted-calendar budget, so an archive that understates its sizes is refused before anything is created. Duplicate archive names are refused. A bad calendar file is listed as skipped, while other files can import. A host can still stop a long request; each calendar commits separately, and retrying leaves calendars already imported alone. To recover rejected events from a partly imported ZIP, extract the affected .ics file, correct its dates and import it into the calendar already created. Reimporting the ZIP skips calendars with names already present.

A date field that should contain one date cannot contain a comma-separated list. Such an event is rejected before parsing in feeds, email and imports; good events in the same input continue. Imports report skipped events, feeds show a warning in their settings and Settings, System, and mail creates a durable Review notice. Feeds keep previous rows for a rejected event's UID. If its UID cannot be recovered, that poll skips deletions but still applies valid events; normal deletion resumes on a completely readable poll. CalDAV refuses a malformed submitted object as a whole, including jCal, so no recurring series is saved partially. Correct the dates in the original calendar and retry.

An import into a calendar moving to Google is refused until the move finishes or is stopped. A finished Google calendar is not a local import target. No event is acknowledged and then lost at cutover.

### Trips and reminder devices

Trip Planner keeps its existing input and candidate budgets. Event text is stored once in date intervals; candidate windows keep scores, and only selected windows get conflict explanations. Its existing run budget is checked during large loops, and a refusal keeps previous suggestions. Trip membership Undo records contain only the changed link, so adding many members does not repeatedly store the whole trip. Reading a whole trip still costs work proportional to its members.

An account may register 100 distinct push destinations by default, adjustable with `BETTERCAL_LIMIT_PUSH_DEVICES_PER_ACCOUNT` up to 500. A person normally has a handful of phones and browser profiles, so this leaves room for many old devices. A URL fragment does not make another device. Existing registrations can refresh at the limit, and an upgrade never removes distinct devices merely because the account is above it. Remove old devices in Settings, Notifications to make room. Old aliases with different credential owners cannot silently take over each other; the enrollment error asks you to remove conflicting registrations first.

Delivery still visits devices in order and each outside request has its own timeout. This bounds new enrollment, not the total duration of a delivery run: many slow destinations, especially an existing account already above the limit, can still occupy the worker for a long time. There is no new delivery queue in this change.

## Background work

The worker runs every minute and spreads slow work across runs: up to ten emails per run, one plain-language filter model call per job, and a few ranking calls at a time. On a busy day that means a new invitation or filter verdict can take a few minutes to appear, not that the calendar event is skipped.
