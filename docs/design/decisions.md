# Decisions

A record of the calls Better-Cal makes where two or more readings were each defensible and one had to be codified, so any of them can be revisited later without digging through commits or reviews. Started 2026-11-04; revised the same day after an outside review of the first draft.

Each entry carries a status, because "we chose this" and "the code does this" are different claims:

- **Standard:** the standard says it; we follow it.
- **Deviation:** the standard says otherwise; we depart on purpose, and say what it costs.
- **Choice:** the standard or the situation leaves it open; we picked.
- **Workaround:** what the code does because of a constraint (a library, a storage shape), not because it is the ideal reading; kept until the constraint goes.
- **Defect:** the code does this and shouldn't; its repair is scheduled, which is a release decision, not a reading.
- **Planned:** decided, not yet built.
- **Open:** undecided.

And each says whether it is verified: by which check, or not yet. What doesn't belong here: visual design choices, which keep their own records ([calendar marks](calendar-marks.md), [day marks](day-marks.md)).

Two principles behind most of the entries. A change previews its consequences and offers the reasonable readings as choices. And a preselected reading avoids silent deletion, unexpected detachment and the overwriting of a deliberate exception, while every addition and every changed bound is shown plainly; preserving a count and preserving intent are different things, and no default guarantees the latter.

## Repeating Events and the Standard

- **A clock time the spring change skips, for any spelling of the rule.** Status: deviation. Chosen: the occurrence is kept that day, at the same clock plus the gap (02:30 becomes 03:30), and later days are at their own clock. The standard's 3.3.10 says a generated instance at a nonexistent time "MUST be ignored and MUST NOT be counted"; its 3.3.5 rule for a single date-time (the offset before the gap) is what we apply to every generated instance. Why: a weekly event that vanishes one week a year is worse than one an hour later. Cost: a strict client reading the app's ICS may leave that day out and end a COUNT series an occurrence later. One rule for every spelling: a plain DAILY, a rule naming its hour in BYHOUR, and a rule listing all seven weekdays express no different intention, since another calendar or serializer picks the spelling. The app's own expander (1.5.0) applies the one rule with no state carried between days, for every interval and from any window; until then a BYHOUR rule omitted the day and a rule listing weekdays kept the shifted clock for the rest of the series (the library's readings, [#201](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/201)), and migration 064 moves the keys those readings wrote. Verified 2026-11-04: the expander's hand-listed tests (`server/tests/expander.php`, "Gap:": five spellings, a monthly and a yearly rule, a half-hour gap, two windows and the model reading the same keys), and the migration's database test; the harness's evaluator still reads the library's two gap readings and is updated by its owner. Verified 2026-11-05 from windows far from the start ([#201](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/201)): `server/tests/expander.php`, "Far windows (#201)", hand-listed, a short and a long window, a year and nine years from the start, across the gap day, for daily, weekly and monthly rules with and without BYHOUR; the issue's own reproduction is "a daily weekday list, April 2028, short and long". Where: [series model](series-model.md#zones-dst-and-all-day-days). Chosen 2026-11-02 for plain clocks, extended to every spelling 2026-11-04 on review; built 2026-11-04.
- **A clock time the fall change repeats.** Status: standard, with a choice on top. Chosen: a wall clock given with no instant is its earlier instant, as 3.3.5 says; an instant given stays that instant; the plan's note says which of the two the series is at and what the zone data says of it. Else: the later instant (what PHP reads a repeated wall clock as, and what the library's monthly and yearly steps gave a later year's day before 1.5.0, whose keys migration 064 moves). Verified: the harness's second-fold case and the fold mutation, with the model's unit tests and the expander's ("Fold:", 2026-11-04). Where: series model, same section; the `fold` and `daylight` fields of a note in the [contract](../api-contract.md). Settled 2026-11-02; the note's data 2026-11-04.
- **A start that would fall in the skipped hour.** Status: workaround. The code stores the series one period earlier, on the rule's latest day before it where the clock exists, as a generated skip with COUNT raised by one, and every sentence names the first day shown. Why a workaround: storage holds one instant and the expander takes every day's clock from the start, so a start stored at the shifted clock would move the whole series an hour. The ideal is a start that is the day the person named; that needs storage of the clock apart from the instant. Every count a person sees is the events shown: the API's rule says the shown COUNT and a COUNT sent is the shown one, the writer adding the stored day back (was, until 1.5.1: the editor's "Ends after" showed one more than the events shown, [#202](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/202)). A start typed into the skipped hour reaches the server as the clock typed (`typed` in the contract), so a new series typed there is stored by this same rule (was: the server received the shifted instant and stored a series an hour later). Verified: the harness's anchored-start cases; 2026-11-05, `server/tests/series-model.php`, "Typed clock:" and "Ends after:", and the smoke test "quick add typed clock". Where: series model, same section. Settled 2026-11-02; count and typed clock 2026-11-05.
- **An end typed after a start in the skipped hour.** Status: choice. Chosen: the end is its clock moved by the push the gap gave the start, then read by the one wall-clock rule, so the range moves as one: 02:30 to 03:30 typed on the spring change day is an hour, shown 03:30 to 04:30 that day and 02:30 to 03:30 on every other day of a series, and 02:30 to 05:00 keeps its two and a half hours (03:30 to 06:00). A start that exists has no push, so every other range is its two clocks' own instants, as before: 01:30 to 04:00 across the gap is 01:30 to 04:00, and the fall change's ranges are untouched. An end at or before the start on the clocks typed is refused (400 "end must be after start"), checked on the clocks since the push could carry it past the start; quick add's strip still reads such an end as the next day. Else: each clock read alone, which put 02:30 and 03:30 on one instant and refused the hour ([#222](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/222), 1.3.0 to 1.5.1) and gave a series typed 02:30 to 04:30 there an hour on every other day; or the length as the clocks' difference for every range, which reads 01:00 to 04:00 on the spring day as 01:00 to 05:00 and 00:30 to 02:30 on the fall day as ending at 01:30, against clocks that exist. Why: a series has one length and the person named it by the clocks of an ordinary day, and a single event typed there shows as that series' change-day occurrence would; where both clocks exist they are what the person named. One function (`Wall::typedEnd`) reads it for the create, the edit and quick add's parse, and the client's `typedInstants` mirrors it for the editor's and quick add's checks. An instant the request gives beside the clocks stays that instant where it is one the clock names, the rule above for a repeated clock applied to a typed one (fixed 2026-11-07: a two-hour event dragged to end in the hour the fall change repeats sends the later 02:30 beside the clocks, and the typed end read alone as the earlier one made it an hour; `server/tests/run.php`, "typed onto the fall change", and the harness's fixed case of the seed 31 drag that found it); the client's reading of clocks alone is unchanged. Verified 2026-11-06: `server/tests/run.php`, "typed across the gap" (a northern zone, a southern one, a half-hour offset and a half-hour gap; create, series, edit and parse), and the smoke tests of the same name. Where: series model, Zones, DST and All-Day Days; `typed` in the [contract](../api-contract.md). Settled 2026-11-06.
- **A time dragged on the grid.** Status: choice. Chosen: the time grid is a wall clock, drawn the same on a day whose clocks change, and a drag is measured on it in absolute wall minutes (a day's number times 1440 plus the minute), never in elapsed time: a moved event keeps its length on the clock, a resized end goes to the clock it was dropped at, a drawn range is the clocks it covers, and the result shows where the preview was. The minutes are then read by the typed range's rule above, one function (`wallRangeInstants`, which `typedInstants` calls in the device's zone), with the one addition typing has no need of, which pass of a repeated hour: a resize holds the end not dragged at its exact instant and reads the dragged one beside it (an end after the start, a start before the end); a move reads the start on the side of the instant it left that the drag went to (at or after it going forward, at or before it going back), so a nudge of an event in the second pass stays in it, and an event brought from another day takes the first pass going forward and the second going back; a drawn range, like a typed one, takes the first. A start pushed by the gap onto or past a held end is backwards, and the resize does nothing. Else: elapsed minutes from the pointer, which made an hour-long event dropped at 1:30 on the fall day end at 1:30 again and read a nudge within the second pass as the first ([#190](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/190)); or every repeated clock as the first, which put a resized end before its start ([#185](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/185)); or the earlier instant for every move, which turns a 15-minute nudge of a second-pass event into a 45-minute jump the other way. Why: the grid shows clocks, so a drop must mean what the preview showed, and a drag asks the same question a typed time does. Verified 2026-11-06: the smoke tests "times when dragging (#190)": a move, a resize and a draw into the repeated hour (both passes), the skipped hour and an ordinary day, in a northern zone, a southern one and one on a half-hour offset, and the typed reading equal to the drawn one. Where: `web/src/lib/dates.js` (`wallRangeInstants`), `web/src/ui/monthmath.js` (`movedDates`, `resizedDates`, `drawnDates`). Settled 2026-11-06.
- **HOURLY across a skipped hour.** Status: choice. Chosen: the walk steps by wall clock, so an every-3-hours series reads 00:00, 03:00, 06:00 on the change day and keeps its pattern after; a skipped wall clock resolves by the offset before the gap. Else: elapsed time (00:00, 04:00, 07:00 that day), under which the series' clocks shift by the change until the next change shifts them back. Why: a rule in hours names clock positions a person reads off a day, like a daily rule does. A rule in minutes or seconds is different on purpose: its periods are stepped in elapsed time from the start's own, as the standard's table reads them, so `FREQ=HOURLY;INTERVAL=3` and `FREQ=MINUTELY;INTERVAL=180` are not the same rule here. That distinction is the weakest-argued entry in this record: it rests on how people write the two, not on the standard, and only imports write minute rules. Verified: the harness's evaluator steps the wall clock since c74c57a, with a spring and a fall example in its hand-listed set, and the expander's tests list the spring and fall days of an hourly and a minute rule side by side ("HOURLY:", "MINUTELY:", 2026-11-04); the earlier revision of this record claimed agreement before the evaluator had it. Where: `tools/edit-harness/README.md`, series model. Settled 2026-11-04.
- **An instant the walk reaches twice.** Status: standard. A wall-clock step that resolves onto the next step's instant (an hourly rule's step to the skipped 02:00 resolves to 03:00, and the next step is 03:00) yields that instant once and counts it once: 3.8.5.3 says "Duplicate instances are ignored." Stated once in the walk, for every frequency. Verified 2026-11-04: `server/tests/expander.php` and the harness's hand-listed examples through the window expander, which is where the duplicate was found. Settled 2026-11-04.
- **A set position on a day whose clocks merge.** Status: standard. A period's set is its instants: its local times are resolved (a skipped clock by the gap rule above) and two that land on one instant are one member, and BYSETPOS counts positions among those instants, positive from the first and negative from the last, for every frequency. So `DAILY;BYHOUR=2,3;BYMINUTE=0,30;BYSETPOS=3` has no occurrence on Los Angeles' spring change day, whose four clocks are two instants (03:00 and 03:30). Else: the position taken among the nominal clocks before they resolve (03:00 that day), which the harness's evaluator read for date frequencies while its hourly path and the expander resolved first. Why: an occurrence is an instant and 3.8.5.3 ignores duplicates, so a clock merged into another holds no position of its own. Verified 2026-11-04: the harness's hand-listed examples (positive and negative positions, with and without a collision), its fixed case, and the mutation that restores the other reading in the expander. Where: `tools/edit-harness/README.md`, The Oracle. Settled 2026-11-04.
- **A rule naming its hour in a zone whose clocks move by half an hour.** Status: defect, fixed in 1.5.0. Read from the start the library's hour-by-hour stepping drifted the minute (03:00 became 03:30 after the change); read from a later window it didn't; keys were stored by the from-start reading. The app's own expander expands clock parts by the standard's table, as wall clocks of each day, so 03:00 stays 03:00 from any window, and migration 064 moves the keys the drift wrote. Verified 2026-11-04: the expander's test "Clock parts: 03:00 by BYHOUR stays 03:00 after a half-hour change", and the half-hour gap in "Gap:". Where: [#202](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/202). Deferred 2026-11-04; fixed 2026-11-04 for 1.5.0.
- **COUNT and the days that are not shown.** Status: standard. Two different things: a day the person skipped (an EXDATE) is one of the N, since the rule generates its set, COUNT bounds that set, and exclusions are taken out afterwards (3.8.5.1's construction); a generated instance the rule can't place (30 February) is not an occurrence and is not counted (3.3.10). Under "keep N events" the count is raised for each skip the change itself generates, never for one the person made earlier, because that was a choice about that day, not a request for one more at the end. Verified: the harness's bound checks and the COUNT mutation, and the expander's "COUNT:" tests (the 31st three times, a skip one of the three). Where: series model, Bounds. Settled 2026-11-02; the wording corrected 2026-11-04.
- **Where a series starts.** Status: choice. Chosen: on a day of its rule; a start the rule doesn't name moves forward to the rule's first day, keeping the time, and the editor says so. The standard's 3.8.5.3 says a start not synchronized with its rule gives an undefined set, so there is no standard reading to follow. Else: keep the typed start as a first occurrence outside the pattern, which some calendars do. Why: one reading everywhere; a stray first day confuses every later edit. Verified: the creation tests in the PHP suite. Where: contract, event creation. Settled in 1.5.0.
- **Minute and second rules.** Status: choice (was: workaround, while the library's iterator, which has no step for them, expanded the other frequencies). They are rows of the app's own expander like any other: periods stepped in elapsed time from the start's own minute or second, BYSECOND expanding a minute, every other part limiting, as the standard's table reads them. Verified 2026-11-04: the model's unit tests, the harness's evaluator, and the expander's table tests (every SECONDLY and MINUTELY cell). Where: series model, "The expander is the app's own". Settled 2026-11-02; a choice from 2026-11-04.
- **A rule part invalid for its frequency.** Status: deviation. Chosen: a rule carrying a part the standard's table marks N/A for its frequency is one the expander can't read: refused where a rule is written, edited only by field. 3.3.10 says such parts "MUST be ignored". Why: a rule read with a part dropped means something the person didn't write, and an imported one keeps its text for the next client. Verified: the PHP suite's refusal tests, and the expander's (every N/A cell of the table refused by the expander and by the writer's check, 2026-11-04). Where: series model, same section. Settled 2026-11-02.
- **A walk that can't complete.** Status: choice. Chosen: a rule has ended when the walk has gone round the calendar's 400-year cycle together with the rule's interval phase without an occurrence, since from there it would only repeat (an interval of 401 years still finds its year; the cycle is the calendar's and the phase's together). Any other limit of the walk (a search budget) is an error the plan reports as a rule it can't read, never a series that ends there. Else: stop silently after a fixed number of tries, which 1.5.0's first candidate did, and which cut a leap-day series to one event while its check agreed. Verified: the harness's sparse cases and the count-cap mutation; the evaluator reports its own horizon as incomplete rather than ended; the expander's "End:" tests (no 30 February, an unreachable hour, 401 years, year 9999, and a walk limit reached as an error). Where: `server/src/Domain/Series/RuleIterator.php`. Settled 2026-11-04.
- **The expander is our own.** Status: choice, built for 1.5.0. Chosen: one expander written from the standard's table (`Series\RuleIterator`), read by the window, the model, the occurrence check and mail admission, with the library kept for reading and writing iCalendar text and for the CalDAV server. Why: the library's iterator ignores BYSETPOS outside MONTHLY and YEARLY, BYMONTHDAY on DAILY and every BY part on HOURLY, places a BYWEEKNO Sunday a week early, yields a phantom occurrence at an UNTIL past a month's end, and loops on a rule with no day; every fix so far was a patch around it, and the patches are where this release's caps came from. The start is always the first occurrence, an UNTIL before it bounding only what the rule generates (3.8.5.3's set is built from DTSTART). The harness's evaluator is a separate writing of the same table and stays the check. The CalDAV server reads it too, from 1.5.1: a calendar-query's time range on VEVENT is answered from the occurrences the window shows (its gap days, repeated hours, skips and edited days) under RFC 4791 9.9's table, an object passing the library's validation for every other part of the filter as well, and an `expand` request (9.6.5) on calendar-query or calendar-multiget is served from the same occurrences ([#202](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/202)). It reads them as the window does, through `Recurrence::expand` with the request's budget, which is charged for every occurrence the walk generates, skipped and edited ones included; the range is read in windows the expander's per-window cap never cuts, each opening one second before the last one ended so an occurrence with no length on a boundary is in one, and a window still cut at two seconds is refused like a range past the budget, never answered short. What still reads the library's iterator: a free-busy-query's busy times (which objects it reads is the expander's), a calendar-query sent with Depth 0 to one object without expand, and a time range on an alarm or a property; clients expand the objects themselves, so what they show is theirs. Verified 2026-11-04: `server/tests/dav-query.php`, hand-listed, each row of 9.9's table and a series' edited, skipped, gap and all-day days through calendar-query and expand. Verified 2026-11-05: `server/tests/dav-query.php`, 1,100 occurrences running at one second refused and 1,000 answered in full, an occurrence with no length on the first window's end found once, and 900 skipped occurrences refused by a budget of 100. Verified 2026-11-04: `server/tests/expander.php`, one hand-listed case for every cell of the table (RFC 5545's own examples among them) and every reading in this section, with the PHP and database suites and the harness. Verified 2026-11-05 that mail admission's estimate of whether a series is still active reads the rule's BY parts through this expander ([#202](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/202)): `server/tests/mail-security.php`, "mail capacity: a weekday list, BYHOUR, a minute rule limited by its hour and a plain rule are active through their last occurrence and no longer". Decided 2026-11-04; built 2026-11-04.

## Edits to a Series

The [series model](series-model.md) is the full statement; these are its judgment calls.

- **A drag is measured against the occurrence as it shows.** Status: choice. Every part of a drag is a delta from the occurrence as it shows, its own values where it owns them, never an absolute value passed through, because a person drags what they see. What this does not settle: a value typed into the editor. Setting an exception's 19:30 to 20:00 for all events can mean "shift the series half an hour" or "make the series 20:00", and a length typed has the same two readings; 1.5.0 reads both as deltas; from 1.5.1, from a day that owns the field, assignment is the adoption reading beside the delta, by operation and by field: a time, adopted first for a drag and a typed value alike; a length, adopted first when typed (`typed` in the request), the delta first for a resize; a day, by the period threshold either way. Where the day doesn't own the field the two readings are one plan. Verified: the harness's oracle for drags; 2026-11-05, `server/tests/series-model.php`, "Adopt, length" and "Adopt, case 14". Where: series model, Changing the Series from an Edited Day; [#202](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/202). Settled 2026-11-02 for drags; typed values 2026-11-05.
- **A sent rule equal to the stored one is no rule.** Status: choice. Lists compare as sets, INTERVAL=1 is dropped, WKST counts only where the standard makes it count, a bare rule equals its one-member form. Why: an editor round-trip must not read as a change. Verified: the PHP suite. Settled 2026-11-02.
- **Written, not inferred.** Status: choice. "Every 31st" stays `BYMONTHDAY=31`, never rewritten into the last day, and a reading picked in the dialog is written in its own form. Why: what the person wrote is what the editor shows them next time. Verified: the harness's shape check. Settled 2026-11-02.
- **A drag on the 29th to 31st: which reading first.** Status: choice. "On the 30th, or the last day in shorter months" preselected; "skipping months without one" second; a day counted from the end keeps counting from the end first. Else: skipping first, which is what other calendars do. Why: it keeps every month's appointment; it also adds one in months the skipping reading had none, which the row says. Settled 2026-11-02.
- **A list member dragged to another weekday.** Status: choice. "Move every Wednesday to Thursday" preselected over shifting the whole pattern. Settled 2026-11-02.
- **A day moved on its own, when the timetable changes.** Status: choice. It stays where it was moved. Settled 2026-11-02.
- **Changing every event from an edited day.** Status: choice, built for 1.5.1. Each part the day owns and the change moved (its date, time, length) is a group: adopt it as the series' own, or move the series the same way and keep it the day's (1.5.0's reading). Adopting the day is preselected when it moves every event by less than one period of the rule's frequency, the same way first when it is a period or more; adopting the time always. The period is the frequency's as written, never the family's: a DAILY list (Mondays and Wednesdays written DAILY) repeats daily, so its period is a day, where a WEEKLY list's is a week (was: the family's week for both, found by the edit harness, fixed 2026-11-05, `server/tests/series-model.php`, "Adopt, by frequency"). An adoption that would be refused is disabled and never preselected; the same way is then the selection. The threshold is a weak signal (a small change across it flips the default) and stays a judgment; what has to hold is that each choice shows its concrete schedule and the selection is explicit. Verified 2026-11-05: `server/tests/series-model.php`, "Adopt, case 12/13/14/21" and "Adopt, apart", and the smoke test "adoption:" for the dialog's words. Where: [#202](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/202), series model. Settled 2026-11-02, built 2026-11-05.
- **A day the new rule gains at an edge.** Status: choice. Left out by default, as a generated skip, with "Add it" offered. Why: nothing appears the person didn't ask for. Settled 2026-11-02; preselection moved after the fourth review.
- **A count that would end before the day dragged.** Status: choice. "Keep the end" preselected, the dragged day staying in the series, and the row says the count grows; "keep N events + this one on its own" offered. Settled 2026-11-02; preselection moved after the fourth review.
- **A bound the person sent that cuts off the day acted on.** Status: choice, built for 1.5.1. The Day Acted On Comes First is a rule for drags, where the destination is the person's intent, so a kept count that would end before it is rewritten to keep it. An UNTIL or COUNT typed into the editor is the person's own statement, so a day acted on that it cuts off is an ordinary day outside the bound: it takes the outside group's choice, kept as an event of its own where it was put (preselected, as for every day outside) or deleted, and has exactly one outcome in the plan. Each day's outcome comes from one function in the model, so no day is in two of the plan's lists, and `next` is read from the same outcomes: kept, the day is listed once where it shows, whether or not the request placed it; deleted, not at all. Was: the day acted on was always kept on its own, and with "Delete" chosen for the other days outside it was both detached and deleted (found by the edit harness on a week-number rule); and an UNTIL sent alone kept the day but left it out of `next` (found by the edit harness on a monthly rule). Verified 2026-11-05: `server/tests/series-model.php`, "Sent bound (weekly)", "Sent bound (week number)" and "Sent bound (UNTIL alone)", and `server/tests/db/series-plans.test.php`, "a bound sent from an edited day that cuts it off" (with "sent UNTIL alone"), each choice committed and undone. Settled 2026-11-05.
- **Scopes a calendar kind cannot take are not offered.** Status: choice, built for 1.5.1. A scope the server refuses for every occurrence of a kind of calendar is left out of every chooser (the delete, answer and new-time rows, the drop chip after a drag, resize, reschedule or a calendar or person drop, and the editor's Apply to), which all read one function, `seriesScopes` in `web/src/app/scopes.js`; where it leaves a scope out the chooser says why in one line, taken from `planwords.js` beside the refusal panel's own sentence for the same code, so the two cannot drift. On a Google calendar that leaves out "This and following" until 1.5.4 (the server refuses the split from any day after the first; from the first day it is all events), and the server's 422 `series_refused` stays as the backstop. A date edited on its own that is moving to another calendar loses following through the same function. Every series dialog has Change scope: delete following and an answer from a day on return to the row that asked, and the choice made there goes through the same preview path. Verified 2026-11-05: the smoke section "the scopes an occurrence has, one rule for every chooser". Settled 2026-11-05.
- **An UNTIL after a drag.** Status: choice. Kept as the end by default, rewritten as the latest image at or before it, with "keep N events" offered when the count changes. Settled 2026-11-02.
- **How far an unbounded series is read.** Status: choice. Chosen: one whole cycle of the rule's periods past the latest day the change names (the new start among them), widened by the change's reach (series model, Bounds: one cycle per family, one period daily and weekly, 4,800 / gcd(n, 4,800) monthly, 400 / gcd(n, 400) yearly), no number chosen; losses and gains are reported by class (the reason and the member, the first days, a count for a bounded series, a rate over the cycle for one with no end), never as a list of every day, and the old occurrences, their images and the new rule's days are the rules' own arithmetic over periods, never an expansion. Why: the first draft listed every day and took the model's tests from 5 seconds to over 4 minutes and out of memory; a whole cycle costs what a walk of its periods costs, and the classes are what a person reads anyway. Verified 2026-11-06: `server/tests/series-model.php` (Book club, skipping: one class, 1 in every 12 months; Salon night: one class of gains, 5 in every 12 months; Cello lesson, keep 6: a bounded series counts its gains; Book club of fourteen: six lost, five listed and one more; a start moved alone on Book club, Stand-up and Book club of six: the days before the new start counted, never given a rate) and the harness's c-placement (every listed class against the harness's own classes over the same span, seed 23, 3,000 cases). Where: [#205](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/205). Settled 2026-11-04; built 2026-11-06.
- **No display cap.** Status: choice. Bounds and splits are worked out over the actual span; the one limit left (400,000 occurrences) is a refusal, never a partial result. Settled 2026-11-02.
- **Rules several times a day.** Status: choice. Every series-wide move of their days, time or zone is refused with its reason; "this event" edits a day by its key. Why: only imports carry them, and the identity machinery for two occurrences a day isn't worth building for them. Settled 2026-11-02.
- **An edited day's all-day flag.** Status: choice. A field like the others; a day deliberately timed in an all-day series stays timed when the series toggles. Reverses the first draft's decision 8. Settled 2026-11-02.

- **The model never overrides a typed range.** Status: choice. Settled in 1.5.0.
- **Date words are never names, and a weekday beside a date.** Status: choice. A word the date and time readers take (a weekday, a month with its day, a time, "tomorrow") is never offered as a person or a place: the with-clause and the place are read by those same readers and keep only what is left, so there is no separate list of words. - **A date already in the editor.** Status: choice. A client with a chosen day keeps it unless the text names one. Settled before 1.0.

## Sync and Storage

- **All-day series are stored in UTC** (since 0.9.16). Status: choice. Their local day is the UTC date, and a timed series made all-day takes the dates of its days in its old zone. Settled 0.9.16.
- **Too many wrong CalDAV passwords.** Status: choice. A 429, not a 401, so the client waits rather than deciding its password is wrong. Settled before 1.0.
- **A refused change touches nothing, a Google calendar included.** Status: choice. The trip rule runs before any outward call. Verified: the database suite asserts no call on a refusal. Settled 2026-11-04.
- **Exceptions are carried, not trusted.** Status: choice. Chosen: a series plan on a Google calendar is sent as the master, then the series is read back and every day the read-back doesn't hold as the plan has it is sent on its instance id (an edited day whole with its own fields, a skipped day as a cancelled instance, a record the plan has no exception for put back to the series' values); what Google holds after the last read-back is what the rows here store, and a difference is said in Activity and Review, never papered over. Verified 2026-11-07: `server/tests/db/google-series.test.php` (one scenario per contract row, a 412 mid-way with Finish and Put back, a lost insert answer) against the probe's stand-in Google, and the edit harness's `--google` mode and `google-split` fixed case (`8-google`, `e-google`: the stand-in holds each series as the rows here hold it, a poll changes nothing). - **Floating times are stored as their wall clock in the event's own zone** (1.5.4, [#167](https://github.com/Oshyan/better-cal/issues/167)). Status: choice. Chosen: an `events.floating` flag, with `start_utc` and `end_utc` the wall clock read in the row's `tzid` (the Home zone when the event was made), so every query, index, key and the series model read a floating row as an ordinary row in that zone; one function (`Domain\Floating`) moves a floating row's instants to a viewer's clock and back, and the window, the single record, the reminder scan and every request's instants go through it. The viewer is the device a request names (`X-Device-Tz`), else Home; the reminder scan's viewer is where the device was last seen. Else: keys written as wall clocks as if UTC (the series model's first sketch), which no clock change can touch but which every existing reader would read as an instant, or a separate wall-time column, which no index or query reads. A generated occurrence shows at the clock the rule generated it from (the expander's wall clock, before its zone reads it), read on the viewer's clock by the one wall-clock rule; its key stays the instant the own zone gives that clock. So a wall clock the Home zone skips (2:30 on its spring change day) is 2:30 for a viewer whose zone has it and 3:30 only where the viewer's zone skips it too, and on the Home clock 3:30 as before (fixed after 1.5.4, which showed 3:30 on every clock). On its spring change day the clocks its skipped hour lands on an instant another clock has (an hourly 02:30 and 03:30, a minutely 02:xx and 03:xx) are one occurrence everywhere, shown at the later clock, so a viewer whose zone has that hour sees it empty; minutely and secondly rules step elapsed time there, so one whose interval does not divide the change (every 12 hours as 720 minutes, every 90 minutes) keeps the moved clock after it, and the hour the zone repeats holds its minutes twice. The key cannot carry two clocks on one instant: it is that instant at the second (`recurrence_instance_utc`, skips, instance ids, RECURRENCE-ID out), and a secondly rule already holds every second. The fix is a release of its own: a floating series generated and keyed by its nominal wall clock, as RFC 5545 keys a floating RECURRENCE-ID, read the same way by the expander, the model and the harness's evaluator, with a one-off rekeying of existing edited days and skips. Pinned by `server/tests/floating.php`, "floating limit:". Verified: `server/tests/floating.php`, `server/tests/db/floating.test.php`, and the edit harness with a share of its series floating. Settled 2026-11-07; the skipped clock and the window settled 2026-11-08.

Else: keep floating events off Google calendars, which would make a routine on a Google calendar impossible. Settled 2026-11-07.

The entry's before side holds every row the import brought back or removed, with its links, as it was; its after side the rows as the import leaves them with the links of the days and of every row brought back, so its undo deletes the series again and puts its days back as they were. A day it brings in follows its series (the link rule). Why: the lookup did not say whether it meant live rows, the app's delete is a soft delete, and the unique key on calendar, UID and key means a deleted row holds its key: a deleted series counted as already there, the file's edited day went in under the deleted series, and an old export imported again after a delete brought nothing back and said it was there (an independent hunt, F2). Else: insert new rows beside the deleted ones (the key forbids it for days); count a deleted row as there (what failed). Siblings, every lookup by UID: `Feeds::sync` reads every stored row, deleted ones included, and brings a deleted row back when the feed carries it (follows); the mail ingest counts a deleted event as there on purpose (a deleted mail event stays deleted; follows); Review's invite gate names a deleted series on purpose (follows); plugins' sync reads live rows and inserts a row of no key beside a deleted one, which the key allows (follows); quick add has no such lookup. Verified: link-ownership ("importing a series and its edited day after the series was deleted ..."), failing on the tree before it. Settled 2026-11-10.
- **What a store of a half-done change takes from a row is given back by recovery.** Status: choice, built for 1.5.5. The rule, as it applies to recovery: a change, however it ends, leaves every day holding exactly what it held here alone before the change began, wherever the day still is and still shows what it showed; what it cannot keep it names. Finish and Put back are not exempt because a store ran in between: what a store of the half-done change took from a row is the change's own passing effect, never something that happened at Google. The one thing a store takes from a row that stays is the coordinates of its place, which go when the place text changes (`Feeds::followed`, and the sync's record path), and with them the day's ownership of a place it held by coordinates alone; a row left holding nothing goes whole and comes back whole from the snapshot, and tags, people, reminders, an answer, an icon and what refers to a row are never written by a store. Chosen: one decision, `GoogleSeries::placeBack(the snapshot's row, the row now)`, for every way a snapshot's row comes back: left standing (Finish's `rewind`, on the rows it puts back before the change's writer runs, and Put back's `placesBack`, once Google's state is stored) or made again under another id (`relink`). A row showing the snapshot's place text has the snapshot's coordinates and where they came from; a row showing another text keeps what it has (A1: they belong to the place it shows now); and so does a row whose coordinates a person or a client placed here since for the same text, as every field held here alone changed since is kept (a lookup's are not kept over the snapshot's: the geocode sweep fills what a store emptied). Before, `rewind` gave a standing row back the columns a sync writes, its place text among them, and left the coordinates as the store had left them, so a day that stood through the stores for a tag or its reminders showed its own place again with no map spot and no word (seed 61349 case 272, seed 60611 case 153); Put back had the same fault where the row stood, and lost the series' own coordinates when a new place for all events was put back. Else: keep the sync from emptying them while a change is half done (a second reading of a place inside the sync, for one caller); record what each store took in the pending row (a stored form, for one group of columns); or let the snapshot win always (a place picked here since would go back to the earlier pick). Costs: a pick made here since is told from a store's effect by where the coordinates came from, so coordinates copied in with no source would read as a store's leftovers and go back to the snapshot's; no write reaches such a row today, the series taking none while its change is half done. Verified: google-series-recovery-held (every kind of thing held here alone, a tag, a person, reminders, an answer, an icon, coordinates, a trip link, a duplicate decision and plugin data, on a row that stood through the stores and that the plan moves, by Finish and by Put back; the two seeds' shapes stopped after the master write and with its answer lost; a new place for all events overwriting the day's text at Google; a row a store removed; a day past a new end, put back; the series' own coordinates, put back; a lookup filling the place while half done; a pick made here since), the interleaving fixed cases `a-day-tagged-then-given-coordinates-a-place-for-all-then-a-flip-stopped-after-its-master-write-then-finished`, the same `-then-put-back` and `a-day-with-reminders-then-its-own-place-with-coordinates-a-time-change-whose-second-answer-was-lost-then-finished`, and the mutations `finish-leaves-what-a-store-cleared` and `put-back-leaves-what-a-store-cleared`. - **Finish's entry records what the change did with the days by the function the commit uses.** Status: choice, built for 1.5.5; completes "Finish's ground is what the change it completes changed" and "The days a change brought into being are its ground". The rule: a series change's entry records, as its grounds, what it did with each day (a move for every row and every skip whose key it moved, none for a key that stays, and the days it added) by one function, whether the change completed in one go or was completed by Finish. Chosen: `Undo::planGrounds(series, the plan's writes)` is the planner's record as grounds (each skip's move, the days added), `Undo::changeGround(before, after, that record)` is an entry's ground (what the snapshots show, read with the record so a skip the plan carried is its move and never two skips changed, then the record itself), and every entry of a change for all events goes through both: the commit here, the commit on a Google calendar, an undo's or redo's plan, and Review's Finish, which reads the record from the plan its pending row kept (`plan_json`: the change's kind, its series and the planner's writes, `skipMoves` and `added` among them, so it lacks nothing the commit has) and the rows before from the same pending row (`local_json`). A row's move needs no record: rows keep their ids through a recovery, so the snapshots show it. The rows Finish leaves are read as the commit reads them: those of every series the pending row named, and of every series and event of its own the change inserted, by the uid Google gave each when Finish ran; before, they were named from the pending row as it stood when Finish began, which holds no uid for a series not yet inserted, so a split stopped before its insert left the new series out of the entry, and a day kept as an event of its own read as removed with its tags. Before, Finish's ground was the rows' difference alone, so a skip the change carried read as the old key and the new key both changed, and the undo of an earlier delete of that day was refused "Newer changes exist for this item" after a change finished from Review, where the same undo went through after the same change in one go (seed 61349 case 359, seed 82447 case 139); and the days a finished change added were not its ground. A split records none by either path, as before: the days before its cut stay, and those from it on leave the series for one the entry makes whole, which the snapshots show. Put back's entry stands on the half-done change it closes and on no row. Finish and Put back stay log only. Else: record a finished change through the commit's own recorder, with its snapshots and what to send back, which would make it undoable from its Finish entry (a change to api-contract.md, Activity); or work the skips' moves out again from the rows at Finish (a second derivation of what the planner recorded, with nothing to say which skip went where when two could pair two ways). ## Request Credentials

- **What a request may write, and when its credential is checked.** Status: choice. A write made under a request is one of two things. Either it is a change to what the account holds, made inside a transaction in which the request's session, API key or proved password was checked with a lock: `Undo::write` where the change is journaled, `RequestAuthority::checkedTx` where it is not. Or it is bookkeeping the server keeps about requests themselves, which belongs to no account and is written the same whoever asked. The bookkeeping is a fixed list of tables, `RequestAuthority::BOOKKEEPING`, each with its reason: throttle counters (`rate_events`), admission to the model and to outside actions (`model_admissions`, `external_action_admissions`), lookup caches (`http_cache`, `geocode_cache`) and the server's health record (`system_health`). Nothing else is on it. A write to any other table outside a checked transaction is a journaling misuse: logged in production, failing the suites. The check must be of the request's own credential: a locked check marks its transaction with what it proved (the key's id, the session's hash, the password's hash), and only the request's own among them covers its writes; a check of another account's key, or of the account's own key under a browser session, covers nothing. Signing in is a request made with the password and writes its session as that. Why: a credential revoked after a request signed in then stops every change the request has not yet committed, whichever route it came by, and a new route can't forget the check. A flow that ends its own credential (signing out, a key revoking itself, a password change) checks it and ends it in one transaction and writes nothing after; a password change signs in again and writes the rest as the new session. The account's change marker moves in the checked transaction of a request that may change things, not after the response. A request's work run after its response (a plugin's queued job) runs under the same credential. Verified 2026-11-06: the write-boundary checks in the PHP suite (among them a transaction that checks another account's key, or the account's own key under its session, and then writes, which is a misuse, and the request's credential checked beside another's, which covers the write), `server/tests/db/revocation.test.php`, and the completeness test run with every request operation's credential registered. Where: `server/src/Domain/RequestAuthority.php`. Settled 2026-11-05 for 1.5.1.
- **An undo and its CalDAV journal are one transaction.** Status: choice. The undo's restore, its mark as undone and the `dav_changes` rows that tell CalDAV clients are written in the one transaction that checked the request's credential, the rule above applied to the journal: check and write together. Else: the journal in a checked transaction of its own after the restore commits, which 1.5.1's first candidate had, where a credential revoked between the two left the undo done, the request failing and CalDAV clients never told. Verified 2026-11-05: `server/tests/db/undo.test.php` revokes the credential the moment another connection sees the undo committed, and the undo still answers ok with its journal row written. Where: `Undo::apply`. Settled 2026-11-05 for 1.5.1.
- **Every CalDAV journal row is written with its change, and a journal that fails fails the save.** Status: choice. Chosen: the `dav_changes` row and the synctoken bump are written in the transaction of the change they tell about (its `Undo::write` or `tx`), whatever made it: the app, CalDAV, a feed poll, a plugin's sync, an invitation answer, a migration; a journal row asked for outside a transaction is refused as a mistake in the code. A failed journal write rolls the change back and the request fails visibly. Else (was, to 1.5.1): the journal's database errors caught and logged, kept for a rolling deploy before migration 003, so a change could be saved with CalDAV clients never told; a feed poll and an answer's journal also ran in transactions of their own after the change committed. Why: deploys now pause traffic and migrate before resuming, so the reason for the catch is gone, and a change a sync client never hears about is a silent divergence, worse than a failed save the person sees. Verified 2026-11-06: `server/tests/db/dav-journal.test.php` refuses the journal's inserts and the create fails with no event, entry or token change, then saves once the journal is writable. Where: `ChangeLog::record`, `ChangeLog::recordMany`, `Feeds::sync`, `Rsvp`. Settled 2026-11-06.
- **A job queue's state is the server's own record.** Status: choice. A request's work run after its response (a plugin's queued job) runs under the request's credential and is checked at its writes; whether the queue claimed it, finished it or failed it is the server's, written as no request (`RequestAuthority::asServer`). The `jobs` table is not on the bookkeeping list, because a job row is queued by a request, under its check; only its claim, done and failed marks are written apart. A credential revoked meanwhile fails the job once, for good: nothing is left running until stall recovery, and the worker never reruns it with no credential. Verified 2026-11-05: `server/tests/db/revocation.test.php` revokes a session and an API key during a run. Where: `Plugins::runQueuedNow`, `JobQueue::markFailed`. Settled 2026-11-05 for 1.5.1.
- **The address cache stays bookkeeping.** Status: choice. `geocode_cache` is keyed by the location text someone typed, and stays on the list: its row is the same whoever asks (a text and where it is), it belongs to no account and nothing reads it but the same lookup, so a revoked request can at most add an address string to a shared cache. `http_cache`, keyed by a URL, is the same shape. Settled 2026-11-05 for 1.5.1.

## Moving Through the Calendar

- **When a move has arrived, and what a month step starts from.** Status: choice. Chosen: a move (Today, an arrow, a jump, a view switch) has arrived once the view has reported the days it shows with the day gone to among them, and the month grid is not still gliding there; until then what is on screen is on its way, and where you are is the day gone to. One predicate (`arrived`) feeds every reading of where you are: the arrow keys, a view switch, and a step. Next and Prev in the month and agenda views step from the month the view names once it has arrived; until then from the month of the day gone to, so a second press mid-glide steps on from the first. A view whose window cannot frame a month names another month than the one gone to even after arriving: the phone's 3-day ribbon shows about two weeks, so a 1st landed on reads as the month before; the agenda's list shows the next month's events for a month with nothing in it. There the month of the day gone to stands while that day is on screen, in both directions. Else (was, 1.4.3 to 1.5.2): three readings of "still on the way" side by side (the grid's glide flag, a 900 ms timer after a press, the report sequence) with a branch for the ribbon and one for the agenda's Prev only; the timer's window was already covered by the report sequence, and in the agenda a Next after a Prev into an empty month skipped the month on screen. Why: a timer is a guess at an event the store already records, and the step rule follows from what a view's window can show, not from which view it is. Verified 2026-11-06: the smoke tests "next month:" and "prev month:" (before the view has shown the anchor, mid-glide, arrived, scrolled on with the anchor still on screen, the ribbon on a 1st, the agenda's empty month, both scrolled off), and in a browser the view checks "month view: Next five times" and "month view: a glide arrives after a load that changes no heights". Where: `web/src/app/actions.js` (`arrived`, `hereKey`, `stepMonth`), `web/src/lib/glide.js`. Settled 2026-11-06.
- **A glide under a load.** Status: choice. Chosen: a glide (Today or an arrow in a month view, over a near distance) heads for one row and ends in two ways only: the scroller arrives within a pixel of it (or of as far as the scroller goes), or you take the scroller, by an input on it (wheel, touch, pointer, key) or a scroll that moves away from the target, since a glide only ever closes in. Rows changing height under it keep the same week, the same way into it, on screen, and aim the glide again from there; a change that moved neither that place nor the target leaves the scroll under way alone. The browser's scrollend says nothing: it fires for the grid's own writes as for yours. Else (was, three rounds in 1.5.0 and 1.5.1): scrollend among the inputs that end a glide, then a counter of the grid's own writes to swallow their scrollend, which assumed one scrollend per write, then a threshold because an unchanged write still stopped it. Why: the end of a glide is defined by its distance, which the grid measures at every scroll, not by an event whose source it cannot tell. Cost: a smooth scroll stopped short by something that is neither an input nor a move away (a scroll from elsewhere on the page) leaves the glide marked under way until a load aims it again or the next move. Verified 2026-11-06: the smoke tests "glide:" (the distance rule) and "same place:" (the place kept across fixed, fitted and resized rows); the browser checks above. ## Revisiting One

Changing a choice is a changed decision: change the rule where it lives, say so in the changelog, and update the entry with the new reading and the date, keeping the old one as "was". Repairing code that broke a recorded choice is a bug fix, and the entry's verification line is what changes. A defect's entry is closed when its repair lands, with the release named.
